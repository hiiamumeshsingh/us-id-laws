North Carolina ID Laws: What You Need to Know

North Carolina issues standard and REAL ID credentials through NCDMV and also provides separate voter photo ID options. This guide covers driver's licenses, state IDs, required documents, residency, REAL ID, voting identification, renewals, replacements, fees and non-U.S. citizen requirements.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

North Carolina driver's license requirements

North Carolina licenses are issued by NCDMV. New drivers follow the state's licensing process, which includes the appropriate knowledge, vision and road testing requirements. Drivers ages 15 to 17 enter the graduated licensing system, while adult applicants use the regular licensing process. citeturn2view0

Standard driver's license

For a standard credential, applicants generally provide one document proving identity and date of birth, one document proving a Social Security number and one document verifying a physical North Carolina address. If the applicant was not born in the United States, proof of legal presence is also required. citeturn0search1

Liability insurance

A North Carolina driver's license applicant must provide proof of liability insurance from a provider licensed to do business in North Carolina. NCDMV permits a printed document or a digital copy that can be securely uploaded during the office visit. The insurance requirement does not apply to a learner's permit. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Teen drivers

North Carolina uses a graduated licensing system for drivers ages 15 to 17. The system progresses through learner and provisional stages before a full provisional license. citeturn2view0

New residents

People moving to North Carolina must obtain a North Carolina driver's license when they establish residency. NCDMV's new-resident process requires an out-of-state license or identity document, Social Security documentation, proof of North Carolina address and, where applicable, legal-presence documentation. citeturn0search1

North Carolina state identification cards

NCDMV issues non-driver state identification cards for residents who need official photo identification but do not need a driver's license. State IDs can be obtained at a driver license office, and NCDMV also provides homebound service for qualifying residents who cannot travel to an office. citeturn0search2

Standard state ID

U.S. citizens generally provide one document proving identity and age, a Social Security card or other Social Security-number document and one proof of North Carolina residency. Non-U.S. citizens provide qualifying proof of legal presence instead. citeturn0search2turn0search12

REAL ID state ID

A REAL ID state ID requires one identity/date-of-birth document, one document with the full Social Security number and two documents showing the current physical North Carolina address. Non-U.S. citizens must also meet the legal-presence requirements for REAL ID. citeturn0search0

State ID fees

NCDMV lists a state ID as free for residents age 17 and older. A standard state ID for someone age 16 or younger is $16.75, although qualifying minors may receive a no-fee ID under specific programs. citeturn0search2turn0search12

Documents needed for a North Carolina ID

The exact documents depend on whether you are applying for a standard credential or REAL ID. NCDMV requires documents that establish identity, Social Security number and North Carolina residency. Non-U.S. citizens have an additional legal-presence requirement. citeturn0search0turn0search11

Identity and date of birth

Examples of acceptable identity documents include a valid U.S. passport, certified birth certificate and qualifying government-issued identity documents. For REAL ID, the identity document must show the applicant's full name and date of birth. citeturn0search0

Social Security number

NCDMV accepts a Social Security card or certain documents showing the applicant's full Social Security number, including qualifying W-2s, 1099 tax forms and payroll records. The name on the Social Security document must match the identity documentation unless a qualifying name-change document is supplied. citeturn0search0

Name changes

If the name on identity and Social Security documents does not match, NCDMV requires proof of the name change, such as a certified marriage certificate, divorce decree or court/Register of Deeds document. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Original documents

For REAL ID, documents must generally be original or certified copies issued by the appropriate government authority. Photocopies are not accepted unless they are certified by the issuing agency. citeturn0search0

Proof of North Carolina residency

NCDMV recognizes several documents as proof of North Carolina residency. Residency documents cannot be used as identity documents, so applicants should prepare the documents required for both parts of the application. citeturn0search3

Common residency documents

  • North Carolina vehicle registration card, insurance policy or title
  • North Carolina voter registration card
  • Utility or cable bill
  • Housing lease or contract
  • Mortgage statement
  • Property or income tax statement
  • Financial statement
  • School records
  • Military orders or other military documents
  • Qualifying government correspondence
  • Letter from a homeless shelter

For a standard driver's license or ID, one proof of North Carolina residency is generally required. REAL ID requires two documents with the current physical address. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Minors

NCDMV recognizes additional residency evidence for applicants under 18, including certain school records, mailed correspondence, lease or housing records, tax records, medical records and financial records. citeturn0search3

North Carolina REAL ID requirements

North Carolina REAL ID is optional. A REAL ID credential has a star at the top and can be used for federal purposes covered by the REAL ID rules. A standard credential is marked Not for Federal Purposes. citeturn0search4

REAL ID checklist

  • One document proving identity and date of birth
  • One document showing the full Social Security number
  • Two documents proving current physical North Carolina residency
  • Proof of legal presence for non-U.S. citizens
  • Proof of name change when applicable
  • Liability insurance documentation for a driver's license, except a learner's permit
REAL ID is optional: You do not need a North Carolina REAL ID to drive, vote, receive federal benefits or carry out ordinary state activities. If you do not have one for a covered federal purpose, you can use another federally accepted form of identification. citeturn0search4

Getting REAL ID

Your first North Carolina REAL ID requires an in-person visit to an NCDMV driver license office so the required documents can be reviewed and scanned. citeturn0search4

North Carolina voter identification requirements

North Carolina voters are asked to show an acceptable photo ID when voting in person. The State Board of Elections lists driver's licenses, NCDMV non-driver IDs, U.S. passports, qualifying student and employee IDs, tribal IDs and other approved photo credentials among the acceptable forms. citeturn0search6

A voter age 65 or older may use an expired acceptable ID if the ID was unexpired on the voter's 65th birthday. citeturn0search6

If you do not have an acceptable photo ID

A voter without an acceptable photo ID can still vote. For in-person voting, the voter may cast a provisional ballot and complete a Photo ID Exception Form explaining the applicable exception. If the voter simply forgot an ID, they may also return with an acceptable ID and vote a regular ballot. citeturn0search6

Free voter photo ID

County boards of elections can issue free voter photo IDs to registered voters. The voter provides their name, date of birth and last four digits of their Social Security number and has their photograph taken. No supporting documents are required for this county-issued voter ID process. citeturn0search7

Absentee voting

North Carolina absentee-by-mail voters generally include a photocopy of an acceptable ID with the ballot envelope or complete an ID Exception Form. Special provisions apply to military and overseas voters. citeturn0search6

Renewing a North Carolina driver's license or ID

Eligible North Carolina residents can renew a driver's license or state ID online, at a self-service kiosk or at an NCDMV driver license office. Online renewal is generally available up to six months before a driver's license expires or within two years after expiration. A state ID can be renewed online up to one year after expiration. citeturn0search8

Online renewal limits

NCDMV says customers can use online renewal only every other renewal. If the previous renewal was completed online, the next renewal must generally be completed in person. Certain customers must also visit an office because of license status, restrictions or legal-presence documentation. citeturn0search8

Renewing a state ID

State IDs can be renewed up to six months before expiration. Eligible customers can renew online, at a kiosk or at a driver license office. citeturn0search8turn0search12

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged North Carolina ID

NCDMV provides replacement services for driver's licenses and IDs. Customers who replace a lost or stolen driver's license in person must provide two documents verifying identity plus a Social Security card or another accepted Social Security-number document. citeturn0search8

Office visit may be required

Some customers cannot complete replacement or renewal online, including people who need to establish or re-establish certain legal-presence documentation. NCDMV directs customers who fall into these categories to a driver license office. citeturn0search8

Name and address changes

Name changes

When the name on an identity document does not match the Social Security record or other required documents, NCDMV requires proof of the legal name change. Common examples include a certified marriage certificate, divorce decree or court/Register of Deeds document. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Address changes

North Carolina residents should keep the address on their DMV record current. A physical North Carolina address is used for residency verification, particularly for REAL ID transactions, which require two documents showing the current physical address. citeturn0search0turn0search3

North Carolina IDs for non-U.S. citizens

People who were not born in the United States must provide a document proving legal presence when applying for a North Carolina driver's license or ID. The exact acceptable document depends on immigration status. citeturn0search1turn0search11

Documents accepted for REAL ID

  • Valid, unexpired Permanent Resident Card (Form I-551)
  • Unexpired Employment Authorization Document
  • Valid, unexpired foreign passport with the required U.S. visa and I-94
  • Consular Report of Birth Abroad
  • Certificate of Naturalization
  • Certificate of Citizenship

NCDMV maintains a separate legal-presence list for documents that can establish legal presence but do not qualify for REAL ID. Some temporary immigration documents must be accompanied by additional supporting documents. citeturn0search11

Credential duration

For non-U.S. citizens, the validity of the credential can depend on the period of lawful presence established by the applicant's immigration documentation. NCDMV's legal-presence rules should be checked for the specific status before applying. citeturn0search11

Counterfeit, altered and fraudulent identification

NCDMV requires acceptable identity documents and does not treat altered or unsupported documents as valid proof of identity. REAL ID applications in particular require original or certified documents from the issuing authority. citeturn0search0

North Carolina ID law FAQs

What documents do I need for a North Carolina driver's license?

You generally need proof of identity and date of birth, a Social Security number and proof of physical North Carolina residency. Non-U.S. citizens also need qualifying legal-presence documentation and driver's license applicants need proof of liability insurance.

How many proofs of residency do I need for a North Carolina REAL ID?

Two documents showing your current physical North Carolina address are required.

Is North Carolina REAL ID mandatory?

No. REAL ID is optional. A standard license or ID can still be used for ordinary state activities, while another federally accepted document can be used for covered federal purposes.

Does North Carolina require photo ID to vote?

Yes. Voters are asked to show an acceptable photo ID when voting, but voters without an acceptable ID can use the state's exception and provisional-ballot procedures.

Can I get a free North Carolina state ID?

Yes. NCDMV lists state IDs as free for residents age 17 and older. Qualifying younger residents may also receive a no-fee ID under specified programs.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a North Carolina ID?

Yes, if they satisfy NCDMV's documentation requirements. Non-U.S. citizens must provide qualifying proof of legal presence. The documents required depend on the credential and immigration status.

Can I renew my North Carolina ID online?

Eligible customers can renew a state ID online up to one year after expiration. Certain customers must renew in person, and online renewal is limited to every other renewal.

What happens if I lose my North Carolina driver's license?

NCDMV provides replacement services. For certain in-person replacements, you must bring two identity documents plus a Social Security card or other accepted proof of your Social Security number.
