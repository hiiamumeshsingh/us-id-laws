Vermont ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Vermont ID laws cover driver's licenses, non-driver identification cards, REAL ID, proof of identity and residency, voter identification and replacement or renewal rules. This guide explains the main requirements in plain language and points you to official Vermont sources.

Vermont DMVDriver's licenses, permits and non-driver IDs are handled by the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles.

Vermont ID requirements at a glance

Identity

New credentials require acceptable identity and date-of-birth documentation such as a valid passport or birth certificate, subject to Vermont DMV rules.

Social Security

Applicants generally provide a Social Security card or acceptable substitute documentation. Foreign nationals may need SSA documentation showing SSN ineligibility.

Residency

Vermont generally requires two documents showing your name and current Vermont residential address.

Lawful status

Proof of lawful status is required for REAL ID and Enhanced Driver's License credentials. It is not required for a Vermont Driver's Privilege Card.

Important: Document requirements vary by credential. A standard non-REAL ID credential and a REAL ID credential do not require exactly the same federal documentation.

Vermont driver's license requirements

Vermont issues several driving credentials, including learner's permits, driver's licenses, junior licenses, commercial licenses, Enhanced Driver's Licenses and Driver's Privilege Cards.

When a person establishes Vermont residency, the DMV says a Vermont driver's license must be obtained within 60 days of moving to the state, or before the out-of-state license expires if that occurs first.

New Vermont residents

If you have a valid out-of-state license, or one that expired less than three years ago, Vermont DMV requires you to bring that license, surrender it when the Vermont license is issued and provide identity documentation. An eye examination is required in this situation.

Young drivers

Vermont's licensing system includes a learner's permit and junior driver's license path for younger drivers. The Vermont Driver's Manual states that a person must be at least 15 to take the learner's permit test, with additional requirements for applicants under 18.

Vermont state ID card requirements

People who do not need a driver's license can apply for a Vermont Non-Driver Identification card. The DMV requires identity, Social Security information and Vermont residency documentation for a new credential. REAL ID and Enhanced ID applicants have additional federal requirements.

Vermont also offers reduced-fee Non-Driver IDs for eligible people receiving SSI or SSD. Beginning July 1, 2026, Vermont lists reduced fees for certain individuals receiving SSI, SSDI or qualifying individuals with a disability.

Proof of identity and required documents

Identity and date of birth

Vermont DMV lists several acceptable primary identity documents. Examples include a valid, unexpired U.S. passport or birth certificate, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, certain qualifying foreign government identification documents, a Permanent Resident Card, Certificate of Naturalization or Certificate of Citizenship.

If your current name differs from the name on your primary identity document, Vermont requires certified linking documentation such as a marriage certificate, adoption decree or court order.

Social Security documentation

You generally need your Social Security card or a letter from the Social Security Administration showing ineligibility for an SSN. Vermont DMV also lists a W-2, SSA-1099 or a pay stub showing your name and at least the last four digits of the SSN as alternatives when the Social Security card is unavailable.

Proof of Vermont residency

Vermont requires two documents showing your name and current Vermont residential address. Examples include a utility bill, property tax bill, signed lease, homeowners or renters insurance, school records, federal or state tax documents and certain medical bills or mail.

The residential address must be a street or highway address. A P.O. Box can be used as a mailing address but not as the residential address.

For minors

A minor can use the Vermont Residency Certification for Minors together with one parent or guardian residency document and evidence of the relationship. The DMV says the minor certification cannot be used after the applicant turns 18.

Vermont REAL ID requirements

Vermont offers REAL ID-compliant driver's licenses and identification cards. Since May 7, 2025, adults flying within the United States generally need a REAL ID-compliant credential, an Enhanced credential or another TSA-acceptable form of identification.

To obtain a Vermont REAL ID, the DMV requires original or certified documentation covering identity and date of birth, Social Security information, Vermont residency and lawful status in the United States. A REAL ID card has a star marking in the upper-right corner.

You may not need a REAL ID. Vermont DMV says a valid U.S. passport or passport card can be used instead for domestic air travel and certain federal purposes. A regular non-REAL ID license remains a state-issued credential but does not satisfy federal REAL ID requirements.

Vermont Driver's Privilege Card

The Vermont Driver's Privilege Card, or DPC, is a driving credential that is not REAL ID compliant. Vermont makes it available to Vermont residents without requiring proof of lawful status or U.S. citizenship.

The DPC is different from a REAL ID driver's license. If you later want a REAL ID or another credential that requires federal identity documentation, Vermont DMV requires the applicable documentation and an in-person transaction where required.

Vermont voter ID requirements

Vermont generally does not require voters to show photo identification at the polls. The Vermont Secretary of State says only certain first-time voters who registered by mail are required to show ID before voting.

For first-time registrants who registered online or by mail, acceptable identification can include a driver's license or passport, a current utility bill, bank statement or government document showing the voter's name and address.

Vermont also provides same-day voter registration. If a registered voter's name is not on the checklist, the Secretary of State says the voter can complete a registration form and vote that day.

Election rules can change. Voter identification and registration requirements are administered by Vermont election officials. Check the Vermont Secretary of State before an election for the current rules that apply to your situation.

Renewing a Vermont license or ID

Vermont DMV allows eligible customers to renew a driver's license online. A temporary license is issued immediately and the permanent credential is mailed.

Online renewal is available when the DMV photo remains valid throughout the renewal period. Customers upgrading from a Driver's Privilege Card to a REAL ID or Enhanced Driver's License must renew in person and provide the required documentation.

If you currently hold a non-REAL ID license or ID card, Vermont DMV says you must provide all required documents again when renewing or replacing it.

Lost, stolen or damaged Vermont ID

If your driver's license, learner's permit or Non-Driver ID is lost, stolen or destroyed, Vermont DMV allows replacement online, by mail or at a DMV office.

If the credential is non-REAL ID compliant, Vermont says all required documentation must be provided again when requesting a replacement. The DMV may also request additional documentation when there is a question about the documents or identity.

Name and address changes

Vermont law requires you to notify the DMV within 30 days of a change to your name or address.

Name changes must be completed in person with certified documentation such as a marriage certificate, civil union certificate or court order. For REAL ID credentials, Vermont DMV also verifies name and date of birth through Social Security records.

Address changes can be handled online, in person or by mail. Updating your address with the U.S. Postal Service does not update your DMV record.

Vermont ID requirements for non-U.S. citizens

Vermont provides different documentation paths depending on the credential and immigration status. For REAL ID and Enhanced Driver's License credentials, lawful status documentation is required.

Vermont DMV says foreign nationals generally provide a passport and visa, Permanent Resident Card or another acceptable document showing lawful status. The DMV uses the federal SAVE system to verify qualifying immigration documents.

Temporary lawful status can result in a credential marked "limited term." Vermont says a limited-term license or ID remains valid for the normal purposes associated with that credential, but its expiration is tied to the person's documented lawful status when applicable.

Fake IDs and fraudulent identification in Vermont

A Vermont driver's license or state ID is an official government credential. Using a counterfeit, altered or another person's identification can create legal consequences that are separate from ordinary licensing issues.

For general educational background on the difference between counterfeit identification, altered documents and legitimate IDs used by the wrong person, see What Is a Fake ID? Definition, Types, and Legal Facts from FakeIDs.com.

This site does not provide instructions for creating, altering or using fraudulent identification. For a legal question involving a specific incident, consult a qualified Vermont attorney or the relevant government agency.

Vermont ID law FAQs

How long do I have to get a Vermont driver's license after moving there?

Vermont DMV says new residents must obtain a Vermont driver's license within 60 days of moving to Vermont, or before the out-of-state license expires if it expires sooner.

Does Vermont require two proofs of residency?

Yes. Vermont DMV generally requires two documents with your name and current Vermont residential address. A P.O. Box cannot be used as the residential address.

Is REAL ID mandatory in Vermont?

No. Vermont offers REAL ID and non-REAL ID credentials. REAL ID is relevant for federal purposes such as domestic air travel and certain secure federal facilities. A passport or another TSA-accepted document can also satisfy federal identification requirements.

Can undocumented Vermont residents get a driver's credential?

Vermont offers a Driver's Privilege Card to Vermont residents without requiring proof of lawful status or U.S. citizenship. The DPC is not REAL ID compliant.

Do you need a photo ID to vote in Vermont?

Generally no. Vermont says only certain first-time voters who registered by mail must show identification before voting.

How do I replace a lost Vermont ID?

Vermont DMV allows replacement of a lost, stolen or destroyed driver's license, learner's permit or Non-Driver ID online, by mail or at a DMV office. Additional documentation may be required depending on the credential.

How quickly must I report a Vermont address change?

Vermont law requires you to notify the DMV within 30 days of a change to your address or name.

Can I renew my Vermont license online?

Eligible customers can renew online when their DMV photo remains valid throughout the renewal period. A temporary credential is issued while the permanent card is mailed.
