Delaware ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Delaware's Division of Motor Vehicles issues driver's licenses and identification cards. This guide covers the documents Delaware requires, proof of residency, federally compliant credentials, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and special credential programs.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Delaware driver's license requirements

Delaware DMV uses a document-verification process for driver's licenses and identification cards. Applicants generally need a primary identity document, a secondary identity document, proof of Social Security number if eligible and two proofs of Delaware residency.

Non-U.S. citizens must provide documents establishing lawful presence. Delaware uses federal verification systems to confirm immigration status and authorized length of stay.

New drivers

Delaware has a graduated licensing system for younger drivers. A new driver may need a learner's permit and must meet the applicable driver education, knowledge, road-test and supervised-driving requirements before receiving a full license.

Applicants under 18 also have additional documentation rules. Delaware DMV states that a parent or court-appointed guardian listed on the minor's birth certificate must accompany an applicant under 18 for an identification card.

Out-of-state applicants

People moving to Delaware with an existing driver's license should use the DMV's current transfer requirements. The documentation process can differ depending on whether the existing credential already meets federal identification standards.

Delaware state ID without a driver's license

Delaware residents can obtain an identification card without holding a driver's license.

The current DMV information lists the ID card fee as $40. The card expires eight years from the applicant's next birthday. A Delaware resident must provide the documents required by the DMV's documentation table.

Applicants under 18 must have a parent or court-appointed guardian listed on the minor's birth certificate accompany them.

Documents needed for a Delaware ID

Delaware separates the document requirements into identity/legal presence, Social Security number, residency and, when applicable, immigration status.

Primary and secondary identification

An applicant generally needs one primary document and one secondary document. A primary document must contain the applicant's full name and date of birth and be verifiable with the issuing agency.

Examples of primary documents include a certified U.S. birth certificate, valid U.S. passport, certificate of naturalization or certificate of citizenship. Qualifying immigration documents can also establish identity and legal presence.

Delaware requires original documents or certified copies. Photocopies and non-certified copies are not accepted for the source-document verification process.

Social Security number

Accepted proof can include a Social Security card, W-2, SSA-1099, SSA non-1099 form, pay stub showing the full SSN or another valid Social Security Administration document showing the full number.

If an applicant is not eligible for an SSN, Delaware can accept an SSA ineligibility letter. That does not by itself make the applicant eligible for a federally compliant credential.

Proof of Delaware residency

Delaware requires two separate proofs of Delaware residency for a driver's license or identification card.

The two documents must come from separate sources and show the applicant's physical address. A P.O. box by itself is not sufficient.

Examples listed by Delaware DMV include:

  • Utility bills
  • Credit-card statements
  • Auto or life insurance policies
  • Voter registration cards
  • Bank account records
  • Employment records
  • Rental agreements
  • USPS change-of-address confirmation or qualifying postmarked mail
  • Other non-personal USPS mail postmarked or received within the last 60 days

For mailed or postmarked residency evidence, Delaware specifically says photocopies and printouts of bills are not accepted. The DMV also requires the two proofs to come from separate sources.

Delaware REAL ID requirements

Delaware has issued federally compliant driver's licenses and identification cards since July 1, 2010. The DMV's SecureID process verifies source documents for identity, legal presence, date of birth, Social Security number and Delaware residence.

Applicants should bring original or certified source documents. The DMV says these documents are verified electronically and that photocopies and non-certified documents are not accepted.

What a compliant credential is used for

A federally compliant Delaware credential can be used for federal purposes covered by REAL ID rules, including TSA identity requirements for domestic air travel. A passport or another TSA-accepted document can also satisfy federal identification requirements.

Existing Delaware license and ID holders may be asked to revalidate source documents when obtaining a compliant credential. The DMV describes this as a one-time revalidation in ordinary circumstances, with additional documentation potentially required after changes such as a name change or expiration of immigration documents.

Delaware voter identification rules

Delaware poll workers ask voters to show proof of identity at polling places and early voting sites. However, Delaware does not require a photo ID specifically.

The Department of Elections says there is no defined list of acceptable forms of identification. Examples include a Delaware driver's license or state ID, U.S. passport, polling-place card, Social Security card, signed vehicle registration, signed credit card with photo and similar documents that identify the voter by photo or signature.

If a voter does not show ID but is at the correct polling place and appears on the poll list, the voter can complete an Affirmation of Voter Identity and vote.

For a federal provisional ballot, Delaware separately requires proof of identity and address. The Department of Elections lists a current valid photo ID, utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck or other government document showing the voter's name and address.

Renewing a Delaware ID

A Delaware identification card can be renewed at a Division of Motor Vehicles office before it expires. The current DMV information lists a $40 renewal fee and requires the current identification card to be brought to the DMV.

Delaware's federally compliant credential process can require source-document revalidation. If your information has changed, such as your legal name or immigration documentation, additional documents may be required.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Delaware ID

If your Delaware identification card is lost, stolen or damaged, request a replacement through the DMV. The replacement process uses the DMV's identification and record-verification requirements.

If the replacement involves a change in personal information, such as a legal name change, the DMV requires the appropriate supporting documents rather than treating the transaction as a simple duplicate.

If a government ID was stolen, also consider the identity-theft risk. A replacement card fixes the credential problem, but it does not automatically address personal information that may have been exposed.

Name and address changes

Delaware DMV requires supporting documents when the name on a credential changes. The DMV's documentation guidance says applicants should change their name with the Social Security Administration first and wait 48 hours before appearing at the DMV.

For multiple legal name changes, such as several marriages and divorces, Delaware can require documentation for each change connecting the names.

Address changes should update the DMV record so the credential and state records contain current information. Use the DMV's current address-change service for the available online or in-person options.

Delaware IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Delaware DMV can issue driver's licenses and identification cards to non-U.S. citizens who can establish qualifying legal presence and meet the other document requirements.

The DMV verifies immigration status through federal systems such as SAVE. Applicants must provide USCIS or former INS documentation containing an Alien Registration Number or I-94 number that can be electronically verified.

Delaware says it will not issue a new driver's license or ID when the applicant's legal status or authorized length of stay cannot be verified. Non-citizens renewing or requesting a duplicate can be required to provide immigration documents again.

Driving Privilege Card

Delaware has a separate Driving Privilege Card program for qualifying applicants who cannot establish lawful presence. Applicants must provide an SBI receipt, proof of name and date of birth, a Division of Revenue Certification of Filing Compliance covering the prior two years and two separate proofs of Delaware residency.

U.S. citizens and people with valid legal presence are not eligible for a Delaware Driving Privilege Card.

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

Delaware's DMV verifies source documents and warns that false statements, attempted fraud using invalid licenses or identification cards and misrepresentation can result in perjury, denial of services and other consequences.

That verification process is one reason a government credential should not be viewed simply as a card. It is tied to source documents and state records.

Delaware ID law FAQs

Can I get a Delaware ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Delaware DMV issues identification cards separately from driver's licenses.

How many residency documents does Delaware require?

Two separate proofs of Delaware residency are required. The DMV says they must come from separate sources and show the applicant's physical address rather than only a P.O. box.

How much does a Delaware state ID cost?

The current Delaware DMV fee is $40 for an identification card, and the card expires eight years from the applicant's next birthday.

Does Delaware require photo ID to vote?

No. Delaware asks voters to show proof of identity, but it does not require a photo ID specifically. Voters without ID can complete an Affirmation of Voter Identity when they are properly listed at the polling place.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a Delaware ID?

Eligible non-U.S. citizens can obtain a Delaware driver's license or ID when they meet the DMV's legal-presence and documentation requirements. Delaware also has a separate Driving Privilege Card for qualifying applicants who cannot establish lawful presence.

How many documents do I need for a Delaware REAL ID?

The DMV generally requires one primary identity document, one secondary document, proof of Social Security number if eligible and two separate proofs of Delaware residency, along with applicable legal-presence documentation.
