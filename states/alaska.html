Alaska ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Driver's licenses, state ID cards, REAL ID, required documents, rural residency, voter ID, renewals, replacements and other Alaska identification rules.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026. Alaska identification requirements can change. Use this page to understand the rules, then verify current details with the relevant Alaska agency before applying.

Alaska driver's license requirements

Alaska driver's licenses are issued by the Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV). A person generally must be at least 16 to receive a driver's license. Applicants must provide the required identity, lawful-status, Social Security and residence documentation and complete the testing required for the credential they are seeking.

For a first Alaska license, the DMV says applicants must provide documentation establishing their legal name and date of birth, current residence address and Social Security number. The DMV also provides separate requirements for applicants who are new to Alaska, minors and people who need a REAL ID-compliant credential.

Common first-license steps

  1. Complete the Alaska driver license or ID application.
  2. Bring the required identity and lawful-status document.
  3. Provide your Social Security number or the required documentation if you are not eligible for one.
  4. Provide proof of your principal residence.
  5. Pass the applicable knowledge, vision and road tests.
  6. Pay the applicable licensing fee.

New drivers under 18

Alaska allows an instruction permit beginning at age 14. A permit is generally valid for two years and may be renewed once. Applicants under 18 need parental or legal-guardian consent. At age 16, a driver can move toward a provisional license after holding the learner's permit for at least six months and meeting the other requirements.

The Alaska DMV also states that applicants under 18 need to document supervised driving experience and meet the applicable recent driving-record requirements before receiving a provisional license. Provisional restrictions include limits on late-night driving and passengers under 21, subject to listed exceptions.

Alaska state ID card without a driver's license

You do not need to have a driver's license to get an Alaska state identification card. The DMV says anyone living in Alaska can obtain an ID card, including children, and parental consent is not required for the state ID itself.

The Alaska ID card requires documentation of your legal name and date of birth, lawful status, principal residence and Social Security number or proof from the Social Security Administration that you are ineligible for an SSN.

An Alaska state ID can be either a standard credential or a REAL ID-compliant credential. The DMV currently lists the standard ID fee as $15 and the REAL ID fee as $35. Standard ID cards are free for Alaskans age 60 and older, while a REAL ID card for that age group is $20.

Documents required for an Alaska ID or license

Alaska organizes credential documentation into several categories. The exact documents you need depend on whether you are applying for a standard credential or REAL ID, whether your name has changed and whether you have a Social Security number.

Identity & lawful status

Examples include a certified U.S. birth certificate, U.S. passport or passport card, certificate of citizenship or naturalization, certain foreign passports with immigration documentation, permanent resident cards and employment authorization documents.

Social Security

If you have an SSN, Alaska requires it on the application and verifies it against Social Security records. If you do not have an SSN, the DMV accepts documentation from the Social Security Administration showing that you are ineligible for one.

Residence

Examples include utility bills, Alaska voter registration cards, Alaska title or registration, pay stubs, first-class mail, school records, insurance documents, bank statements, leases, mortgages and qualifying employer or organization letters.

Name change

If your current legal name differs from your primary document, Alaska requires original or certified government documents establishing the connection between the names, such as a marriage certificate, divorce decree, adoption record or court order.

Proof of residency in Alaska

Alaska generally requires proof of your principal residence for a driver's license or state ID. A residence document should show your legal name and physical residence address. A post office box or mail cache address does not substitute for a physical residence address.

Alaska has special guidance for rural and off-highway communities where standardized street addresses may not exist. In those situations, documents can use an appropriate physical description of the residence, and the DMV lists examples such as utility service documents, leases, tribal ID cards, employment records and a notarized letter from an appropriate local official.

Rural Alaska matters: If your community does not use conventional street addresses, do not assume a standard address document is required. Review the DMV's rural-residency guidance and contact the DMV if your available documents use a village, milepost or other non-standard physical description.

Alaska REAL ID requirements

Alaska is REAL ID compliant, but a REAL ID-compliant license or ID is not mandatory for every Alaskan with a non-commercial credential. Since May 7, 2025, federal REAL ID rules affect people who use a state-issued license or ID to board commercial domestic flights or enter certain federal facilities. A passport or another federally accepted credential can also satisfy those federal identification requirements.

To obtain an Alaska REAL ID, applicants must apply in person and provide source documents. The Alaska DMV lists proof of U.S. citizenship, permanent residency or other lawful status, proof of name change when applicable, two documents verifying Alaska residency and an SSN or SSA ineligibility letter.

REAL ID document checklist

  • Identity/lawful status: qualifying passport, birth certificate, citizenship/naturalization certificate, permanent resident card, employment authorization document or other accepted document.
  • SSN: your complete Social Security number or an SSA ineligibility letter issued within the required period.
  • Alaska residence: two qualifying documents showing your first and last name and current residence address.
  • Name change: documentation connecting your current legal name to your primary document when needed.

Alaska's DMV notes that an individual can have only one REAL ID credential at a time: a REAL ID driver's license or a REAL ID identification card, not both.

Alaska voter ID requirements

Alaska's Division of Elections administers voter identification rules. When voting in person, voters are generally asked to present a valid photo ID such as an Alaska driver's license, Alaska DMV ID card, U.S. passport, federal military ID or another current or valid photo ID, including certain student or tribal IDs.

If a voter does not have a photo ID, Alaska election materials list alternatives that can show the voter's name and current address, including a birth certificate, hunting or fishing license, current utility bill, government check, bank statement, paycheck or another government document. If a voter arrives without the required identification, Alaska provides a questioned-ballot process in which the voter's eligibility and identity are reviewed after the election.

Alaska's Division of Elections also explains that the identification requirement can be waived when the election official knows the voter's identity, with a separate rule for certain first-time voters whose identification has not been validated through the state's registration process.

Renewing an Alaska driver's license or state ID

Alaska allows eligible residents to renew a driver's license online. The DMV recommends starting when the credential is within one year of expiration. Eligibility can depend on age, prior renewal method and whether your personal information has changed.

For drivers turning 21, the license expires 90 days after the 21st birthday. The DMV says the renewal after turning 21 includes an alcohol awareness test and changes the credential from the vertical format used for younger drivers.

State ID cards can also be renewed. The Alaska DMV says an ID card can be renewed up to one year before expiration. Online renewal eligibility depends on how the previous renewal was completed.

Lost, stolen or damaged Alaska ID

If your Alaska driver's license or state ID is lost, the DMV provides an online duplicate-card service for eligible applicants. The online duplicate process can be used for either a standard credential or the same type of REAL ID credential currently held. The DMV states that online duplicate applicants must be at least 18 and meet additional eligibility checks.

If you need to upgrade from a standard credential to REAL ID, that is a separate in-person transaction because the DMV must verify the required source documents.

For an Alaska credential that is lost while you are outside the state, the DMV also provides online replacement and temporary-license options for eligible residents.

Name and address changes

If your legal name changes, Alaska DMV requires documentation establishing the link between your old and new names. The DMV says your name should first be updated with Social Security before the DMV name change is completed.

For an address change, the DMV provides a process for obtaining a new credential showing the current address. Keeping the DMV record current is important because residence information is also used in credential and voter-registration processes.

Alaska IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Alaska can issue driver's licenses and identification cards to eligible non-U.S. citizens. The DMV requires immigration documentation that establishes lawful status and uses the federal SAVE system to verify immigration information.

The DMV notes that a credential issued to a person with temporary authorized stay may have a duration shorter than eight years so that its expiration matches the authorized stay. Applicants should bring original, valid and unexpired immigration documents, including relevant USCIS documents and any I-797 extension documentation that applies to their status.

Using a genuine government ID vs. an altered or counterfeit ID

An Alaska driver's license or state ID is a government-issued credential tied to the information and documents verified by the DMV. Altering, counterfeiting or using another person's identification is different from obtaining a legitimate credential through the state's application process.

If you are researching what people mean by terms such as “fake ID,” “novelty ID” or counterfeit identification, this educational explanation of what a fake ID is provides background on the terminology. It is not a substitute for Alaska's official credential requirements.

Where to apply

Alaska DMV services are available through DMV offices and participating business partners, with additional mobile DMV services for rural communities. Some transactions can be completed online, including eligible renewals and duplicate credentials.

Before visiting, check the Alaska DMV for the current office list, available services, testing requirements and any appointment instructions. Rural residents should also review the DMV's rural-driving and mobile-service information.

Alaska ID law FAQs

Can I get an Alaska ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Alaska issues state identification cards to people living in the state, including children. A driver's license is not required to obtain the state ID card.

Is REAL ID mandatory in Alaska?

No, not for every non-commercial Alaska credential holder. REAL ID requirements apply to certain federal uses, including boarding commercial domestic flights and entering certain federal facilities. A passport or another accepted federal credential can also be used for those purposes.

How many proof-of-residency documents are needed for Alaska REAL ID?

The Alaska DMV requires two documents verifying Alaska residency for a REAL ID application.

Can someone without an SSN get an Alaska ID?

Potentially. If you do not have an SSN, the Alaska DMV says you must provide documentation from the Social Security Administration showing that you have not been issued one.

How old do you have to be to get an Alaska driver's license?

Alaska may issue a driver's license at age 16. Applicants under 18 have additional requirements, including parental consent and a period of instruction-permit driving before receiving a provisional license.

Can I replace a lost Alaska license online?

Eligible applicants age 18 or older can use Alaska's online duplicate-card service. The DMV checks the information against its records before processing the replacement.

Does Alaska accept non-standard rural addresses?

Yes. Alaska DMV provides specific guidance for rural communities where standard street addresses are not used and allows qualifying documents to use physical descriptions appropriate to the community.

What ID can I use to vote in Alaska?

Alaska election materials list several acceptable photo IDs, including an Alaska driver's license, Alaska DMV ID card, U.S. passport, federal military ID and certain current student or tribal IDs. Other documents showing name and current address can also be used in circumstances described by the Division of Elections.
