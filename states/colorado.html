Colorado ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Colorado issues driver's licenses, instruction permits and identification cards through the Division of Motor Vehicles. This guide covers the documents, residency rules, REAL ID standards, voter identification, renewal, replacement and the different credential rules that apply to Colorado residents.

Colorado driver's license requirements

Colorado's licensing process depends on age, driving history and whether you are transferring a credential from another state. New drivers may need an instruction permit and must complete the testing and graduated licensing requirements that apply to their age.

When applying for a Colorado credential, the DMV verifies identity and other required information. Applicants transferring driving privileges from another state may also need their previous license or a motor vehicle record that meets Colorado's requirements.

Teen and minor drivers

Colorado uses a graduated driver licensing system. Applicants under 21 have additional rules around permits, supervised driving and minor licenses. The exact requirements depend on age, so a teenager should use the DMV's age-specific instructions rather than an adult checklist.

Moving to Colorado

An out-of-state license can be transferred when the applicant meets Colorado's requirements. The DMV may use the existing license to waive some testing requirements, but identity, Social Security and Colorado address documentation can still be required.

Colorado state ID without a driver's license

You can have a Colorado identification card without holding a Colorado driver's license. The ID is a government-issued identity credential and is not permission to drive.

For U.S. citizens and permanent residents, Colorado's identification checklist requires acceptable identity and lawful-presence documents, an SSN and proof of Colorado residential address. The standard credential issued through this process is REAL ID compliant.

Colorado also issues identification cards under its Road and Community Safety Act for qualifying applicants who cannot demonstrate lawful presence. Those cards carry a notice stating that they are not valid for federal identification, voting or public benefit purposes.

Documents needed for a Colorado ID

Colorado's DMV document lists are specific. Documents generally must be original, certified or otherwise accepted by the issuing agency. Photocopies, notarized documents and ordinary online printouts are not accepted where the checklist requires an original or certified document.

Identity and lawful presence

For U.S. citizens and permanent residents, accepted documents include items such as a U.S. passport, certified U.S. birth certificate, certificate of naturalization or citizenship and qualifying immigration documents. The DMV can require a combination of documents when one document alone does not establish all required information.

Social Security number

The standard identification checklist says U.S. citizens and permanent residents must provide their Social Security number. An applicant can present a document showing the number, such as a Social Security card, W-2 or 1099, or provide the number for verification through the Social Security Administration.

Translation and name differences

Documents that are not in English must be translated under Colorado DMV rules. If documents show different names because of marriage, adoption, a court order or another legal change, certified evidence connecting the names may be required.

Proof of Colorado residency

Colorado focuses on the applicant's physical residential address. Documents showing only a P.O. Box are not enough when the DMV requires proof of physical residence.

Examples of acceptable address documents include a utility bill, credit-card statement, bank statement, pay stub, insurance policy, lease or mortgage document, vehicle registration and other documents accepted by the DMV.

For many applicants, two documents are required. They should show the applicant's name, full Colorado residential address and a date within the applicable period. Colorado's standard address rules generally require documents dated within one year.

If you already hold a Colorado license, permit or ID that is valid or expired less than one year and shows your current physical address, the DMV may allow that credential to satisfy the address requirement instead of two separate documents.

Colorado REAL ID requirements

Colorado's standard credentials for U.S. citizens and permanent residents are REAL ID compliant when issued under the standard DMV documentation process. The REAL ID requirement is therefore built into the normal document-verification process rather than being a completely separate card program.

For a standard credential, the DMV verifies identity and lawful presence, Social Security information and Colorado residential address. The specific documents depend on the applicant's circumstances.

Federal use

A REAL ID-compliant Colorado credential can be used for federal purposes covered by the REAL ID rules, including TSA identity requirements for domestic air travel. A credential issued under the CO-RCSA program is different and carries a federal-use restriction on the face of the card.

Colorado voter identification rules

Colorado requires identification when voting in person. The Secretary of State's accepted-ID list includes a valid Colorado driver's license, a valid Colorado identification card, a valid government-issued photo ID from another U.S. state or territory, a U.S. passport, military ID and qualifying tribal identification.

Colorado also recognizes additional identification documents in circumstances where the voter does not have one of the primary photo IDs. The Secretary of State maintains the current list, so voters should check the official election guidance before an election.

Mail voters can also be asked to provide a copy of acceptable identification in specific circumstances, including certain first-time voting situations.

Renewing a Colorado ID

Colorado offers renewal options based on the type of credential and the applicant's status. The DMV's renewal form distinguishes U.S. citizens and permanent residents, undocumented residents and temporarily legal residents.

Temporarily legal residents must renew in person according to the DMV's current renewal instructions. Other applicants may qualify for renewal by mail or electronically when the applicable requirements are met.

The DMV's current renewal form lists the regular identification card fee as $13, with no fee for customers over 60.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Colorado ID

A lost, stolen or destroyed Colorado credential can be replaced through the DMV. The exact process depends on the credential type and whether you are in Colorado or temporarily out of state.

Colorado provides a duplicate process for driver licenses and instruction permits. A first duplicate costs $12 and a second costs $16 under the DMV fee schedule. Additional duplicates require department approval and the applicable fee.

For identification cards, use the DMV's current duplicate or replacement instructions rather than assuming that the driver's-license replacement process applies in exactly the same way.

Name and address changes

If your legal name changes, Colorado DMV requires documentation that establishes the legal change. When source documents show different names, certified evidence connecting those names may be required.

Address documentation is important because Colorado requires a physical residential address for many DMV transactions. If you change the address on your credential, the DMV may require two current proof-of-address documents unless you qualify for an exception based on an existing Colorado credential.

Colorado IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Colorado has different credential rules depending on immigration status.

Lawfully present residents

Temporarily legal residents can apply for Colorado credentials when they provide documents establishing identity, date of birth, lawful presence and current Colorado residence. The validity of a credential can depend on the applicant's legal-presence documentation.

Undocumented residents

Colorado provides a separate standard credential path for qualifying undocumented residents under the Colorado Road and Community Safety Act. The DMV checklist requires identity documentation and proof of a current Colorado residential address. The resulting credential carries the statement that it is not valid for federal identification, voting or public benefit purposes.

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

A genuine Colorado credential is tied to a state issuance process and identity records. A counterfeit card, an altered genuine card or a genuine card used by someone other than the person it identifies are different situations, but each can create legal problems when it is used to misrepresent identity or eligibility.

For background on the distinction between counterfeit documents, altered IDs and novelty cards, see Fake ID vs. Novelty ID. That article is general educational material; Colorado law and official DMV guidance control what is legal in Colorado.

Colorado ID law FAQs

Can I get a Colorado ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Colorado issues identification cards separately from driver licenses.

How many proof-of-residency documents does Colorado require?

Many applicants must provide two documents showing a current physical Colorado residential address. Some current Colorado credential holders can meet the requirement with their existing credential when it shows the current address.

Are Colorado IDs REAL ID compliant?

Standard credentials issued to U.S. citizens and permanent residents through the regular DMV process are REAL ID compliant. CO-RCSA credentials are different and carry a federal-use restriction.

Does Colorado require ID to vote?

Yes. Colorado requires identification for in-person voting and publishes a list of acceptable forms of ID.

Can undocumented residents get a Colorado ID?

Colorado has a separate credential process for qualifying undocumented residents under the Colorado Road and Community Safety Act. The resulting credential is marked as not valid for federal identification, voting or public benefit purposes.

How much does a Colorado ID cost?

The DMV's current renewal form lists $13 for an identification card and $0 for customers over 60. Other credential categories can have different fees.
