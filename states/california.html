California ID Laws: What You Need to Know

California uses the Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) to issue driver’s licenses and identification cards. This guide explains the documents, residency rules, REAL ID requirements, voter identification rules, renewal and replacement procedures that matter when you need a California credential.

Rules can differ depending on whether you are applying for a standard credential, a REAL ID, a senior ID or a credential for someone with limited legal presence.

California driver's license requirements

A California driver’s license is a driving credential, while a California identification card is used to establish identity or age and does not permit driving. DMV handles both.

For a new credential, California DMV requires documentation that establishes your identity and other information required for the credential you are requesting. The exact process also depends on age and whether you are applying for a REAL ID.

New drivers and younger applicants

California uses a graduated licensing system for younger drivers. A person who has not previously held a California license generally starts with an instruction permit and must complete the applicable knowledge, training and behind-the-wheel requirements before receiving a provisional license.

Because teen licensing rules contain additional education, supervised-driving and testing requirements, applicants should use the DMV's current instructions for their age rather than relying on a general adult-license checklist.

Existing out-of-state drivers

If you move to California and establish residency, the DMV process is different from applying as a first-time driver. The DMV determines which documents and tests apply based on your circumstances.

California state ID without a driver's license

You do not need a California driver’s license to have a California identification card. DMV may issue an ID card to a person of any age.

To apply, you complete a DL/ID card application, visit a DMV office, provide your Social Security number, establish your identity, provide residency documentation when required, pay the applicable fee, have your thumbprint scanned and have your photograph taken.

California has three ID card categories described by DMV: regular ID cards, REAL ID cards and senior citizen ID cards. A senior citizen ID is available without a fee to eligible applicants age 62 or older.

Documents needed for a California ID

The documents you need depend on the credential you are requesting. DMV distinguishes identity documents, Social Security documentation and California residency documents.

Identity

DMV accepts specific documents to establish identity and date of birth. Examples include a valid U.S. passport or passport card and an original or certified U.S. birth certificate. The document list is broader than these examples, so the DMV checklist should be used before an appointment.

Social Security number

California DMV requires applicants for an ID card to provide their Social Security number. If your situation does not fit the standard documentation path, check DMV's instructions before applying rather than assuming that another document will be accepted.

Residency

For a California ID application, DMV may require a residency document, particularly when you have never had a California DL or ID card. Examples include a lease, mortgage bill, utility bill, insurance document, bank or credit-card record, government document, school document or California vehicle registration.

Residency documents generally need to show your name and California mailing address. DMV also provides rules for situations where a document is in another household member's name and you need to establish the relationship.

Proof of California residency

California's residency rules are document-based. A qualifying document should identify you and show your California address as required by DMV.

For a first-time REAL ID application, the requirement is more specific: you must provide two different printed documents showing your California mailing address. Both documents must show your first and last name and the same mailing address listed on the REAL ID application.

If you use a P.O. Box, DMV says one of the documents must show both the P.O. Box and physical residence address, while the other may show only the P.O. Box.

California REAL ID requirements

California's REAL ID is a federally compliant driver’s license or identification card. Since May 7, 2025, a standard California ID or driver’s license is not accepted by TSA for domestic air travel. A federal-compliant credential such as a REAL ID, U.S. passport or another accepted document is required for those purposes.

What you need for a REAL ID

  • One acceptable proof of identity and date of birth.
  • Proof of Social Security number.
  • Two different printed proofs of California residency.

California DMV's REAL ID checklist includes documents such as a valid U.S. passport or passport card, an original or certified U.S. birth certificate and certain immigration documents as proof of identity. The exact list should be checked before the DMV visit.

Non-U.S. citizens

California allows lawfully present non-U.S. citizens to obtain a REAL ID when they can establish current legal presence with an accepted document. For people with temporary legal status, the credential can be limited to the period of documented legal presence.

California voter identification rules

California generally does not require voters to show identification when voting. There is a limited exception for certain first-time voters in a federal election.

The exception can apply when a voter is voting for the first time in a federal election, registered by mail or online and did not provide a California ID number or the last four digits of a Social Security number when registering.

When identification is required, California allows several forms of photo and non-photo identification, including a California driver’s license or state ID, U.S. passport, student or military ID, utility bill, bank statement, paycheck or certain government mail.

California's Secretary of State also explains the separate requirements for registering to vote. Registration requires U.S. citizenship and California residency, among other qualifications.

Renewing a California ID

California ID cards can be renewed online, by mail or in person when the applicant meets the DMV's eligibility requirements for that method.

For online renewal, DMV says an ID applicant generally must be within 90 days before or 12 months after expiration, must not need to change the address or personal description and must not be applying for a REAL ID for the first time.

For mail renewal, additional restrictions apply, including limits involving previous mail or internet renewals, an ID that has been expired for more than one year and a name change.

In-person renewal involves a new application and, as applicable, the required fee, thumbprint and photograph.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged California ID

If your California ID card is lost, stolen or damaged, DMV provides a replacement process. ID cards are not eligible for the online replacement driver's-license application, so an ID replacement requires a new application and a visit to a DMV office.

At the office, DMV may validate your photograph and scan your thumbprint. The replacement card is mailed after the application is completed.

If an ID was stolen, replacing the card is only one part of protecting yourself. Review financial accounts and other records for unfamiliar activity if the lost credential could expose personal information.

Name and address changes

California DMV requires drivers and ID cardholders to keep their records current. A legal name change requires supporting documentation and a new DL/ID application.

For a name change, DMV says you should first update your name with the Social Security Administration. DMV then verifies the information with SSA before completing the credential update.

California law requires you to notify DMV within 10 days of changing your address. An address change can be submitted online, by mail or in person. Updating an address does not by itself require a new physical card unless you request one or another transaction requires it.

California IDs for non-U.S. citizens

California issues DL/ID credentials to eligible non-U.S. citizens who can provide the documentation required for their status. For a REAL ID, DMV requires evidence of current legal presence in addition to identity, Social Security and residency documentation.

Documents can include a Permanent Resident Card, certain visas and other immigration or federal documents recognized by DMV. Applicants with temporary legal status may receive a limited-term credential tied to the period of documented legal presence.

Because immigration documents and expiration dates vary, applicants should use the California DMV's current legal-presence and REAL ID document lists for their specific status.

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

A California driver's license or ID card is a government-issued credential. Using an altered credential, someone else's credential or a document that falsely represents government authority can create legal and identity-related problems.

For general background on how counterfeit identification is defined and why legitimate credentials contain security features, see California Fake ID Red Flags: Signs to Check First. The article is useful as background on the distinction between a genuine California credential and a counterfeit or altered one; it is not a substitute for California law or DMV guidance.

California ID law FAQs

Can I get a California ID without a driver's license?

Yes. California DMV can issue an identification card to a person of any age, and an ID card does not authorize driving.

How many residency documents do I need for a California REAL ID?

Two different printed documents showing your California mailing address are required for a first-time REAL ID application.

Can I use a standard California ID to fly domestically?

Not for TSA purposes after May 7, 2025. You need a REAL ID or another federally accepted form of identification such as a U.S. passport.

Does California require ID to vote?

California generally does not require voters to show identification at the polls, although a limited first-time federal-election exception applies in certain circumstances.

How much is a California ID?

A regular California ID card costs $40. Reduced-fee and no-fee programs are available to qualifying applicants, and eligible seniors age 62 or older can receive a no-fee senior ID.

How quickly do I need to report an address change?

California law requires you to notify DMV within 10 days after changing your address.
