Texas ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Texas has detailed documentation requirements for driver's licenses and identification cards, including proof of identity, lawful presence, Social Security information and Texas residency. This guide covers standard credentials, REAL ID, voter identification, renewals, replacements, fees and requirements for non-U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Texas driver's license requirements

Texas driver's-license applicants must establish identity, date of birth, citizenship or lawful presence, Social Security information and Texas residency. Applicants must present original or certified documents when required; ordinary photocopies are not accepted. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Identity documents

Texas uses a three-level identity-document system. An applicant can generally establish identity with one primary document, two secondary documents, or one secondary document plus two supporting documents. The information on the documents must match, and legal name-change documents are required when the names differ. citeturn0search3

Citizenship or lawful presence

Applicants must provide one approved document establishing U.S. citizenship or lawful presence. Examples include a U.S. birth certificate, valid U.S. passport, Certificate of Citizenship or Naturalization, Permanent Resident Card, Employment Authorization Document or qualifying foreign passport and I-94. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Social Security number

Texas verifies the SSN electronically with the federal government. If the number cannot be verified, the applicant may need additional documentation. Texas also provides a limited affidavit process for a person who has never been issued an SSN, is not eligible for one and meets the DPS conditions. citeturn0search0

Insurance and vehicle registration

If the applicant owns a vehicle, Texas DPS asks for current Texas vehicle registration or title and current automobile insurance information as part of the application checklist. citeturn0search0

Texas identification card requirements

Texas issues identification cards that are valid for up to six years. Applicants must establish U.S. citizenship or lawful presence, Texas residency, identity and Social Security number. citeturn0search2

Applying for a Texas ID

  • Complete the ID card application
  • Provide citizenship or lawful-presence documentation
  • Provide identity documentation
  • Provide Texas residency documents
  • Provide SSN information
  • Provide signature and thumbprints
  • Have a photograph taken
  • Pay the applicable fee

After issuance, Texas provides a temporary identification card and mails the permanent card. DPS says the permanent card generally arrives in approximately two to three weeks. citeturn0search2

ID card fees

The current DPS fee schedule lists a new or renewed ID card at $16 for applicants age 59 and younger and $6 for applicants age 60 and older. A replacement ID card is $11. citeturn2search0

Documents needed for a Texas ID

Texas DPS uses a structured document system for identity, citizenship or lawful presence, SSN and residency. The agency can reject documents that cannot be verified and may request additional documentation. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Primary identity documents

Examples include a valid U.S. passport, Texas driver's license or ID that has not been expired more than two years, qualifying citizenship documents, permanent resident card, employment authorization document, qualifying foreign passport with visa and I-94, and certain military IDs. citeturn0search3

Secondary and supporting documents

If an applicant does not have a primary identity document, Texas allows combinations of secondary and supporting documents. Examples include certified birth certificates, consular birth reports, Social Security cards, W-2s, prior driver's licenses, school records, voter registration cards and certain government records. citeturn0search3

Legal name changes

Texas accepts documents such as a marriage license, divorce decree, revised birth certificate, court-ordered name change or Department of State Health Services marriage verification letter. Documents must be original or certified copies. citeturn0search3

Proof of Texas residency

Texas requires two printed documents showing the applicant's name and Texas residential address. One document generally must verify that the applicant has lived in Texas for at least 30 days. The 30-day requirement is waived in certain cases, including when surrendering a valid, unexpired out-of-state license or ID. citeturn0search1

Common residency documents

  • Deed, mortgage statement or residential lease
  • Texas voter registration card
  • Texas vehicle registration or title
  • Utility or residential service bill
  • Paycheck or payment stub
  • W-2, 1099 or 1098
  • Homeowners or renters insurance
  • Automobile insurance
  • Bank or credit-card statement
  • Government statement
  • Texas school report card or transcript
  • Military or Veterans Affairs document showing residence

Printed electronic statements are accepted when they meet the DPS rules. The two documents normally must come from different companies or agencies, although multiple services from the same local governmental provider can qualify under the stated exception. citeturn0search1

Applicants under 18

A Texas Residency Affidavit can be used by an applicant under 18 who cannot otherwise show the required residency documents. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Alternative addresses

Texas provides special residency procedures for certain people, including participants in the Address Confidentiality Program, judges and their spouses, active-duty military members and dependents, people incarcerated in Texas facilities and certain youth in state care. citeturn0search1

Texas REAL ID requirements

Texas driver's licenses and identification cards are issued under federal REAL ID standards. Texas DPS says REAL ID-compliant credentials are accepted for official federal purposes such as entering secure federal facilities and boarding domestic flights. citeturn0search4

REAL ID checklist

  • One proof of citizenship or lawful presence
  • One proof of identity
  • Social Security number
  • Two proofs of Texas residency
  • Legal name-change documents when applicable

Texas DPS's REAL ID checklist states that applicants should bring original or certified documents and warns that laminated documents and photocopies may not be accepted. citeturn0search0

Federal use

Beginning May 7, 2025, federal rules require a REAL ID-compliant state credential or another federally accepted document for covered federal purposes. A passport is one alternative for federal identification purposes. citeturn0search4

Document review

Texas DPS provides an interactive REAL ID Document Check tool that lets applicants review the documentation they have before visiting a Driver License Office. The tool does not guarantee issuance, because DPS retains authority to verify and accept documents. citeturn0search0

New Texas residents

Texas DPS requires new residents who hold an out-of-state driver's license or ID to surrender the current credential when receiving a Texas credential. Texas residency documentation is still required, but the 30-day residency-document requirement is waived when surrendering a valid, unexpired out-of-state license or ID. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Out-of-state credentials

Texas does not allow a person to retain an active out-of-state license or ID after receiving a Texas credential. DPS requires the current out-of-state credential to be surrendered. citeturn0search0

Foreign credentials

Non-U.S. citizens transferring into Texas must establish lawful presence and identity using the documents that apply to their immigration status. A qualifying foreign passport, visa and I-94 can be among the accepted documents. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Texas voter identification requirements

Texas law requires voters who possess one of the state's seven acceptable forms of photo identification to present that identification when voting in person. The Texas Secretary of State lists the current accepted forms and procedures. citeturn0search5turn0search6

Seven acceptable photo IDs

  • Texas driver's license issued by DPS
  • Texas Election Identification Certificate issued by DPS
  • Texas Personal Identification Card issued by DPS
  • Texas Handgun License issued by DPS
  • U.S. military identification card with photograph
  • U.S. Citizenship Certificate with photograph
  • U.S. passport book or passport card

Texas's Secretary of State identifies these seven documents as the approved photo-ID forms for voters who possess them. citeturn0search6

Expired photo ID

For voters age 18 through 69, an acceptable photo ID can be expired for up to four years and still be accepted. Voters age 70 or older can use an otherwise acceptable photo ID regardless of how long it has been expired. citeturn0search7

Reasonable Impediment Declaration

A voter who does not possess and cannot reasonably obtain one of the seven approved photo IDs can complete a Reasonable Impediment Declaration and present an alternative document. Examples include a utility bill, bank statement, government check or voter registration certificate. citeturn0search6turn0search7

Provisional voting

A voter who does not present acceptable ID and does not qualify for the reasonable-impediment process may cast a provisional ballot. The voter must follow the Secretary of State's current procedures for resolving the identification issue after Election Day. citeturn0search7

Election Identification Certificate

Texas DPS issues an Election Identification Certificate for eligible voters who need an acceptable photo ID for voting. The certificate is available without a fee. Applicants must meet the DPS identity and eligibility requirements. citeturn0search5

Renewing a Texas driver's license or ID

Texas driver's licenses for adults ages 18 through 84 are generally valid for eight years. Most driver's licenses and ID cards can be renewed up to two years before expiration and up to two years after expiration, subject to eligibility rules. citeturn2search0turn2search6

Online renewal

Eligible U.S. citizens with a Social Security number on file can renew qualifying Class C, M or CM licenses online or by telephone. ID-card holders age 18 and older who are U.S. citizens and have an SSN on file may also qualify for online renewal. citeturn2search6

Age 79 and older

Drivers age 79 or older must renew in person. Online renewal eligibility generally ends before age 79. citeturn2search6turn2search8

Expired more than two years

A Texas driver's license or ID card that has been expired for more than two years cannot simply be renewed. The applicant must follow the new-application process. citeturn2search6

2026 virtual issuance

Texas DPS announced in June 2026 that Virtual Issuance is coming for eligible customers with REAL ID-compliant records, including certain U.S.-citizen adult Class C license and ID transactions. Eligibility depends on the transaction and the customer's record. citeturn2search2

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Texas ID

A duplicate Texas driver's license or identification card costs $11. Eligible customers can use the online replacement service when they have the required card and account information, including the audit number. citeturn2search0turn2search5

Lost or stolen card

If the card is lost, stolen or destroyed and the applicant does not have the information needed for online replacement, Texas DPS requires an appointment at a Driver License Office. citeturn2search9

Stolen identity credential

If a stolen Texas license or ID has been used fraudulently, DPS recommends filing a police report and bringing a copy to the Driver License Office. DPS can evaluate whether issuing a new credential number is appropriate to protect the applicant. citeturn2search9

Texas name and address changes

Address changes

Texas requires the address on a driver's license or ID card to be changed within 30 days after moving to a new residence. Address changes can be requested online, by mail or in person when the applicant is eligible for the selected method. citeturn2search3

Name changes

A name change requires accepted legal documentation connecting the old and new names. Texas lists marriage licenses, divorce decrees, revised birth certificates, court orders and certain state health-department verification letters. citeturn0search3

Replacement fee

Changing the address or name requires a replacement credential and the applicable duplicate fee. The current standard duplicate fee is $11. citeturn2search0turn2search3

Texas IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Non-U.S. citizens can qualify for Texas driver's licenses and identification cards when they establish identity, lawful presence, Social Security information and Texas residency under the applicable DPS rules. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Common lawful-presence documents

  • Permanent Resident Card
  • Employment Authorization Document
  • Valid foreign passport with qualifying U.S. visa and I-94
  • Machine-readable immigrant visa with temporary I-551 language and ADIT stamp
  • Other DHS or USCIS documents accepted by DPS

The exact document depends on immigration status and the transaction. DPS verifies the documentation and may request additional evidence. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Limited-term credentials

Texas issues limited-term driver's licenses and ID cards to certain temporary visitors. The credential generally expires when the period of lawful presence expires, or after one year when the lawful-presence document indicates duration of status. citeturn2search0

Renewal

Non-U.S. citizens can use online replacement in eligible cases, but Texas DPS says non-U.S. citizens are not eligible for online renewal. They must follow the applicable in-person or mail process and provide lawful-presence documentation when required. citeturn2search9turn2search6

Texas ID law FAQs

Does Texas have REAL ID?

Yes. Texas driver's licenses and identification cards are issued under federal REAL ID standards. Compliant credentials can be used for covered federal purposes.

How many proof-of-residency documents do I need in Texas?

Generally two printed documents showing your name and Texas residential address. One normally must verify that you have lived in Texas for at least 30 days, although exceptions apply.

How long is a Texas driver's license valid?

For adults ages 18 through 84, a standard Class A, B or C license is generally valid for eight years.

How long is a Texas ID card valid?

A Texas identification card is generally valid for six years.

Does Texas require photo ID to vote?

Texas requires voters who possess one of the seven approved photo IDs to present it when voting in person. A reasonable-impediment process is available for voters who cannot reasonably obtain an approved photo ID.

What if I do not have an acceptable Texas voter ID?

If you cannot reasonably obtain an approved photo ID, you can complete a Reasonable Impediment Declaration and provide an alternative qualifying document. Provisional voting is also available under the applicable election procedures.

How much does a Texas driver's license cost?

A standard Class A, B or C license for ages 18 through 84 costs $33 for a new license or renewal.

How much does a Texas ID card cost?

The current fee is $16 for applicants age 59 and younger and $6 for applicants age 60 and older. A replacement ID costs $11.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a Texas driver's license?

Yes, qualifying non-U.S. citizens can obtain Texas credentials by providing the identity, lawful-presence, Social Security and residency documentation required for their status.

How soon must I change my Texas license address after moving?

Texas requires the address on a driver's license or ID card to be changed within 30 days after moving to a new residence.
