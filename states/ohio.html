Ohio ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Ohio offers Standard and federally compliant driver licenses and identification cards. This guide explains Ohio's document requirements, residency proof, REAL ID, voter identification, renewals, replacements, fees and requirements for non-U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Ohio driver's license requirements

Ohio driver's licenses are issued by the Bureau of Motor Vehicles. First-time applicants must provide the identity and legal-presence documents required by BMV and complete the testing applicable to their license class and age.

Standard driver's license

Ohio's Standard card documentation establishes the applicant's full legal name, date of birth, legal presence in the United States, Social Security number and Ohio street address. A first-time applicant must provide the required identity documents even when choosing a Standard card. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Compliant driver's license

An Ohio Compliant card meets federal REAL ID standards. Applicants must provide full legal name, date of birth, legal presence, SSN, two Ohio street-address documents from different sources and proof of any applicable name change. citeturn0search0

New drivers

Applicants follow Ohio's graduated driver licensing process when applicable. A temporary instruction permit identification card is issued before the applicant progresses through the required supervised-driving and testing stages.

Insurance

Ohio requires proof of financial responsibility for drivers. The insurance requirement is separate from the identity documents used to issue the driver's license.

Ohio state identification cards

Ohio identification cards are available with no minimum age. Applicants can choose a Standard ID or a Compliant ID. The card is a non-driver credential and cannot be used to operate a motor vehicle. citeturn0search2

Standard ID

A Standard ID requires proof of full legal name, date of birth, SSN if ever assigned, legal presence and Ohio residency. First-time applicants must provide the complete required identity documentation. citeturn0search2

Compliant ID

A Compliant ID requires the additional federal documentation, including two Ohio street-address documents from different sources. citeturn0search0

Free identification cards

Ohio BMV states that residents age 17 and older can receive an identification card for free under the state's ID-card program. Free replacement is also available to qualifying residents age 17 and older. citeturn0search2

Documents needed for an Ohio ID

Ohio BMV's acceptable-document system is organized around the facts the applicant must prove rather than a single universal document combination.

Full legal name and date of birth

Applicants can use qualifying birth, passport, citizenship, immigration and government-issued documents listed by BMV. The exact combination depends on the applicant's citizenship and credential type. citeturn0search0

Social Security number

Ohio requires an SSN for a Standard or Compliant credential when one has been assigned. BMV's acceptable-document rules identify the specific records that can establish the number. citeturn0search0

Legal presence

Applicants must establish legal presence in the United States. U.S. citizens generally use a qualifying birth certificate, passport or citizenship document. Non-U.S. citizens use qualifying immigration documentation. citeturn1search3

Name changes

If the current legal name differs from the name shown on a birth certificate, passport or other foundational document, BMV requires documents connecting the former name to the current legal name. citeturn0search0

Proof of Ohio residency

Ohio distinguishes the street address requirement for Standard cards from the stronger residency documentation required for a Compliant card.

Standard credential

A Standard credential requires proof of an Ohio street address. The acceptable-document list contains the specific documents BMV will accept. citeturn0search0

Compliant credential

A Compliant credential requires two documents from different sources proving the Ohio street address. The documents must be from the acceptable-document list. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Address accuracy

Applicants should make sure the address documents show the current Ohio street address and match the information supplied to BMV. A mailing address alone does not substitute for the required street-address evidence on a Compliant application.

Ohio REAL ID requirements

Ohio calls its federally compliant credential a Compliant Card. The card is identified by a star and is designed to meet federal REAL ID standards. A Standard Card remains available for people who do not need a federally compliant credential. citeturn0search1

Compliant card checklist

  • Full legal name
  • Date of birth
  • Legal presence in the United States
  • Social Security number
  • Two Ohio street-address documents from different sources
  • Proof of name change, if applicable
Standard vs. Compliant: Ohio says a Standard license or ID cannot be used for covered federal purposes such as domestic air travel without another federally accepted document. A Compliant card can satisfy the REAL ID requirement for those covered uses. citeturn0search1

Renewal

Ohio says an existing Standard license or ID does not require the applicant to resubmit the additional Compliant documentation simply to renew the Standard credential. A first-time Compliant credential requires the full documentation set. citeturn0search1

Ohio voter identification requirements

Ohio law requires voters to present a valid, unexpired photo ID showing the voter's name and photograph when voting. The Ohio Secretary of State's current list includes Ohio driver's licenses, Ohio state IDs, Ohio BMV interim ID forms, U.S. passports and qualifying military or veterans' IDs. citeturn0search4turn0search6

Accepted forms of photo ID

  • Ohio driver's license
  • Ohio state identification card
  • Ohio BMV interim identification form
  • U.S. passport or passport card
  • U.S. military or military dependent ID
  • Ohio National Guard ID
  • U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs ID

The ID must be valid and unexpired. Ohio does not accept utility bills, bank statements, paychecks, Social Security cards, birth certificates, insurance cards, out-of-state driver's licenses or Ohio Mobile ID as voter identification. citeturn0search4

Noncitizen marker on an Ohio credential

An Ohio driver's license or ID containing a “NONCITIZEN” identifier is not sufficient by itself for voting. The voter may use it only together with additional proof of U.S. citizenship as specified by the Secretary of State. citeturn0search4turn0search8

If you do not have photo ID

A voter who cannot provide acceptable photo identification must receive a provisional ballot. For the provisional ballot to count, the voter must provide an acceptable form of identification to the county board of elections within four days after Election Day. A religious objection to being photographed has a separate affidavit procedure. citeturn0search4turn0search6

Renewing an Ohio driver's license or ID

Ohio allows eligible customers to renew a current driver's license or one that has been expired for less than six months at a deputy registrar license agency or online. A license can be issued for four or eight years depending on the applicant's choice and qualifications. citeturn1search13

Renewal documents

If the current license is not presented for renewal, BMV requires proof of full legal name, date of birth, SSN if assigned, Ohio residency and citizenship or legal presence. A name-change request also requires proof of the change. citeturn1search13

Age 21 renewal rule

Drivers turning 21 cannot renew more than 30 days before their birthday. citeturn1search13

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Ohio ID

Ohio BMV offers duplicate/replacement services for driver's licenses and identification cards. Eligible customers can complete certain transactions online, while others require a visit to a deputy registrar.

Replacement ID

Ohio residents age 17 and older can receive a free replacement state ID under the BMV's current ID-card rules. citeturn0search2

Replacement driver's license

The current BMV fee schedule lists a $29 duplicate operator license fee. Additional transaction requirements depend on the credential and the applicant's circumstances. citeturn0search3

Name and address changes

Name changes

Ohio BMV requires proof connecting the applicant's former legal name to the current legal name when the names on foundational documents do not match. Common qualifying documents include certified marriage, divorce or court documents. citeturn0search0

Address changes

Ohio credentials use an Ohio street address for residency verification. When the address changes, the BMV record should be updated and a replacement credential may be requested when the new address needs to appear on the physical card.

Ohio IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Ohio BMV is required to verify legal presence before issuing a driver's license or identification card. The name on the Ohio credential will generally be the name shown on the applicant's USCIS documents. citeturn1search3

Permanent residents

A person with a valid Permanent Resident Card can receive a regular Ohio driver's license. BMV says the credential may expire in four or eight years depending on the applicant's choice and qualifications. citeturn1search3

Temporary lawful status

Other non-U.S. citizens must provide the immigration documents required by BMV to establish legal presence. The validity period of the Ohio credential can be tied to the applicant's documented immigration status. citeturn1search3

REAL ID and non-citizens

Non-U.S. citizens seeking a Compliant card must meet the federal identity, legal-presence, SSN and residency requirements in addition to the BMV's immigration-document rules. citeturn0search0turn1search3

Counterfeit, altered and fraudulent identification

Ohio BMV requires identity and legal-presence documents that meet its acceptable-document rules. Documents that cannot establish the required facts are not sufficient for issuance, and applicants should use the current BMV document list rather than relying on an unofficial checklist. citeturn0search0

Ohio ID law FAQs

What documents do I need for an Ohio driver's license?

You must establish your full legal name, date of birth, legal presence, Social Security number and Ohio street address. First-time applicants must provide the required identity documents even when choosing a Standard license.

How many proof-of-address documents are needed for Ohio REAL ID?

Two documents from different sources showing your Ohio street address are required for a Compliant credential.

Is Ohio REAL ID mandatory?

No. Ohio offers both Standard and Compliant credentials. A Standard credential can still be used for ordinary state identification, but it does not satisfy covered federal REAL ID uses by itself.

Does Ohio require photo ID to vote?

Yes. Ohio requires a valid, unexpired photo ID showing the voter's name and photograph. If you cannot provide one, a provisional-ballot process is available.

Can I get a free Ohio state ID?

Ohio BMV states that eligible residents age 17 and older can receive an identification card for free, including qualifying replacement IDs.

Can non-U.S. citizens get an Ohio driver's license?

Yes, if they meet Ohio BMV's legal-presence and other licensing requirements. The required immigration documents and credential validity depend on status.

How much does an Ohio driver's license cost?

The current first-issue fee for an operator license at age 21 or older is $27.50 for four years or $54 for eight years. Renewal is $30.25 for four years or $59.40 for eight years.

Can I renew my Ohio license online?

Eligible customers can renew a current license or one expired less than six months online or at a deputy registrar. Some transactions require an in-person visit.
