Iowa ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Iowa issues driver's licenses and non-operator identification cards through the Iowa Department of Transportation. This guide explains Iowa ID requirements, REAL ID documents, residency, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and rules for non-U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Iowa's DMV system is different from states that use a large network of state-operated branches. Iowa has driver's license issuance sites and county treasurer locations for some transactions, so appointment and service availability can vary by location.

Iowa driver's license requirements

Iowa driver's license applicants must establish identity, Social Security information and Iowa residency and complete the testing or graduated licensing requirements that apply to their age and license type.

Drivers under 18

Iowa uses a graduated driver's license system. The first step is an instruction permit, which is available beginning at age 14. Applicants must pass the knowledge and vision tests, provide identification, proof of residency and proof of Social Security number and obtain parent or guardian consent when required.

The graduated system then moves through an intermediate license before a full driver's license. The specific supervised-driving and age requirements depend on the stage of the process.

Adults

Adults applying for an Iowa driver's license must provide the required identity documents and complete the applicable vision, knowledge and driving requirements. The Iowa DOT's document checklist should be used for the exact transaction because requirements differ for first-time applicants, new residents and renewals.

Iowa State ID without a driver's license

Iowa calls its non-driver credential an identification card or non-operator ID. The Iowa DOT says an identification card may be issued to a person regardless of age.

The card can be used as government-issued identification but does not authorize the holder to operate a motor vehicle.

Iowa also provides an Iowa Voter Identification Card through the election system. This is separate from the ordinary non-operator ID and is designed for voters who do not appear in the Iowa DOT driver's license or non-operator ID files.

Turning 21

A person with an Iowa license or ID that still says "Under 21" can optionally purchase a horizontal replacement card on or after the person's 21st birthday. The replacement is not mandatory simply because the person turns 21.

Documents needed for an Iowa ID

Iowa's document process is based on the specific transaction. For a REAL ID, the Iowa DOT summarizes the federal document requirements as one proof of identity, one proof of Social Security number and two pieces of mail proving Iowa residency.

Proof of identity and lawful status

One qualifying document must establish identity, date of birth and lawful status or presence. Examples include a valid U.S. passport, certified U.S. birth certificate, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, certificate of naturalization and qualifying immigration documents.

If the current legal name does not match the identity document, Iowa requires the applicable name-change documentation.

Social Security number

Iowa accepts documents such as a Social Security card, W-2, qualifying 1099, pay stub, certain tax forms, bank statement or military document showing the applicant's current name and full Social Security number.

The Iowa DOT electronically verifies the name, date of birth and SSN with the Social Security Administration. The SSN is not printed on the Iowa license or ID.

People without an SSN

A person who is not authorized for employment does not have to document an SSN, but Iowa verifies the person's USCIS number. A person who is eligible for employment but does not yet have an SSN must obtain and present one.

Proof of Iowa residency

For a REAL ID, Iowa requires two printed documents showing the applicant's current name and current Iowa residential address. The address must be a physical residence, not a P.O. Box.

Accepted examples include an Iowa voter registration card, vehicle registration, insurance document, utility bill, telephone bill, financial-institution statement, credit-card statement, mortgage or lease, property-tax document, pay stub, school enrollment record, government document or certain dated mail.

An Iowa driver's license or ID that has not been expired for more than one year can also appear on the state's residency-document list.

Minors

For a minor, a parent, guardian or custodian can document Iowa residency by signing the required written-consent form. A second printed document showing the minor's name and current Iowa residential address is also required.

Iowa REAL ID requirements

Iowa's REAL ID-compliant license or ID has a gold star in the upper-right corner. A card without the star is still a valid Iowa credential for ordinary state purposes, but it is not accepted for federal purposes covered by REAL ID.

Iowa's REAL ID checklist: Bring one document proving identity, date of birth and lawful status, one document containing your current name and full SSN and two printed documents showing your current name and physical Iowa residential address.

Beginning May 7, 2025, a REAL ID or another federally accepted identity document is required for covered federal purposes such as boarding commercial flights within the United States and accessing certain federal facilities.

Adding the star

Adding the REAL ID star does not cost extra and does not require a new driving test when an existing Iowa driver is simply converting a credential to REAL ID. The applicant must appear in person with the required documents because REAL ID is not available through online renewal when the star is being added for the first time.

Once Iowa has verified the REAL ID documents, the star remains on future renewals. U.S. citizens and eligible permanent residents do not generally need to submit the complete document set again for later renewals unless their information changes.

Iowa voter identification requirements

Iowa voters are required to show an approved form of identification when voting in person. Accepted forms include an Iowa driver's license, Iowa non-operator ID, U.S. passport, U.S. military or veteran ID, tribal ID or Iowa Voter Identification Card.

A registered voter who does not have an Iowa driver's license or non-operator ID can receive an Iowa Voter Identification Card for free. The card can be used for absentee-by-mail, absentee in-person and Election Day voting.

Election Day registration

A voter who registers on Election Day must prove both identity and residence. Iowa lists additional photo IDs that can be used in this situation, including an out-of-state driver's license or non-operator ID, an employer-issued ID and an Iowa high school or college student ID. If the photo ID does not show the current address, another document showing the voter's name and current address can be used as proof of residence.

If you do not have acceptable ID

A voter without the required ID can use Election Day Registration documents to prove identity and residence. A voter who cannot provide the required ID or an attester can be offered a provisional ballot and may provide the necessary documentation within the applicable canvass deadline.

Renewing an Iowa ID or driver's license

Iowa allows eligible U.S. citizens to renew certain driver's licenses and non-operator IDs online. Online renewal is subject to age, expiration, residency, citizenship and record restrictions.

For a driver's license, online renewal is generally available to Iowa residents who are at least 18 and younger than 70, have an eligible non-commercial license and meet the other requirements. A license can generally be renewed online every other issuance.

For a non-operator ID, online renewal is available to eligible Iowa residents who are at least 18 and meet the citizenship, expiration, record and information-change requirements. IDs can also generally be renewed online every other issuance.

If you want to add a REAL ID star for the first time, you must appear in person with the required documents.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Iowa ID

Iowa provides duplicate and replacement transactions for driver's licenses and non-operator IDs. A replacement license or non-operator ID generally costs $10. A defective license can be replaced without charge under the Iowa DOT fee schedule.

Eligible U.S. citizens can complete some replacement transactions online. If the replacement involves adding a REAL ID star for the first time, the applicant must appear in person with the required documents.

If a card is lost or stolen, the replacement transaction creates a new credential. Keep the replacement information with your records and report other identity concerns through the appropriate agency if necessary.

Name and address changes

If your legal name changes, Iowa requires documents establishing the change, such as a certified marriage certificate or other qualifying legal record.

A name or date-of-birth change requires documentation even when the person already has a REAL ID. Iowa's REAL ID guidance specifically says these changes require supporting documents.

For an address change, the Iowa DOT uses the applicant's current Iowa residential address. Certain online replacement or renewal transactions are available only when the applicant has no change to the information stored on the credential.

Iowa IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Iowa issues driver's licenses and identification cards to eligible foreign nationals who establish the lawful status required by federal REAL ID rules and meet the state's other credential requirements.

The Iowa DOT verifies USCIS documentation electronically. Some cases require additional DHS/USCIS review, which can take substantially longer than a normal transaction.

Temporary lawful status

Temporary foreign nationals receive credentials with renewal periods established by Iowa law. Iowa requires temporary foreign nationals to provide proof of lawful status at each renewal.

If a person is not authorized for employment, Iowa does not require an SSN but verifies the USCIS number. If the person is authorized for employment but does not have an SSN, the person must obtain and present one.

Iowa licenses issued to people age 78 and older are valid for two years. Temporary foreign nationals and people under 18 can receive shorter renewal periods under Iowa law. County locations may also charge convenience fees, and credit-card payments have a processing fee.

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

An Iowa driver's license or identification card is a government credential issued to a specific person after the required identity information is verified. A counterfeit card, altered credential or another person's genuine card does not have the same status as a credential issued to the person presenting it.

Iowa ID law FAQs

Can I get an Iowa ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Iowa issues non-operator identification cards to people of any age. The card is for identification and does not authorize driving.

How many documents do I need for an Iowa REAL ID?

Iowa's basic REAL ID structure is one identity document, one Social Security document and two printed documents proving Iowa residency.

Does Iowa require ID to vote?

Yes. Iowa voters generally show an Iowa driver's license, non-operator ID, U.S. passport, military or veteran ID, tribal ID or Iowa Voter Identification Card.

Can I get a free Iowa voter ID?

Yes. Registered voters without an Iowa driver's license or non-operator ID can receive an Iowa Voter Identification Card for free.

Do I need a REAL ID to drive in Iowa?

No. A REAL ID is not required for ordinary driving or voting. It is used for federal purposes covered by the REAL ID rules.

Can non-U.S. citizens get an Iowa ID?

Yes, eligible foreign nationals can receive Iowa credentials after the DOT verifies their lawful status and they satisfy the other requirements.

Can I renew my Iowa ID online?

Many eligible U.S. citizens can renew a non-operator ID online, generally every other issuance. First-time REAL ID conversion requires an in-person visit.
