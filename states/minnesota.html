Minnesota ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Minnesota issues driver's licenses and identification cards through Driver and Vehicle Services. This guide explains Minnesota ID requirements, standard and REAL ID credentials, Enhanced IDs, residency, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and the rules for people who do not have U.S. immigration status.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Minnesota driver's license requirements

Minnesota's standard driver's license process requires applicants to establish identity and provide a Minnesota residence address. The standard credential does not require an applicant to demonstrate U.S. citizenship or lawful presence. citeturn4search0turn4search25

For a Class D driver's license, an applicant must complete the applicable knowledge and road-skills testing. Minnesota's Driver's License for All information sheet states that standard-license applicants provide two documents, with one including their full name and date of birth, and provide their home address in Minnesota. citeturn4search25

Class D license

The Class D credential is Minnesota's ordinary passenger-vehicle driver's license. The current published fee schedule lists a $32 fee for a regular Class D license. citeturn2search2

New Minnesota residents

People moving to Minnesota must establish a Minnesota residence address when applying for a Minnesota driver's license or identification card. Applicants who hold a credential from another jurisdiction should bring it and the documents required for the Minnesota transaction.

Vision and testing

Driver's license applicants must meet Minnesota's vision and testing requirements. Renewal transactions can also require a vision screening depending on the transaction and the applicant's circumstances.

Minnesota State ID without a driver's license

Minnesota offers a standard identification card to residents who need government-issued identification but do not need a driver's license. The standard ID does not require proof of immigration status. Minnesota's Driver's License for All law expressly covers standard identification cards as well as standard driver's licenses. citeturn4search25turn4search0

The published fee schedule lists the standard Minnesota identification card at $21.50 for people under 65 and $20.75 for people age 65 and older. A qualifying person with a physical or developmental disability or qualified mental illness can receive an ID for $0.50 under the published schedule. citeturn2search2

Standard ID vs REAL ID

A standard Minnesota ID is not the same as a REAL ID. The standard card is available under Minnesota's noncompliant credential rules, while a REAL ID requires federal identity, lawful-status and Social Security documentation. citeturn4search0turn1search32

Documents needed for a Minnesota ID

Minnesota uses different document standards for standard, REAL ID and Enhanced credentials. The current DVS document guide should be checked before applying because the required documents depend on the credential selected.

Standard license or ID

For a standard credential, Minnesota's Driver's License for All guidance says applicants provide two documents, with one including the applicant's full name and date of birth. The applicant must also provide a Minnesota home address. citeturn4search25

REAL ID

REAL ID applicants need one document proving identity, date of birth and lawful U.S. presence, knowledge of the full Social Security number and two different documents proving current Minnesota residency. Original documents or documents certified by the issuing agency are required. citeturn1search32turn1search33

Name changes

If the name on the identity document does not match the current name, Minnesota requires proof of every name change from the original name to the current name. Examples include certified marriage certificates, divorce decrees or court-ordered name changes. citeturn1search32

Proof of Minnesota residency

Minnesota's REAL ID document guide requires two different documents proving current Minnesota residence. The name and address must match the application and a P.O. Box alone does not satisfy the requirement. citeturn0search9

Examples of accepted residency documents

  • Valid Minnesota driver's license, permit or ID
  • Current insurance declaration page or card
  • Federal or state income tax return
  • Minnesota property tax statement
  • Minnesota vehicle certificate of title
  • Mortgage documents
  • Filed property deed or title
  • Utility bill or utility hook-up work order
  • U.S. bank or financial statement
  • Residential lease agreement
  • Employment pay stub
  • Minnesota unemployment benefit statement
  • Supplemental Security Income award statement

Some documents have age limits, such as utility bills, financial statements and leases. Under 18 applicants can use a parent or guardian's qualifying residency document in the circumstances described by DVS. citeturn0search9

Minnesota REAL ID requirements

Minnesota began issuing REAL ID-compliant credentials in 2018. A REAL ID is optional for ordinary state driving and identification purposes but is used for covered federal purposes. Since May 7, 2025, adults 18 and older need a REAL ID, Enhanced credential or another federally accepted identity document for covered purposes such as domestic air travel. citeturn1search32

REAL ID checklist: one document proving identity, date of birth and lawful U.S. presence; your full Social Security number; and two different documents proving current Minnesota residency. Original or certified documents are required. citeturn1search32turn1search34

REAL ID and standard credentials

A standard Minnesota license or ID remains a valid state credential. It simply does not satisfy the federal REAL ID standard. A person can instead use another federally accepted document, such as a valid U.S. passport, for covered federal purposes. citeturn1search32

REAL ID cost

Minnesota's published fee materials state that REAL ID uses the applicable regular license or ID fee rather than a separate REAL ID surcharge. Enhanced credentials, by contrast, have an additional $15 fee. citeturn2search0turn2search2

Minnesota Enhanced ID and Enhanced Driver's License

Minnesota Enhanced Driver's Licenses and Enhanced Identification Cards are separate from standard and REAL ID credentials. Only Minnesota residents who are also U.S. citizens and at least 16 years old may apply for an Enhanced Driver's License or Enhanced Identification Card. citeturn1search31

An Enhanced credential requires proof of date of birth, full legal name, Social Security number, photographic identity, U.S. citizenship and two different Minnesota residency documents. Applicants also complete an interview questionnaire. citeturn1search31

Border travel

Enhanced credentials are federally recognized documents for qualifying land and sea travel between the United States and Canada, Mexico, Bermuda and the Caribbean under the Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative. They are also accepted for covered federal identification purposes. citeturn2search3

Enhanced fee

The published Minnesota fee schedule adds $15 to the regular applicable driver's license or identification-card fee for an Enhanced credential. citeturn2search0turn2search2

Minnesota voter identification requirements

Minnesota does not require an actively registered voter to bring identification to the polls. The Secretary of State says that if registration is current and active, the voter does not need to bring ID. citeturn1search3

When proof of residence is required

If a person needs to register or update registration at the polling place, or has not voted in four years or more, proof of residence is required before voting. citeturn1search3

Election Day registration options

Minnesota offers several ways to establish residence on Election Day. One option is a Minnesota driver's license, learner's permit or ID showing the current name and address. Another is an approved photo ID combined with a document showing the current name and address. A registered voter from the same precinct can also vouch for a voter's residence. citeturn0search0turn0search5

Automatic voter registration

Minnesota's automatic voter registration system began in 2024. When an eligible person applies for or renews a state-issued ID and provides citizenship-affirming documentation, DVS can send the information to election officials for verification and registration. Eligible applicants can opt out after receiving the notice. citeturn0search3

Renewing a Minnesota ID or driver's license

Minnesota driver's licenses and identification cards can be renewed through DVS services when the applicant is eligible. Renewal may be completed online or through an office transaction depending on the credential, photo requirements and other circumstances.

A published Minnesota fee schedule lists $32 for a regular Class D license renewal and $21.50 for a standard ID card for applicants under 65. citeturn2search2

Photo updates

Minnesota requires periodic in-person photo updates. A standard license or ID can generally be renewed through remote services when eligible, but an office visit is required when the applicant must update the photo or satisfy another in-person requirement.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Minnesota ID

Minnesota provides duplicate driver's license and identification-card services. The published fee schedule lists a $17 duplicate driver's license or ID card fee. citeturn2search2

If the replacement also involves a legal name change, REAL ID upgrade or another document issue, the applicant may need to provide additional documentation and complete the transaction in person.

Name and address changes

Minnesota requires proof of each legal name change when the name on an identity document differs from the current name. For REAL ID and Enhanced applications, the name-change chain must connect the identity document to the current legal name. citeturn1search32turn1search31

The Minnesota driver's license or ID application must include a Minnesota residence address. When voter registration is linked to an eligible state-issued ID transaction, a name or address change can also update the voter's registration record under the automatic registration process. citeturn4search1turn0search3

Minnesota IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Minnesota has an important distinction between its standard noncompliant credentials and REAL ID or Enhanced credentials.

Standard license or ID

A person applying for a standard Minnesota driver's license or identification card is not required to demonstrate U.S. citizenship or lawful presence. Minnesota's Driver's License for All law specifically expanded standard credentials to applicants who do not have proof of immigration status. citeturn4search0turn4search25

REAL ID

A REAL ID is different. The applicant must provide documentation establishing lawful U.S. status, along with the Social Security and residency requirements. citeturn1search32

Enhanced credential

An Enhanced Driver's License or Enhanced ID is available only to Minnesota residents who are U.S. citizens and at least 16 years old. Citizenship documentation is part of the application. citeturn1search31

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

A Minnesota driver's license or identification card is a government-issued credential tied to the person to whom it was issued. A counterfeit credential, altered genuine credential or another person's genuine card does not become a valid Minnesota ID for the person presenting it.

Minnesota ID law FAQs

Can I get a Minnesota ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Minnesota offers a standard identification card to residents who need official identification but do not need a driver's license.

How many residency documents do I need for a Minnesota REAL ID?

You need two different documents showing your current Minnesota name and address. A P.O. Box alone does not satisfy the residency requirement.

Does Minnesota require immigration status for a standard driver's license?

No. Minnesota's Driver's License for All law allows a standard driver's license or ID without proof of U.S. citizenship or lawful presence.

Do I need a REAL ID to drive in Minnesota?

No. A standard Minnesota credential remains valid for state driving and identification purposes. REAL ID matters for covered federal purposes.

Does Minnesota require ID to vote?

An actively registered voter does not need to bring ID to the polls. People registering or updating registration at the polling place must provide proof of residence through one of Minnesota's accepted methods.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a Minnesota driver's license?

Yes. Eligible applicants can obtain a standard Minnesota driver's license or ID without proving immigration status. REAL ID and Enhanced credentials have separate federal status and citizenship requirements.

Is Minnesota Enhanced ID the same as REAL ID?

No. Enhanced credentials are separate from REAL ID and have additional requirements, including U.S. citizenship. They also have border-travel functionality.

How much does a Minnesota state ID cost?

The published standard fee is $21.50 for applicants under 65 and $20.75 for applicants age 65 or older. Some qualifying applicants can receive an ID for $0.50.
