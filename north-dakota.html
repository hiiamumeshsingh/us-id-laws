North Dakota ID Laws: What You Need to Know

North Dakota uses a straightforward document system for driver's licenses and non-driver identification cards, with additional documentation for REAL ID. The state also has a distinctive voter-ID system because North Dakota does not use voter registration.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

North Dakota driver's license requirements

The minimum driving age in North Dakota is 16. A restricted license may be obtained at age 15 under the state's graduated licensing rules. A parent or legal guardian must sign for an applicant under 18 who is receiving a permit or license. citeturn0search0

Identity and legal presence

Every applicant for a permit, driver's license or identification card must show proof of identity, date of birth and legal presence in the United States. NDDOT accepts documents such as a certified U.S. birth certificate, valid U.S. passport, Certificate of Naturalization, Certificate of Citizenship, valid Permanent Resident Card, Employment Authorization Card or qualifying foreign passport with I-94. citeturn0search0

Social Security number

NDDOT requires a Social Security number unless the applicant is a nonimmigrant who is not eligible for one or qualifies for the state's affidavit process for someone who was not assigned a Social Security number. citeturn0search0

Testing

North Dakota charges $5 for the written test and $5 for the road test. The exact tests required depend on the applicant's licensing history and the type of credential sought. citeturn0search0

New residents

A person who becomes a North Dakota resident can generally use a non-commercial out-of-state license for up to 60 days. An out-of-state commercial license can be used for up to 30 days. citeturn0search0turn0search3

North Dakota non-driver identification cards

North Dakota offers non-driver photo identification cards to residents who need official identification but do not need a driver's license. An ID card may be obtained at any age, and the applicant must visit a North Dakota Driver License Site. citeturn0search1

Basic requirements

  • Proof of identity, date of birth and legal presence
  • Social Security number or qualifying exception
  • Proof of North Dakota residence address
  • Certified or original documents rather than photocopies

NDDOT says a person who has lived in North Dakota for 90 consecutive days is considered a resident for purposes of a non-driver photo ID, subject to the state's residency rules. citeturn0search1

ID fees

A North Dakota ID is free for applicants age 18 and older. The fee is $8 for applicants under 18. Replacement of a lost, stolen or destroyed ID card is $8. citeturn0search1

Documents needed for a North Dakota ID

NDDOT's documentation rules apply to driver's licenses, permits and identification cards. Documents must generally be original or certified copies. Hospital certificates and ordinary photocopies are not accepted. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Identity, date of birth and legal presence

Acceptable documents include a state-certified U.S. birth certificate, valid U.S. passport or passport card, Report of Birth Abroad, Certificate of Naturalization, Certificate of Citizenship, Permanent Resident Card, Employment Authorization Card or qualifying foreign passport and immigration documentation. citeturn0search0

Name changes

If the current name differs from the identity document, NDDOT accepts a certified government-issued marriage certificate, certified adoption document or certified court order authorizing the name change. The court order must have the required court seal. citeturn0search0

Social Security documentation

For REAL ID, NDDOT specifically lists a Social Security card, W-2, SSA 1099, non-SSA 1099 or pay stub containing the applicant's name and full Social Security number. citeturn0search2turn0search14

Proof of North Dakota residency

NDDOT requires proof of a North Dakota resident address. The documents must contain the applicant's name and current physical residence address. A P.O. Box is not accepted as proof of residence address. citeturn0search0

Common residency documents

  • Current mortgage, lease or rental document
  • Current homeowner's or renter's insurance policy
  • Current utility bill
  • Current financial statement
  • Current pay stub or employer statement
  • Current vehicle insurance policy or statement
  • Current government-issued property tax statement
  • School or college transcript issued within six months
  • Current formal resident statement from a property owner or renter
  • Relief agency or shelter certification
  • Parent's proof of address for a minor child

A current resident certificate or qualifying Tribal ID with a North Dakota resident address may also be used. citeturn0search0turn0search2

Standard vs. REAL ID

For a non-REAL-ID credential, NDDOT generally requires one proof of North Dakota residence address. A REAL ID applicant must provide two residence-address documents. citeturn0search3turn0search14

North Dakota REAL ID requirements

North Dakota offers REAL ID as an opt-in credential. A gold star in the upper-right corner identifies a REAL ID-compliant license or identification card. NDDOT requires an in-person transaction with the complete REAL ID document set. citeturn0search2

REAL ID checklist

  • One proof of identity, date of birth and legal presence
  • One proof of Social Security number
  • Proof of legal name change, if applicable
  • Two current proofs of North Dakota residence address
  • Original or certified documents
Digital documents are not accepted for the REAL ID document review. NDDOT's current checklist says REAL ID documents must be in print form and that photocopies are not accepted. citeturn0search14

REAL ID and federal travel

NDDOT states that a REAL ID can be used for covered federal purposes such as domestic airline travel and access to federal facilities where identification is required. A passport remains the document used for international travel. citeturn0search2

Do you have to get REAL ID?

No. NDDOT provides an opt-in/opt-out choice. A person who opts out can continue with a non-federal North Dakota credential and use another federally accepted identity document for covered federal purposes. citeturn0search2

North Dakota voter identification requirements

North Dakota does not use voter registration. To vote, a qualified elector must be a U.S. citizen, at least 18 years old and a resident of the state who has lived in the precinct for at least 30 days before the election. A valid form of identification is required before receiving a ballot. citeturn1search1turn1search5

Accepted forms of voter ID

  • North Dakota driver's license
  • North Dakota non-driver identification card
  • Qualifying Tribal government ID or Tribal letter
  • Long-term care certificate for eligible residents of North Dakota long-term care facilities

The identification used for voting generally must show the voter's legal name, current North Dakota residential street address and date of birth. citeturn1search0turn1search5

If your ID has an old address

A North Dakota-issued ID that is otherwise valid can be supplemented with documentation showing the updated address. Examples include a current bank statement, utility bill, paycheck or government document. A printed higher-education document combined with a student photo ID can also qualify for students under the stated rules. citeturn1search0

If you do not have acceptable ID

A voter who cannot present valid identification or the required supplemental information can cast a set-aside ballot. The voter must then provide or update the required identification information before the County Canvassing Board meets. The Secretary of State says this can be done within 13 days after the election under the applicable process. citeturn1search0turn1search6

Absentee voting

North Dakota's voter-identification requirements also apply to absentee voting. Absentee applications require ID information that can be verified against the voter record. Voters whose ID has an outdated address can provide qualifying supplemental documentation. citeturn1search4

Renewing a North Dakota driver's license or ID

North Dakota offers online driver-license renewal for eligible customers as well as renewal at Driver License Sites. The current non-commercial license renewal fee is $15. citeturn0search5

Expired licenses

Driving privileges are no longer valid after midnight on the license holder's birthday. If a license has been expired for more than one year, the applicant must pass both the knowledge and road tests before becoming relicensed. citeturn0search5

REAL ID renewal

If a person already has a REAL ID, NDDOT says the REAL ID documents do not normally need to be presented at the next visit. Documentation is required again if there has been a name, address or gender change. citeturn0search7

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged North Dakota ID

NDDOT allows eligible residents to request a duplicate driver's license online when the required license information is available. The applicant must still be a North Dakota resident and the driving privilege cannot be suspended, revoked or cancelled. citeturn0search4

Replacement fees

The current duplicate driver's license or permit fee is $8. NDDOT lists a $3 fee when the duplicate transaction is for a name or address change only. Replacement of a non-driver ID card is $8. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Replacing a credential with REAL ID

If you are obtaining your first REAL ID, NDDOT requires an in-person transaction with the full REAL ID documentation. A routine duplicate does not automatically convert a non-federal credential into REAL ID. citeturn0search4turn0search2

Name and address changes

Name changes

A legal name change must be supported by an original or certified document such as a government-issued marriage certificate, adoption document or certified court order. citeturn0search0

Address changes

North Dakota requires current physical residence information on qualifying address documents. If the address changes, NDDOT provides an online address-update service. A new physical credential may require proof of the updated residence address. citeturn0search4turn0search7

North Dakota IDs for non-U.S. citizens

North Dakota accepts certain immigration documents as proof of legal presence for driver's licenses and identification cards. Examples include a valid Permanent Resident Card, Employment Authorization Card, qualifying foreign passport with I-94 and certain refugee or asylee documentation. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Social Security exceptions

A nonimmigrant who is not eligible for a Social Security number can qualify for the state's exception process. NDDOT also provides an affidavit option for a person who was not assigned a Social Security number. citeturn0search0

REAL ID

REAL ID requires the applicant to establish legal presence through one of the documents on NDDOT's REAL ID checklist. A qualifying foreign passport must be accompanied by the immigration documentation specified by NDDOT. citeturn0search2turn0search14

Voting status on North Dakota credentials

North Dakota IDs issued to non-U.S. citizens can carry an indicator showing that the holder is not eligible to vote. The Secretary of State explains that this information helps election officials identify voting eligibility. A naturalized citizen can update the citizenship status on the NDDOT record by presenting proof of citizenship. citeturn1search0turn1search7

Counterfeit, altered and fraudulent identification

NDDOT requires original or certified identity documents and does not accept ordinary photocopies or hospital certificates for credential issuance. REAL ID applicants must bring the required printed documents to the in-person review. citeturn0search0turn0search14

North Dakota ID law FAQs

What documents do I need for a North Dakota driver's license?

You generally need proof of identity, date of birth and legal presence, Social Security documentation unless an exception applies and proof of North Dakota residence address.

How many proofs of residency are required for North Dakota REAL ID?

Two current documents showing your North Dakota physical residence address are required.

Is North Dakota REAL ID mandatory?

No. North Dakota lets residents opt in or opt out of REAL ID. A REAL ID has a gold star and is intended for covered federal purposes.

Does North Dakota require ID to vote?

Yes. North Dakota requires a valid form of identification before a qualified elector receives a ballot. Accepted forms include a North Dakota driver's license, non-driver ID, qualifying Tribal ID or letter and a long-term care certificate for eligible residents.

What if my North Dakota ID has an old address when I vote?

You can generally use a non-updated North Dakota ID with qualifying supplemental information showing your current address. If you cannot meet those requirements, a set-aside ballot process is available.

Can I get a free North Dakota state ID?

Yes. NDDOT lists the non-driver ID as free for applicants age 18 and older. A fee applies to applicants under 18 and to replacement cards.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a North Dakota ID?

Yes. Applicants must provide qualifying proof of legal presence and meet the applicable identity, Social Security and residency requirements.

How much is a North Dakota driver's license?

The current non-commercial driver's license fee is $15. Written and road tests are $5 each, and a duplicate license is $8.

How long can a new resident use an out-of-state license?

A non-commercial out-of-state license can generally be used for 60 days after establishing North Dakota residency. Commercial licenses have a 30-day period.
