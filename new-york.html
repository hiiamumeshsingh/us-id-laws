New York ID Laws: What You Need to Know

New York uses a points-based identity system for DMV credentials and offers Standard, REAL ID and Enhanced documents. This guide explains the documents, residency proof, voter identification rules, renewal, replacement, fees and requirements for non-U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

New York driver's license requirements

New York applicants must complete the DMV application process and provide the identity, age and residence documents required for the credential they are seeking. License and learner-permit applicants must also pass a vision test. citeturn1search8

Standard driver's license

A Standard New York driver's license is marked NOT FOR FEDERAL PURPOSES. The current DMV document guide generally requires proof of date of birth, at least 6 points of proof of name and one proof of New York State residence. citeturn3search6turn0search7

REAL ID driver's license

A REAL ID license requires proof of Social Security documentation, proof of citizenship or lawful status, at least 6 points of proof of name and two proofs of New York State residency. There is no additional REAL ID fee beyond the normal transaction fee. citeturn3search6turn1search3

Enhanced driver's license

An Enhanced Driver License is available to U.S. citizens who are New York residents. In addition to normal licensing requirements, it carries an extra $30 fee and can be used instead of a passport for certain land and sea travel between the United States and Canada, Mexico and some Caribbean countries. It is not a substitute for a passport for international air travel. citeturn1search3

New York non-driver identification cards

New York DMV issues non-driver photo ID cards to people who need state identification but do not need a driver's license. Applicants use the same core DMV documentation framework, with requirements varying by Standard, REAL ID or Enhanced credential. citeturn3search6

Standard non-driver ID

A Standard ID is not federally recognized for purposes covered by the REAL ID Act. The current DMV guide calls for proof of date of birth, 6 points of proof of name and one proof of New York residence for the standard credential. citeturn3search6

REAL ID non-driver ID

A REAL ID non-driver ID requires the federal documentation used for a REAL ID credential, including proof of Social Security documentation, lawful status or citizenship, 6 points of proof of name and two proofs of New York residency. citeturn3search6turn1search3

Enhanced non-driver ID

An Enhanced Non-Driver ID is available to U.S. citizens and carries the same additional $30 Enhanced-document fee. citeturn1search3

New York's 6-point identity system

New York DMV assigns point values to many identity documents. Applicants generally need documents totaling at least 6 points, along with proof of date of birth. Original or certified documents are required and at least one document must contain the applicant's signature. citeturn0search8turn3search5

Document condition

DMV does not accept documents with alterations or erasures. Foreign-language documents generally need a certified English translation. DMV also states that fraudulent documents may be confiscated and can expose an applicant to criminal prosecution. citeturn1search8

Proof of New York residency

The number of residency documents depends on the credential. A Standard license or permit generally requires one proof of New York State residence. REAL ID and Enhanced credentials generally require two. citeturn0search7turn1search3

Examples

  • New York State license, permit or non-driver ID
  • Recent bank statement
  • Recent pay stub
  • Utility bill
  • Credit card statement
  • W-2 or other qualifying tax document
  • Other DMV-approved documents showing the current New York address

The address must be the applicant's current New York residence. A P.O. Box is not accepted as the residence address on the qualifying proof. Certain electronic statements can be used when printed. citeturn1search3turn3search7

One document per source or type

DMV limits duplicate proof from the same source or document type. For example, two statements from the same financial institution may not count as two separate proofs. citeturn1search8

New York REAL ID requirements

REAL ID is optional in New York. It is the credential designed to meet federal identification standards for covered uses such as boarding domestic flights and entering certain federal facilities when identification is required. citeturn1search3

REAL ID checklist

  • Proof of Social Security documentation
  • Proof of U.S. citizenship or lawful status
  • At least 6 points of proof of name
  • Proof of date of birth
  • Two proofs of current New York State residency
  • Original or certified acceptable documents
No extra REAL ID fee: New York DMV says there is no additional fee specifically for REAL ID. Normal license or non-driver ID transaction fees still apply. citeturn1search3

REAL ID vs Enhanced

Both can satisfy federal REAL ID requirements. The Enhanced credential adds a separate border-travel function for eligible U.S. citizens and carries an additional $30 fee. citeturn1search3

New York voter identification requirements

New York does not generally require voters to present a photo ID when voting. The state instead verifies voter identity during registration using a New York DMV number, the last four digits of a Social Security number or, where those are unavailable, another accepted identity document. citeturn0search0turn0search9

When ID may be requested at the polls

If the Board of Elections cannot verify a voter's identity before Election Day, the voter may be asked for identification when voting for the first time. Accepted options can include a valid photo ID, current utility bill, bank statement, paycheck, government check or another government document showing the voter's name and address. citeturn0search0turn0search6

Who can register to vote

New York requires voters to be U.S. citizens and 18 by Election Day. A voter must generally have been a resident of New York State and the relevant county, city or village for at least 30 days before the election and cannot be serving a disqualifying felony incarceration sentence. citeturn0search1turn3search0

2026 general election registration deadline

For the November 3, 2026 General Election, New York's Board of Elections lists October 24, 2026 as the deadline for voter-registration applications to be received. citeturn3search1

Renewing a New York driver's license or ID

New York DMV allows eligible customers to renew certain credentials online, by mail or at a DMV office. The renewal method and documents depend on the credential and the applicant's circumstances.

Common driver's license renewal fees

For the most common Class D or DJ motorist license, the current renewal fee is $64.50. An additional Metropolitan Commuter Transportation District fee applies in the listed counties and can bring the total to $80.50 for an eight-year license. citeturn1search0

REAL ID renewal

There is no additional REAL ID fee. A person converting during an early renewal must meet the REAL ID documentation requirements. citeturn1search3

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged New York ID

New York DMV provides replacement services for licenses, permits and non-driver IDs. Eligible customers can replace certain Standard, REAL ID or Enhanced credentials by the available online, mail or office process. citeturn3search3

Replacement fee

The current DMV replacement page lists a $17.50 payment for replacement of a license or permit by mail under the specified eligibility conditions. A different fee structure can apply to non-driver IDs and office transactions. citeturn3search3turn1search1

If you lost an ID because of a crime, DMV law also provides circumstances in which an identification-card fee may be waived for a qualifying victim of crime. citeturn1search7

Name and address changes

Name changes

For a legal name change, New York DMV generally requires the current credential or qualifying identity documents plus an original or certified marriage certificate, divorce document or court order. REAL ID and Enhanced credentials have additional documentation rules because the DMV must establish the applicant's full legal name. citeturn3search2

Address changes

New York DMV provides a separate address-change process. When a new physical credential is needed, the applicant may need to complete a new document transaction and pay the applicable fee.

New York IDs for non-U.S. citizens

New York's Green Light Law allows people age 16 and older to apply for a Standard, non-commercial driver's license or learner permit regardless of citizenship or lawful status in the United States. A Standard credential is not a federal-purpose credential. citeturn1search2turn1search5

Standard license without a Social Security number

New York DMV states that an applicant for a Standard license or permit can apply without a Social Security card or SSA ineligibility letter. The applicant still must satisfy the DMV's identity, age, document and residency requirements. citeturn1search2

REAL ID for non-citizens

REAL ID has different federal documentation requirements. Applicants must provide proof of lawful status and Social Security documentation or an SSA ineligibility letter as specified by DMV. Temporary lawful status may require current immigration documentation. citeturn1search8turn1search2

Foreign passports and immigration documents

DMV accepts certain foreign passports with qualifying visas, I-94 documentation or I-551 evidence for specific purposes. A foreign passport by itself is not sufficient proof of lawful status for a REAL ID or Enhanced credential. citeturn1search8

Counterfeit, altered and fraudulent identification

New York DMV requires original or certified documents and states that it will not accept altered or erased documents. DMV may confiscate a document that appears fraudulent and warns that submitting a fraudulent document can lead to criminal prosecution. citeturn1search8

New York ID law FAQs

What documents do I need for a New York driver's license?

You generally need proof of date of birth, at least 6 points of proof of name and proof of New York residence. REAL ID applicants have additional federal documentation and two residency-proof requirements.

How does New York's 6-point ID system work?

DMV assigns point values to identity documents. The documents you submit must total at least 6 points, together with the required proof of date of birth and other credential-specific documents.

How many proofs of residency do I need?

A Standard license or permit generally requires one proof of New York residence. REAL ID and Enhanced credentials generally require two.

Is REAL ID mandatory in New York?

No. REAL ID is optional. It is designed for covered federal purposes such as domestic air travel and certain federal facilities when identification is required.

What is an Enhanced New York driver's license?

An Enhanced license is available to U.S. citizens and can be used for certain land and sea border crossings. It has an additional $30 fee.

Can undocumented immigrants get a New York driver's license?

Eligible people age 16 and older can apply for a Standard, non-commercial New York driver's license or learner permit regardless of citizenship or lawful immigration status under the Green Light Law.

Does New York require photo ID to vote?

New York does not generally require voters to show photo ID at the polls. Identification may be requested when voting for the first time if the Board of Elections could not verify the voter's identity before Election Day.

How much does a New York driver's license cost?

The current renewal fee for the common Class D or DJ license is $64.50, with an MCTD fee in specified counties. Other transactions have different fees.
