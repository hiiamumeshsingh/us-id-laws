Nevada ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Nevada issues driver's licenses and identification cards through the Department of Motor Vehicles. This guide explains Nevada ID requirements, REAL ID, residency documents, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and Driver Authorization Cards.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Nevada DMV has three credential pathways: REAL ID, standard Nevada license or ID and the Driver Authorization Card. The documents and uses differ between them, so applicants should choose the credential type before gathering documents. See Nevada DMV identity and residency requirements.

Nevada driver's license requirements

Adults applying for a Nevada driver's license must provide the required identity, Social Security and Nevada residency documents and complete the applicable testing. Nevada's standard noncommercial license is generally valid for eight years, while licenses issued to people age 65 or older are generally valid for four years. citeturn0search5turn1search0

Identity and residency documents

Nevada requires one proof of identity, proof of all legal name changes when applicable, one proof of Social Security number and two proofs of Nevada residential address. Documents must generally be valid originals or certified U.S. copies. Non-English documents require DMV-approved translations. citeturn0search0

Testing

First-time applicants generally complete a vision test, written knowledge test and road skills test unless a testing exemption or waiver applies. Nevada DMV provides separate testing rules for adults, teens and applicants transferring an out-of-state credential. citeturn0search5

New residents

Nevada requires residents to obtain a Nevada license rather than continuing to use an out-of-state credential once they establish Nevada residency. The DMV describes residency through factors such as declaring Nevada a legal residence, living and working or operating a business in Nevada, using Nevada as the home state for vehicles or claiming Nevada residency for state benefits. citeturn0search3

Nevada identification cards

Nevada ID cards are available to residents beginning at age 10. The ID application uses the same basic identity, Social Security and residency documentation system as driver's licenses. Applicants receive an interim paper document while the permanent card is mailed. citeturn0search2turn1search5

One credential at a time

You cannot hold both a Nevada identification card and a driver's license, even when one credential was issued by another state. Nevada DMV also states that standard and REAL ID credentials are separate card types for federal-use purposes.

ID validity and fees

Current Nevada DMV fees vary by age. The DMV lists $9.50 for an ID transaction for applicants under 18, $21.50 for applicants age 18 through 64 and $11.50 for applicants age 65 or older. citeturn1search5turn1search0

Documents needed for a Nevada ID

Nevada's basic document package includes one identity document, one Social Security document, two Nevada residential-address documents and proof of every applicable legal name change. Documents must generally be valid originals or certified copies. citeturn0search0

Identity

Accepted identity documents include a U.S. passport or passport card, original or certified U.S. birth certificate, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, Certificate of Naturalization, Certificate of Citizenship and qualifying immigration documents. A valid REAL ID issued by another state can also satisfy certain Nevada identity requirements. citeturn0search0

Social Security number

Nevada requires one document showing the Social Security number. The DMV lists Social Security cards and other approved documents among the acceptable evidence.

Legal name changes

If the applicant's current name differs from the identity document, Nevada requires the legal records connecting the names, such as a certified marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order. Name changes must be completed with the Social Security Administration before the DMV transaction. citeturn1search3

Proof of Nevada residency

Nevada requires two proofs of Nevada residential address. The documents must show the applicant's name and Nevada residential address. Examples include a utility bill, lease, bank or credit-card statement, employment check stub, insurance record, tax record or other DMV-approved document. citeturn0search0turn1search6

Physical address versus mailing address

REAL ID credentials must show the applicant's physical address. Standard Nevada licenses and ID cards may use a physical or mailing address where the DMV rules allow it. A P.O. Box does not substitute for the required physical residence when the physical address is required. citeturn0search0

Special residency situations

Nevada provides specific forms for people who cannot document a conventional residence, including certain shelter, relief-agency and property-owner affidavit situations. Homeless applicants can also use the DMV's special fee-exemption process when they meet the applicable age and declaration requirements. citeturn1search6turn1search0

Nevada REAL ID requirements

Nevada REAL ID is optional for state purposes. A compliant driver's license, permit or ID has a gold Nevada outline with a star cutout. Since April 2026, the federal enforcement deadline has passed, so a standard Nevada license or ID is no longer accepted by itself for covered domestic air travel or certain secure federal facilities. citeturn0search1

What you need for REAL ID

  • One valid, unexpired proof of identity
  • One proof of Social Security number
  • Two proofs of Nevada residential address
  • Proof of every legal name change, if applicable
  • DMV-approved translations for non-English documents

REAL ID applications and upgrades must be completed in person. Nevada says the foundational documents are a one-time requirement for a REAL ID credential, so applicants who already have a Nevada REAL ID generally do not repeat the full document process at every renewal. citeturn0search1turn0search4

REAL ID is not required to vote. Nevada DMV specifically says REAL ID is not required for voting or voter registration. It is aimed at federal “official purposes” such as domestic air travel and secure federal facilities. citeturn0search1

REAL ID fee

Nevada DMV currently lists an additional $8.50 for upgrading a noncommercial license and $7.50 for upgrading an ID card when the REAL ID transaction is separate. When REAL ID is added during another qualifying license or ID transaction, the additional charge can be $0. citeturn1search7

Nevada Driver Authorization Card

Nevada's Driver Authorization Card, or DAC, is a driving credential for people who do not meet the requirements for a standard Nevada driver's license. A DAC is marked “NOT VALID FOR ID” and does not establish identity for federal purposes.

DAC applicants still provide approved identity and residency documents and complete the applicable testing. The DAC is generally valid for four years and has a current initial fee of $22.50. Renewal is also $22.50 and replacement is $17. citeturn1search6

A DAC is not a state ID card. It gives driving privileges but cannot be used as identification for domestic air travel or secure federal facilities.

Nevada voter identification requirements

Nevada's voting system is different from states that require every voter to present photo ID at the polling place. When a Nevada voter registration application is successfully verified, the voter is placed in active status and does not have to show identification when voting for the first time in Nevada. Signature verification is performed at the polling location. citeturn2search0

When ID can be required

If the information on a registration application cannot be verified, the voter can be placed in “active pending” status. Such a voter cannot vote until the information is confirmed or the voter appears in person with photo identification and proof of residency. citeturn2search0

Registration and DMV records

Nevada's voter registration verification process compares registration information with DMV, Social Security Administration, vital-record and statewide voter-registration records. The Secretary of State also provides online registration and updates for eligible voters. citeturn1search1turn1search2

Same-day registration

Nevada allows same-day voter registration. Voters registering or updating their registration during the election period must follow the identification and residency verification procedures that apply to the registration method and voter status.

Renewing a Nevada driver's license or ID

Nevada offers online, kiosk, mail and in-person renewal options for eligible driver's license and ID holders. The available method depends on the credential, the applicant's record and whether an in-person photo or document update is required.

License validity

Most Nevada driver's licenses are valid for eight years. Licenses issued to people age 65 or older and Driver Authorization Cards are generally valid for four years. Limited-term credentials can have a shorter validity period tied to lawful status. citeturn1search0

REAL ID renewal

Once Nevada has verified the foundational REAL ID documents, a compliant credential remains REAL ID compliant at renewal. A person who has a standard credential and wants to upgrade to REAL ID must appear in person with the required documents. citeturn0search1turn0search4

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Nevada ID

Nevada allows eligible residents to order a duplicate driver's license, Driver Authorization Card or ID card online through MyDMV or in person. Online replacement requires enough information to establish the applicant's existing DMV record. If that information is unavailable, the applicant must apply in person. citeturn0search4

The current duplicate fee for a noncommercial driver's license or DAC is $17.50. ID-card duplicate fees vary by age and credential type. Nevada also has fee exemptions for certain homeless applicants and people recently released from incarceration who meet the DMV's conditions. citeturn1search0

Name and address changes

Nevada requires name changes to be completed in person with the current license or ID and original legal documentation authorizing the change. The Social Security Administration record must be updated before the DMV visit. The current fee is $8.50 for a noncommercial license and $7.50 for an ID card. citeturn1search3

Address changes

Nevada charges $3.50 for an address change only. A replacement credential showing the new address can involve additional requirements or fees. citeturn1search3turn1search0

Nevada IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Nevada offers credentials to eligible foreign nationals who establish identity, lawful status where required and Nevada residency. The DMV accepts qualifying permanent-resident, employment-authorization, visa and other immigration documents under its document rules. citeturn0search0

Limited-term credentials

Nevada marks licenses and ID cards as “Limited Term” when immigration documents are used to establish identity or authorized presence. These credentials expire according to the applicable immigration-document rules, including the holder's U.S. visa or authorized stay. citeturn1search7

Driver Authorization Card

The DAC is available under Nevada's separate driving-privilege system. It is not valid for identification and is not a substitute for a REAL ID or standard identification card. citeturn1search6

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

A Nevada driver's license or ID card is a government-issued credential tied to identity and residency information verified by the DMV. A counterfeit, altered or borrowed credential does not become valid identification for the person presenting it.

For general educational context about what distinguishes a counterfeit ID, altered genuine ID and another person's genuine credential, see What Is a Fake ID? Definition, Types, and Legal Facts. The article provides general terminology and legal context rather than Nevada-specific legal advice.

Nevada ID law FAQs

Does Nevada require REAL ID?

No. Nevada offers standard and REAL ID credentials. REAL ID is needed for covered federal purposes such as domestic air travel, but it is not required for ordinary driving or voting.

How many residency documents does Nevada require?

Two proofs of Nevada residential address are generally required for driver's licenses and ID cards.

Can I get a Nevada ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Nevada issues ID cards to residents age 10 and older. You cannot hold both a Nevada ID card and a driver's license at the same time.

Does Nevada require photo ID to vote?

Not generally for an active, verified voter at the polls. Nevada's voter-registration verification system is used to authenticate voter information. Certain pending registrations require photo ID and proof of residency.

What is a Nevada Driver Authorization Card?

A DAC is a driving credential that is marked “NOT VALID FOR ID.” It provides driving privileges but is not a valid identification document for federal purposes.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a Nevada driver's license?

Eligible non-U.S. citizens can receive Nevada credentials after meeting the DMV's identity, residency and applicable immigration-document requirements. Some credentials are limited term.

How much does a Nevada driver's license cost?

The current original 8-year noncommercial license fee is $41.50. Other license types, ages and transactions have different fees.

How much does a Nevada ID cost?

The current ID fee is $9.50 for applicants under 18, $21.50 for ages 18 through 64 and $11.50 for age 65 or older.
