Kansas ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Kansas issues driver's licenses and nondriver identification cards through the Division of Vehicles. This guide explains Kansas ID requirements, REAL ID documents, residency, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and rules for non-U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Kansas driver's license requirements

People applying for a Kansas driver's license must establish identity and residency, meet the applicable testing requirements and pay the required fees. First-time applicants must pass a vision examination, applicable written examinations and a driving examination. citeturn0search2

Applicants who already hold a valid out-of-state license generally do not have to take the written and driving examinations, although they must provide acceptable proof of identity and Kansas residency and pass the vision examination. An expired out-of-state license can trigger additional testing. citeturn0search2

Applicants under 16

Applicants younger than 16 have additional parent or guardian requirements. Kansas also has graduated licensing rules for younger drivers, including instructional permits and restrictions before a full driver's license.

New Kansas residents

People moving to Kansas must transfer an out-of-state credential and provide the required Kansas documents. Kansas applies a penalty to certain applicants who fail to transfer an out-of-state license within the required period after becoming a resident. The current fee schedule identifies a $1 penalty for applications made more than 60 days after becoming a Kansas resident. citeturn0search14

Kansas State ID without a driver's license

Kansas calls its non-driver credential a nondriver identification card. A Kansas resident who does not hold a current valid Kansas driver's license can apply for one. State law also requires the surrender of a Kansas driver's license before an ID can be issued. citeturn2search0

Kansas also will not issue an ID when the applicant holds an out-of-state driver's license or ID unless there is confirmation that the other credential is being terminated or has been terminated. citeturn2search0

ID for voters

Kansas provides a fee waiver for eligible registered voters who need a nondriver ID to satisfy the state's photo-identification requirement. The applicant must complete the required fee-waiver certification and provide evidence of voter registration. citeturn2search1

Documents needed for a Kansas ID

Kansas's Division of Vehicles uses separate document lists for U.S. citizens and non-U.S. citizens. For an original Kansas driver's license or nondriver ID, the standard checklist requires one document from List A or B, one Social Security document from List D and two Kansas residency documents from List C. citeturn0search0

Identity and lawful presence

U.S. citizens can use documents such as a state-issued birth certificate, unexpired U.S. passport, Certificate of Naturalization, Certificate of Citizenship or Consular Report of Birth Abroad.

Non-U.S. citizens can use qualifying documents such as a valid Permanent Resident Card, Employment Authorization Card, qualifying I-94 or an unexpired foreign passport with valid U.S. entry documentation. Non-U.S. citizen applications are subject to SAVE verification and may require additional review. citeturn0search0

Social Security number

Applicants generally provide one document containing the current legal name and Social Security number. Kansas lists a Social Security card, current W-2 or 1099 and current pay stub as examples. A Social Security number is not required for certain non-work-authorized non-U.S. citizens. citeturn0search0

Name changes

If the applicant's current legal name differs from the name on the lawful-presence document, Kansas requires a legal name-change document such as a government-issued marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order. citeturn0search0

Proof of Kansas residency

Kansas requires two proofs of current Kansas residential address. Each document must show the applicant's name and physical Kansas residential address. A separate mailing address or P.O. Box alone is not accepted as proof of residential address. citeturn0search5

Examples include a Kansas driver's license or ID renewal postcard, current Kansas vehicle registration or title, utility bill, bank statement, deed or mortgage, lease agreement, Kansas voter registration card, insurance bill, educational record, W-2 or 1099, government mail, check stub or USPS change-of-address confirmation. citeturn0search0turn0search5

Minors

If a minor does not have an acceptable proof of address, Kansas allows a parent, stepparent or legal guardian to complete a Certification of Address while providing the required proofs of address and identification.

Kansas REAL ID requirements

Kansas REAL ID is an indicator added to a Kansas driver's license or ID after the required federal documentation has been verified. Kansas says the REAL ID indicator is optional and does not cost extra beyond the normal credential and photo fees. citeturn0search1

For a Kansas REAL ID: bring original or certified documents proving your full legal name, Social Security number and lawful presence, plus two recent proofs of your current Kansas residential address. If names differ, bring the documents connecting the names. citeturn0search1turn0search5

Beginning May 7, 2025, a REAL ID-compliant credential or another federally accepted document, such as a valid passport, is required for covered federal purposes including boarding commercial aircraft and entering certain military facilities. citeturn0search1

One-time document verification

Kansas says the REAL ID document check is a one-time revalidation. Once the required documents have been verified, they generally do not have to be presented again for every renewal unless the applicant's circumstances require additional documentation. citeturn0search1

Kansas voter identification requirements

Kansas law requires voters to show photographic identification when casting a ballot in person. The Kansas Secretary of State lists Kansas or out-of-state driver's licenses and ID cards, U.S. passports, U.S. military IDs, tribal IDs, government employee IDs, Kansas postsecondary student IDs, concealed-carry licenses and government-issued public-assistance IDs among the accepted forms. citeturn2search1turn2search3

Expired IDs

An acceptable photo ID with an expiration date generally must not have expired at the time of voting. Voters age 65 or older may use an expired photo ID. citeturn2search1

Exemptions

Kansas provides exemptions for certain permanent advance voters, military and overseas voters and voters whose religious beliefs prohibit photographic identification. A voter with a qualifying religious objection can use the state's declaration process. citeturn2search1

Free nondriver ID

A registered Kansas voter without an approved government-issued photo ID can apply for a free nondriver identification card through the Division of Vehicles. The applicant must complete the required fee-waiver form and provide evidence of voter registration. citeturn2search1turn2search3

Renewing a Kansas ID or driver's license

Kansas driver's license renewals require proof of identity, Social Security number, Kansas residency and principal residence address. Applicants must also pass a vision examination, have a new photograph and signature captured and pay the applicable fee. citeturn0search7

Kansas generally allows a driver's license to be renewed up to one year before expiration, subject to special rules for applicants under 21. Renewals are handled in person at a full-service driver's license office or an eligible county treasurer location. citeturn0search7

Kansas identification cards are renewed for six-year terms under current state law. The renewal fee is $14, or $10 for people age 65 or older or people with a qualifying disability, plus the applicable photo fee. citeturn2search5

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Kansas ID

Kansas requires additional identification for a replacement credential. The Division of Vehicles lists documents such as a military ID, medical or immunization record, expired Kansas or out-of-state photo ID, student or employee ID, W-2 or check stub, vehicle title or registration, voter registration card and professional license among possible replacement documents. citeturn0search9

Non-U.S. citizens must also present their lawful-presence document for a replacement transaction. The exact documents accepted depend on the applicant's status and the transaction. citeturn0search9

Replacement fees

The current Kansas fee chart lists a replacement ID at $12 total, including the photo fee, and a replacement driver's license or permit at $16 total. citeturn2search37

Name and address changes

For a Kansas legal-name change, the applicant must provide the Kansas credential, proof of the legal name change and proof of current residential address. Kansas also requires the name to be updated with the Social Security Administration at least 24 hours before changing the Kansas credential. citeturn0search4

The current Kansas fee is $16 for a name change on a driver's license and $12 for a name change on an ID, including the applicable photo fee. citeturn0search4

For an address change, Kansas requires the current residential address to be documented when the credential is updated. The Division of Vehicles lists an $8 photo fee for an address change transaction. citeturn0search14

Kansas IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Kansas issues credentials to eligible non-U.S. citizens who establish identity, lawful presence, Social Security information when required and Kansas residency. Non-U.S. citizen applications are processed through the federal SAVE system before the credential is issued. Additional review can sometimes require another visit. citeturn0search0

Temporary credentials

Kansas law permits temporary identification cards for certain people whose lawful presence is temporary. A temporary ID is limited to the period of authorized stay or, when there is no definite end date, to a period of one year, subject to the statutory limits. citeturn2search7

Kansas requires immigration documents on every issuance for non-U.S. citizens. Foreign students and certain exchange visitors may need additional documents such as an I-20 or DS-2019. citeturn0search5

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

Kansas law regulates the issuance and use of nondriver identification cards and includes provisions addressing unlawful acts involving identification cards. Kansas also requires physical security features on ID cards designed to prevent tampering, counterfeiting and fraudulent duplication. citeturn2search0

Kansas ID law FAQs

Can I get a Kansas ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Kansas issues nondriver identification cards to residents who do not hold a current valid Kansas driver's license.

How many residency documents do I need for a Kansas ID?

Two recent documents showing your name and physical Kansas residential address are generally required.

Do I need a REAL ID to drive in Kansas?

No. The REAL ID indicator is optional for ordinary state driving and identification. It matters for federal purposes covered by the REAL ID rules.

Does Kansas require photo ID to vote?

Yes. Kansas requires photographic identification when voting in person, subject to statutory exemptions and voting situations.

Can I get a free Kansas ID for voting?

Yes. A registered Kansas voter who does not possess an approved photo ID can apply for a free nondriver identification card using the state's fee-waiver process.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a Kansas ID?

Yes, eligible non-U.S. citizens can receive Kansas credentials after lawful presence and the other required information are verified. SAVE verification applies.

How long is a Kansas ID valid?

Current Kansas law provides for a six-year renewal term for identification cards, subject to special rules for temporary credentials and other statutory exceptions.
