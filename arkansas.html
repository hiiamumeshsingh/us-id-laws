Arkansas ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Arkansas driver's licenses, state ID cards, REAL ID, required documents, residency, voter ID, renewals, replacements and other identification rules.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026. Arkansas requirements can change. Verify current details with the Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration or the Arkansas Secretary of State before applying or voting.
At a glance: Arkansas uses the Department of Finance and Administration's Office of Driver Services for driver's licenses and state identification cards. REAL ID is available for both credentials. Election identification rules are administered by the Secretary of State and use a separate voter-verification process.

Arkansas driver's license requirements

Arkansas issues non-commercial driver's licenses through the Office of Driver Services. A new applicant generally needs proof of legal presence, proof of identity and a Social Security document. Applicants should also be prepared for a vision exam, photograph and any testing required for the license being requested.

Arkansas uses a graduated licensing system for younger drivers. The learner's license is available at ages 14–15, followed by an intermediate license at ages 16–17. At 18, a teen can become eligible for a regular unrestricted license if the applicable driving-record requirements are met.

For adults

For a first Arkansas license, bring the required identity and legal-presence documents and your Social Security documentation. If you are transferring an out-of-state credential, bring the current credential and any additional documents DFA requires for your circumstances.

Getting an Arkansas state ID without a driver's license

Arkansas issues identification cards for people who need state-issued photo identification but do not need driving privileges. The application uses the same basic identity framework: proof of legal presence, proof of identity and Social Security documentation. Applicants should also expect a photograph and any applicable in-office verification.

A state ID is not the same thing as a driver's license. It establishes identity but does not authorize the holder to operate a motor vehicle.

What documents do you need in Arkansas?

Arkansas separates document requirements into legal presence, identity, Social Security and, for REAL ID, Arkansas residency. Original or certified documents are generally required for legal-presence evidence.

Proof of legal presence

U.S. citizens can use documents such as a U.S. passport, U.S. passport card, U.S. birth certificate, Certificate of Naturalization or Certificate of Citizenship.

Proof of identity

Acceptable identity documents can include a current driver's license or ID, school identification, military identification, certain government records, court documents and other documents listed by DFA.

Social Security

Common options include a Social Security card, W-2, 1099, pay stub showing the full Social Security number or qualifying military documentation.

If your name has changed, Arkansas requires documents connecting the old and new information. A marriage certificate, divorce decree, court order or other qualifying record may be used depending on the change.

Proof of Arkansas residency

Two residency documents are required for a REAL ID. DFA's current list generally calls for documents dated within six months unless a different period is specified.

Examples include a utility bill, lease or mortgage statement, bank statement, telephone, cable or internet bill, insurance policy, recent tax return, voter registration record, personal-property tax record, pay statement, school record or qualifying medical bill.

Children may use qualifying residency documents belonging to a parent when accompanied by the required relationship documentation. A spouse may use a spouse's residency document with the appropriate marriage documentation.

Arkansas REAL ID

Arkansas offers a federally compliant REAL ID driver's license or identification card. To obtain one, applicants must provide proof of legal presence, proof of identity, a Social Security document and two proofs of Arkansas residency.

Arkansas says REAL ID credentials are available at State Revenue Offices. Since May 7, 2025, a REAL ID or another federally accepted credential is required for domestic air travel and certain federal facilities where federal identification standards apply.

Once you already have a REAL ID, DFA says you generally do not need to bring documentation to renew it unless you are changing your name or address or otherwise have a transaction that requires new documents.

Arkansas voter ID requirements

Arkansas requires voters to verify their registration when voting. The accepted identification generally must show the voter's name and photograph, be issued by the United States, Arkansas or an accredited postsecondary institution in Arkansas and, if it has an expiration date, be unexpired or expired no more than four years before the election.

Accepted examples include an Arkansas driver's license, photo ID card, U.S. passport, military identification, qualifying college identification, certain public-assistance cards and an Arkansas voter verification card.

Voters who do not have another qualifying ID can request a free voter verification card from their county clerk. A voter who cannot verify registration at the polling place may cast a provisional ballot and has until noon on the Monday following the election to provide qualifying identification under the state's rules.

Voter registration has its own requirements. Arkansas requires a voter to be a U.S. citizen, an Arkansas resident, at least 18 by the relevant election and otherwise eligible under state law.

Renewing an Arkansas driver's license or state ID

DFA says a routine renewal generally does not require applicants to bring documents unless they are changing their name or want a REAL ID. Applicants should be prepared for a vision exam and a new photograph.

A Class D license for drivers age 21 and older is currently issued for eight years and may be renewed beginning 90 days before expiration. Different duration rules apply to younger drivers and certain optional four-year credentials.

Lost, stolen or damaged Arkansas ID

Arkansas offers online replacement services for lost licenses and IDs. DFA says an online replacement can be mailed to the applicant, generally within 3–5 business days. If the card was stolen or you suspect identity misuse, consider reporting the loss and watching for unauthorized use of your personal information.

Name and address changes

Name changes require documentation linking the previous information to the new legal name. Address updates can be handled through Arkansas's online services in eligible cases. DFA says an online address update does not automatically issue a new card.

Arkansas IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Non-U.S. citizens must provide qualifying proof of legal presence. DFA's document list includes examples such as a foreign passport with a valid U.S. visa and I-94, a valid Permanent Resident Card or a valid Employment Authorization Document. The exact documents depend on immigration status and the credential being requested.

Applicants should use DFA's current document list rather than relying on a general list because immigration documents have different validity periods and verification requirements.

Arkansas ID and driver's license fees

Fees depend on the credential, age, transaction and license type. DFA currently lists a Class D license for drivers age 21 and older at $40 with an eight-year duration. Younger drivers and some optional credentials have different fees and periods. REAL ID is available for the same fee as the underlying Arkansas credential.

Check the current DFA fee information before visiting because fees and transaction rules can change.

Fraudulent, altered or borrowed identification

An Arkansas-issued ID is evidence of the identity recorded by the state. Using a counterfeit card, altering a legitimate credential or presenting another person's credential can create legal problems because the document is being used to make a false identity or eligibility claim.

The distinction matters when reading information about identification fraud. A card can be genuine but still be misused if it belongs to someone else. For a broader explanation of how counterfeit, altered and borrowed credentials differ, see this educational guide on identifying fraudulent IDs.

Where to get an Arkansas ID

Most driver's license and state ID transactions are handled through Arkansas State Revenue Offices under the Office of Driver Services. DFA provides appointment scheduling, online pre-registration, replacement services and other driver services through its online portal.

For voter verification cards and election questions, contact the county clerk or the Arkansas Secretary of State's Elections Division rather than a driver's license office.

Arkansas ID law FAQs

Can I get an Arkansas ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Arkansas issues state identification cards separately from driver's licenses. You still need the required identity, legal-presence and Social Security documentation.

Do I need two residency documents for a regular Arkansas ID?

DFA specifically requires two Arkansas residency documents for a REAL ID. Check the current document list for the requirements that apply to a standard credential.

Can I use a passport instead of an Arkansas ID?

A U.S. passport is a federally issued identity document and is accepted for many purposes, including voter verification under Arkansas rules. Whether it can replace an Arkansas credential depends on the transaction.

What happens if I lose my Arkansas ID?

You can use DFA's replacement service when eligible. If you suspect the card has been used by someone else, take steps to protect your identity as well.

Is an Arkansas REAL ID required to drive?

No. REAL ID is a federal identification standard. You can have an Arkansas driver's license that is not REAL ID compliant and still use it as a state driving credential, subject to the normal licensing rules.
