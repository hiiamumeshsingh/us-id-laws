Arizona ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Driver's licenses, Arizona ID cards, Travel ID, required documents, residency, voter ID, renewals, replacements and other Arizona identification rules.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026. Arizona identification requirements can change. Use this guide to understand the process, then verify current details with ADOT MVD or the Arizona Secretary of State before applying or voting.

Arizona driver's license requirements

Arizona driver's licenses are issued through the Arizona Department of Transportation Motor Vehicle Division (MVD). A first-time applicant generally needs to establish identity, age and authorized presence, provide a Social Security number and complete the applicable testing process. Arizona also has a graduated licensing system for younger drivers.

If you have never held a U.S. driver's license, Arizona says you must pass its permit test before scheduling a road test. The current knowledge test has 30 multiple-choice questions and requires a score of at least 80 percent.

  • Age 15 years 6 months: You can begin the graduated instruction-permit process.
  • Under 18: Arizona's graduated system includes a six-month permit period and supervised driving requirements before a Class G license.
  • Age 18 and older: Adult applicants follow the applicable Class D licensing process.
  • Out-of-state license holders: A current out-of-state license can affect whether written or road testing is required.

Arizona's teen process includes at least 30 hours of supervised driving, including 10 hours at night, unless an approved driver education route changes the practice requirement. A road test is generally required unless an approved driver education program qualifies the applicant for a waiver.

Getting an Arizona state ID without a driver's license

Arizona offers a non-driver identification card for people who need official photo identification but do not need a driver's license. The card is available to people of all ages, including children. ADOT says an Arizona ID card can be used for many identification purposes, but a standard non-Travel ID is not the credential to rely on for federal purposes such as domestic air travel.

For an Arizona ID card, the state currently asks for one proof of identity, your complete Social Security number and two proofs of Arizona residency from different sources. If your legal name differs from the identity document, you also need evidence of the name change.

What documents do you need in Arizona?

The document list depends on the credential and your circumstances. Arizona's MVD uses documents to establish identity, age, authorized presence and Arizona residency rather than relying on a single document for every applicant.

Identity and authorized presence

Common identity documents include an original or certified birth certificate, an unexpired U.S. passport or passport card and other documents listed by MVD. Arizona also requires proof that an applicant's presence in the United States is authorized under federal law.

Social Security number

Arizona requires applicants to provide their Social Security number for driver license and identification purposes. MVD uses it to verify identity and for required state and federal compliance purposes.

Proof of Arizona residency

For a Travel ID or Arizona ID card, MVD generally requires two documents from different sources showing your name and current Arizona residential address. Examples include a utility bill, bank or credit-card statement, insurance policy or government document. If you cannot provide two standard residency documents for a Travel ID, Arizona allows an Arizona Residency Affidavit to serve as one proof, together with another residency document.

Arizona Travel ID: the state's REAL ID

Arizona's federally compliant credential is called the Arizona Travel ID. It is available as either a driver license or identification card and is marked with a star. The Travel ID is the Arizona credential designed to meet federal REAL ID requirements.

Since May 7, 2025, a REAL ID-compliant credential or another federally accepted form of identification has been required for TSA screening for domestic air travel. A passport and certain other federal credentials can also satisfy the requirement, so not everyone needs an Arizona Travel ID specifically.

The Travel ID costs $25. Arizona says it is generally valid for up to eight years, although different periods can apply based on age, immigration-document validity and certain other circumstances.

What documents are needed for an Arizona Travel ID?

The core document combination is straightforward: one acceptable proof of identity, your Social Security number and two Arizona residency documents from different sources. Legal name changes must also be documented when the name on your current documents does not match.

Arizona lists several acceptable identity documents beyond a birth certificate. For example, an unexpired U.S. passport or passport card, Permanent Resident Card and Certificate of Citizenship may qualify under the state's document rules. Always check the current MVD list before your appointment because acceptable documents can vary by circumstance.

Arizona voter ID requirements

Arizona uses a document-based identification system for in-person voting. At the polls, a voter can generally present one acceptable photo ID showing name and address, or two different acceptable documents from the state's non-photo identification lists.

Examples of acceptable photo identification include a valid Arizona driver's license, a valid Arizona non-operating identification card, qualifying tribal identification and certain government-issued IDs. The Secretary of State also lists documents such as a recent utility bill, bank statement, valid Arizona vehicle registration and other records as possible non-photo identification when used in the required combination.

Arizona also has separate proof-of-citizenship rules for voter registration and ballot eligibility. The Secretary of State explains that voters who provide the required proof of citizenship can vote a full ballot, while certain registrants whose citizenship is not verified may be registered as federal-only voters until the required documentation is provided.

If a voter does not provide acceptable identification at the polls, Arizona provides for a conditional provisional ballot. The voter must then provide the required proof of identity to the County Recorder within the applicable statutory deadline for the ballot to be counted.

Renewing an Arizona ID or driver's license

Arizona allows many driver's license holders to renew online through AZ MVD Now. MVD says a driver's license can generally be renewed within six months of its expiration date, although some renewals require an office visit for a new photograph, vision screening or document review.

A renewal can also be a convenient time to switch from a standard credential to an Arizona Travel ID. A Travel ID application requires an office visit for the required documents and photograph.

Arizona recently changed the interval for updating credential photographs. Beginning September 12, 2026, state law changed the photo-update timeframe from 12 years to 16 years. The credential's normal expiration and the separate photo-update requirement should therefore be treated as two different dates.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Arizona ID

If your Arizona driver's license or ID card is lost, stolen or damaged, MVD allows replacement online in many cases. The current replacement fee is $12. If you want to update your photograph as part of the replacement, you need to visit an MVD or authorized third-party driver license office.

If you believe someone is using your lost or stolen credential, ADOT advises reporting the incident to your local police department as possible identity theft.

Changing your name or address

Arizona requires you to report an address change to MVD within 10 days. The address can be updated online, and the change is applied to your driver-license record and associated vehicle records. If you want the new address printed on your credential, you can order a replacement card for the applicable fee.

A legal name change is handled differently. Arizona requires the name change to be updated with the Social Security Administration first. After that update, MVD says you should wait two business days before visiting an MVD office with original or certified documentation showing the legal change. The name-change process requires an office visit.

Non-U.S. citizens and Arizona identification

Non-U.S. citizens can qualify for Arizona credentials when they provide acceptable evidence of authorized presence and meet the other MVD requirements. The credential's validity can be tied to the period of authorized presence shown in the applicant's immigration documents.

For example, Arizona's guidance for foreign applicants identifies documents such as a Permanent Resident Card and notes special documentation requirements for certain students, including Form I-20 or DS-2019 for F-1 or J-1 applicants. Arizona also lists testing exemptions for valid licenses from Canada, Germany, South Korea and Taiwan under specified conditions.

Arizona ID and driver's license fees

Arizona's current MVD fee schedule varies by credential and age. A standard non-Travel ID is $12 for people ages 0–64 and free for people 65 and older, with additional eligibility-based fee waivers for some SSI recipients. A Travel driver license or Travel ID costs $25 for an original or renewal credential. A duplicate license or ID costs $12.

Fraudulent, altered or borrowed identification

An Arizona state credential is intended to establish the identity of the person to whom it was issued. Using a counterfeit, altered or another person's credential to misrepresent identity or age is a separate issue from simply obtaining a legitimate Arizona ID.

This distinction matters because a document can look like a government credential without actually having been issued by the government agency named on it. For a broader educational discussion of how counterfeit identification is defined and why using personal information in these transactions creates additional risks, see what states people make searches before deciding not to buy a fake ID. That article is educational and should not be treated as a substitute for Arizona law or official MVD guidance.

Where to apply or get help

Arizona MVD services are available through MVD offices and authorized third-party driver license locations. Many routine services can also be started or completed through AZ MVD Now. For a first credential, Travel ID or any transaction that requires document review, check the current MVD instructions before choosing an office.

Arizona Driver License and Identification Information is the best general starting point, while Arizona's ID Card page covers non-driver credentials.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get an Arizona ID if I do not drive?

Yes. Arizona issues non-operating identification cards to people who need state-issued photo identification but do not need a driver's license. The card is available to children and adults.

Do I need an Arizona Travel ID?

Not for every purpose. A Travel ID is Arizona's REAL ID-compliant credential and is useful for domestic air travel and certain federal facilities. A U.S. passport and other federally accepted credentials can also satisfy federal identification requirements.

How much does an Arizona ID cost?

A standard non-Travel ID is currently $12 for people under 65. Arizona lists no fee for standard IDs issued to people 65 and older, subject to the state's eligibility rules.

How many residency documents do I need?

For an Arizona ID card or Travel ID, MVD generally requires two documents from different sources showing your name and current Arizona residential address. A residency affidavit can be used as one proof in the circumstances described by MVD.

Can non-U.S. citizens get an Arizona driver's license or ID?

Yes, if they meet Arizona's requirements and provide acceptable evidence of authorized presence. The validity period can depend on the immigration documents presented.

What happens if I lose my Arizona ID?

You can generally order a replacement online through AZ MVD Now for $12. If you believe someone is using the lost or stolen credential, ADOT recommends reporting it to local law enforcement as possible identity theft.

What ID can I use to vote in Arizona?

Arizona accepts specified photo and non-photo identification combinations at the polls. A valid Arizona driver's license or non-operating ID is among the listed photo IDs. The Arizona Secretary of State publishes the complete current list and rules for provisional ballots.
