Editorial Policy

Learn how US ID Laws researches, writes, reviews and updates information about U.S. identification requirements.

Last reviewed: September 2026

Our approach: US ID Laws is an independent educational reference. We explain identification requirements in plain English, use primary government sources where possible and review important pages when rules or procedures change.

What US ID Laws is for

Identification rules can be difficult to understand because requirements vary by state, credential type and the reason you need an ID. Our goal is to make those rules easier to understand without presenting a general explanation as a substitute for an official government requirement.

We cover topics such as state ID cards, driver's licenses, REAL ID, proof of identity, proof of residency, Social Security requirements, replacement and renewal procedures and other identification-related questions.

How we research our articles

We use a source-first approach. When an article discusses a specific requirement, we look for the agency or authority responsible for that requirement.

Depending on the subject, our sources may include:

  • State departments of motor vehicles and driver's licensing agencies
  • State election offices and official election resources
  • Federal agencies and government websites
  • Official statutes, regulations and government publications
  • Other authoritative public sources when a primary source does not fully explain the issue

We prefer primary sources for current rules. Third-party sources may be used for background, context or clarification, but they do not replace the relevant government source when an official source is available.

State requirements come first

There is no single set of identification rules that applies to every person in the United States. A requirement can differ from one state to another and can also depend on the type of credential, the applicant's circumstances and the transaction involved.

For that reason, our articles explain general rules and point readers toward the appropriate state or federal authority when an exact requirement matters.

Important: If you are applying for an ID, renewing a credential, replacing a lost card or dealing with an official document requirement, verify the current instructions with the agency that will process your application.

How we handle changing information

Identification requirements are not static. Fees, accepted documents, renewal options, REAL ID procedures and other rules can change.

We include a Last reviewed date on our articles so readers can see when the page was most recently checked. When we become aware of a material change, we review the affected content and update it when appropriate.

A review date does not mean that every state rule has changed or that every detail on a page has been independently rechecked on that exact date. It indicates when the page was reviewed as part of our publishing and maintenance process.

Accuracy and corrections

We aim to publish accurate, useful and clearly sourced information. When we identify an error or receive credible information showing that a page needs correction, we review the issue and update the content when warranted.

Material corrections may be reflected in the page content and review date. We may also update wording when a government agency changes terminology or when an existing explanation could cause unnecessary confusion.

What we do not do

US ID Laws does not issue identification documents, approve applications or determine whether a person qualifies for a particular credential.

Our content is not legal advice and should not be treated as a guarantee that a particular document will be accepted by a government agency. The agency handling your application has the final say on its requirements and procedures.

Editorial independence

Our editorial content is written and maintained separately from the government agencies whose rules we discuss. We do not present US ID Laws as an official government website or as a representative of any state or federal agency.

Where an article discusses a law, requirement or government procedure, we aim to distinguish the underlying rule from commentary, explanation or interpretation.

Sponsored and commercial content

If we publish sponsored, commercial or affiliate content, we aim to keep it distinguishable from independent informational articles. Commercial relationships do not change the identification requirements described in our editorial content.

Links to external websites may be included when they help readers find a source, understand a topic or explore related information. A link does not mean that US ID Laws endorses the linked organization or guarantees the accuracy of its content.

Use of AI and editorial tools

Digital tools, including AI-assisted tools, may be used during research, drafting, editing or content maintenance. They are treated as editorial aids rather than authoritative sources.

Important factual claims are expected to be checked against appropriate sources before publication or material revision. AI-generated information is not treated as a substitute for a government source, statute or official agency guidance.

Reader feedback

If you notice an outdated requirement, incorrect statement, broken source link or other factual issue, you can contact us with the page URL and details of the issue. Specific source information is especially helpful when reporting a change.

For contact information, visit our Contact page. For the sources we commonly rely on, visit Sources.
