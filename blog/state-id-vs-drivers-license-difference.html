State ID vs. Driver’s License: What’s the Difference?

Understand the difference between REAL ID-compliant credentials and standard state-issued identification.

Last reviewed: September 2026

At first glance, a state ID and a driver's license look like almost the same thing. Both usually have your name, photo, date of birth and other identifying information. Both are issued by a state. Both can be used to prove who you are.

There is one important difference, though: a driver's license gives you driving privileges, while a state ID does not.

That sounds obvious, but the difference matters in everyday situations. It affects what you need to apply, whether you can legally drive, which credential makes sense if you do not drive and whether you need a REAL ID.

The short version: A driver's license is both an identification credential and a driving credential. A state ID is an identification credential only. You do not need a driver's license to have a state ID, although the exact application rules vary by state.

State ID vs. driver's license at a glance

State ID Driver's License
Proves identity Yes Yes
Shows your photo Usually Usually
Can be used as age identification Generally Generally
Allows you to drive No Yes, subject to the license and state rules
Requires a driving test No Usually, when obtaining driving privileges
Can be REAL ID-compliant Yes Yes
Useful if you do not drive Yes Not necessary just for identification

The California DMV puts the distinction very plainly: an identification card can be used to prove identity or age, but it does not allow the holder to operate a motor vehicle. A driver's license, by contrast, authorizes driving. citeturn0search2turn0search10

A state ID is not a "lesser" driver's license

This is one of those small misconceptions that causes unnecessary confusion.

A state ID is not a driver's license with the driving part removed. It is its own credential, issued for identification.

If you do not drive, there is no requirement that you obtain a driver's license simply because you need government-issued photo identification. A state ID can fill that role.

That is useful for people who do not own a car, older adults who no longer drive, people who cannot or do not want to drive and anyone who simply needs a state-issued identity credential.

California, for example, issues identification cards separately from driver's licenses and says its ID cards are available to people of any age. citeturn0search2

What can you use a state ID for?

The exact uses depend on the organization asking for identification, but a state-issued ID is commonly used when someone needs to establish their identity or age.

Examples can include:

  • Identifying yourself at a business or government office
  • Proving your age when an age check is required
  • Completing certain financial or administrative transactions
  • Showing identification for services that accept state-issued IDs
  • Using a REAL ID-compliant state ID for applicable federal identification purposes

It is important not to turn that list into a promise that every business or agency accepts every state's ID for every transaction. Organizations can have their own identification policies, and some situations require a particular document.

What can a driver's license do that a state ID cannot?

The big one is driving.

A driver's license authorizes you to operate a motor vehicle under the conditions attached to that license and your state's laws. A state ID does not.

That distinction also affects the application process. Someone applying for driving privileges may have to complete knowledge testing, a driving test or other licensing requirements. California, for example, requires applicants for a driver's license to complete the applicable testing process, while its identification-card application does not involve a driving test. citeturn0search10turn0search2

So if your only goal is to have government-issued photo identification, there is usually no reason to take on the requirements associated with getting driving privileges.

Do you need a driver's license to get a state ID?

No. In fact, that is one of the main reasons state ID cards exist.

You can apply for a state identification card without having a driver's license. The exact eligibility rules, documents, age requirements and fees are set by the state.

If you already have a driver's license from the same state, however, the state may have rules about holding both credentials at the same time. Do not assume that you can simply order a second card without checking the state's rules.

If you are starting from scratch, our guide on getting a state ID without a driver's license goes into the application side of the question.

What documents do both cards require?

This is where the two processes start to look similar.

Depending on the state and the type of credential, you may need documents establishing things such as:

  • Identity
  • Date of birth
  • Social Security number
  • State residency
  • Legal presence or immigration status, where required
  • Legal name changes, if your current name differs from your source documents

California, for example, requires applicants for an ID card to provide an acceptable identity document and, in applicable cases, residency documentation. Its REAL ID process adds specific identity, Social Security and residency documentation requirements. citeturn0search2turn0search12

The paperwork is therefore not a reliable way to tell the two credentials apart. The purpose of the card is.

What about REAL ID?

This is another area where people often mix up the terms.

REAL ID is not a separate kind of driving license. It is a federal security standard that can apply to a state driver's license or a state identification card.

USAGov explains that when you apply for or renew a driver's license or state ID, you can choose to make it REAL ID-compliant. The state determines the application process and documents. citeturn0search0

So you can have:

  • A standard driver's license
  • A REAL ID-compliant driver's license
  • A standard state ID
  • A REAL ID-compliant state ID

That is why "REAL ID vs. driver's license" is not quite the right comparison. One describes a federal compliance standard. The other describes the type of state credential and its driving function.

For a deeper comparison, see REAL ID vs. a standard driver's license.

Can a state ID be used for air travel?

A REAL ID-compliant state ID can be used for applicable federal identification purposes, including domestic air travel. A standard state ID may not satisfy those federal requirements.

USAGov says REAL ID-compliant driver's licenses and state IDs can be used for domestic flights and certain secure federal facilities. It also notes that travelers can use another federally accepted credential, such as a passport, instead. citeturn0search0

This is why the little REAL ID marking on the card matters more than many people realize. Two state-issued IDs can look similar but have different federal-use status.

Does a driver's license prove more than a state ID?

Not necessarily.

A driver's license proves that the issuing state has granted you driving privileges. A state ID proves your identity under the state's identification system.

Those are different functions, not a ranking of how "official" the cards are.

For an ordinary identity check, the organization accepting the credential may treat both as government-issued photo identification. For a driving-related situation, only the driver's license has the relevant authorization.

Can you have both?

That depends on the state's rules.

Some states may restrict people from holding multiple active credentials or may require you to surrender one credential when another is issued. Others have specific arrangements for different types of cards.

Do not assume that having a driver's license automatically means you can also get a separate state ID. Check your state's motor vehicle agency before applying.

USAGov maintains a state-by-state directory of motor vehicle agencies if you need to find the official office for your state. citeturn0search4

What if your driver's license is suspended?

A state ID and a driver's license are separate credentials, but a suspended driver's license does not automatically mean that you should use a state ID as a substitute for driving privileges.

An identification card identifies you. It does not authorize you to drive.

If your driving privilege is suspended, revoked or otherwise restricted, check your state's rules before getting behind the wheel. A state ID does not change that status.

What if your driver's license is lost?

A lost driver's license and a state ID are not interchangeable replacements.

If you lose your driver's license, contact the state's motor vehicle agency and follow its replacement process. USAGov specifically directs people to their state motor vehicle agency for replacement driver's licenses and state ID cards. citeturn0search7

If you already have a separate state ID, it may still help you identify yourself while you deal with the replacement, but it does not restore your driving credential.

State ID, driver's license and fake IDs are three different things

There is another distinction worth making because the terms are sometimes thrown around together online.

A genuine state ID and a genuine driver's license are government-issued credentials. Their authority comes from the state that issued them and the records behind them.

A document that merely imitates one of those credentials is a different matter. FakeIDs' explanation of what a fake ID is makes the basic distinction between a genuine government credential and a document falsely presented as one.

And if you are researching why a legitimate credential can sometimes be questioned by a business, this discussion of real driver's licenses being mistaken for fake IDs shows why appearance and familiarity are not the same thing as official verification.

For an educational site about identification laws, that distinction matters: a card looking like a government ID does not give it the legal status of a government-issued ID.

Which one should you get?

There is no universal answer because it depends on what you actually need the credential to do.

If your main need is... The relevant credential
Government-issued photo identification without driving State ID
Driving a personal vehicle Driver's license
Identification plus driving privileges Driver's license
State-issued identification for someone who does not drive State ID
Federal identification purposes covered by REAL ID REAL ID-compliant state ID or driver's license, or another federally accepted credential

The useful question is not "Which card is better?" It is "What do I need the card to do?"

Before applying, check these five things

  1. Purpose: Do you need identification, driving privileges or both?
  2. Credential type: Does your state offer a non-driver identification card?
  3. REAL ID: Will you need the credential for federal purposes such as domestic air travel?
  4. Documents: Do you have the required identity, residency and Social Security documents?
  5. Fee: What does your state currently charge for the specific transaction?

Checking these before the appointment is much easier than finding out at the counter that you brought the wrong document or applied for the wrong type of credential.

Frequently asked questions

Is a state ID the same as a driver's license?

No. Both are state-issued identification credentials, but a driver's license also grants driving privileges. A state ID does not.

Can I get a state ID if I do not drive?

Yes. State identification cards are specifically available for identification purposes without granting permission to drive. The eligibility and application rules vary by state.

Can a state ID be REAL ID-compliant?

Yes. REAL ID standards apply to qualifying state-issued driver's licenses and identification cards. citeturn0search0

Can I use a state ID instead of a driver's license?

For identification purposes, often yes when the organization accepts state-issued ID. You cannot use a state ID as permission to drive.

Does a state ID have the same information as a driver's license?

Many of the basic identifying details can be similar, such as your name, photo and date of birth. The exact card design and information vary by state.

Can I have both a state ID and driver's license?

State rules differ. Check your state's motor vehicle agency before applying for both.
