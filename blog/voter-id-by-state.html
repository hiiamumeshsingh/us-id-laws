Voter ID Requirements by State

How identification requirements for voting differ across jurisdictions.

Last reviewed: September 2026

Voter ID rules are not the same across the United States. Each state sets its own requirements for identifying voters, and the rules can differ depending on whether you vote in person, vote by mail or are voting for the first time after registering.

Quick answer: Some states require voters to present an acceptable photo ID. Others accept non-photo documents, use signature or voter-record verification, or provide alternative procedures when a voter cannot present the requested ID. Always check the current rule for your state before an election.

Does every state require voter ID?

No. There is no single voter ID rule that applies across all 50 states and the District of Columbia.

The U.S. Election Assistance Commission reported that, as of 2024, 36 states had laws requesting or requiring voters to show some form of identification when voting in person. The remaining 14 states and Washington, D.C. used other methods to verify a voter's identity. citeturn0search36

That national figure is useful background, but it should not be treated as a current state-by-state checklist. Voting rules can change, so the safest approach is to verify the current requirement through your state or local election office.

What types of voter ID rules exist?

States use several different approaches. The important distinction is not simply whether a state has an "ID law," but what identification is accepted and what happens if a voter does not have it.

What counts as voter ID?

Accepted identification can include a state driver's license, state ID card, passport, military ID, tribal ID or other documents depending on the state. Some jurisdictions also accept non-photo documents such as a utility bill, bank statement or government document showing a voter's name and address. citeturn0search36

The key point is that an ID being "government-issued" does not automatically mean it is accepted for every voting purpose. The election authority decides which documents qualify.

What if you do not have the required ID?

Do not assume that you cannot vote. The procedure depends on state law.

Some states provide an affidavit, reasonable-impediment process, provisional ballot or another way to address the situation. The EAC notes that states with stricter ID requirements commonly provide exceptions or alternative procedures in at least some circumstances. citeturn0search36

Before Election Day: If you are unsure whether your ID qualifies, contact your state or local election office rather than waiting until you arrive at the polling place.

What about voting by mail?

Voter identification rules can also differ between in-person and mail voting. A state may use a signature, identification number, copy of an ID or another verification method for absentee or mail ballots.

Federal law also has a specific identification rule for certain first-time voters who registered by mail. The Election Assistance Commission explains that qualifying first-time voters may need to provide a current photo ID or certain documents showing their name and address unless an applicable exception applies. citeturn0search12

Is a voter registration card an ID?

Not necessarily. Vote.gov specifically notes that you do not need a voter registration card to vote. Whether you need another form of identification depends on your state's rules and your voting situation. citeturn0search2

Can you vote without a driver's license?

Yes, depending on where you live and the documents your state accepts. A driver's license is only one possible form of identification. States may accept a state ID card, passport, military or tribal identification, non-photo documents or other alternatives depending on their rules. citeturn0search36

Why voter ID rules are easy to misunderstand

Several different rules often get grouped together under the phrase "voter ID":

  • ID needed when registering to vote
  • ID needed when voting in person
  • ID needed when voting by mail
  • ID requirements for first-time voters
  • What happens when the requested ID is unavailable

These are not necessarily the same requirement. That is why a state-by-state page should always identify when the ID is required, what documents are accepted and what alternative process exists.

What should you do before voting?

  1. Check your voter registration status.
  2. Confirm your polling place or voting method.
  3. Review your state's current voter ID requirements.
  4. Check whether your ID is on the accepted list.
  5. If your ID is missing, expired or does not match your registration information, contact your election office before Election Day.

What about fake or altered IDs?

A counterfeit, altered or borrowed government ID is not a substitute for an identification document accepted under election law. If you are unsure whether your identification qualifies, use the official election authority's accepted-document list instead of relying on an unofficial document.

For general educational background on why government-issued identification is different from documents that merely imitate its appearance, see FakeIDs.com's explanation of what a fake ID is and how it differs from a legitimate government ID. The distinction matters because a voting authority's accepted-ID list is based on the document's legal status and verification requirements, not simply how much the card resembles a state credential.

Frequently asked questions

Which states require voter ID?

Many states require some form of identification for in-person voting, but the type of ID and the exceptions vary. The EAC reported that 36 states had laws requesting or requiring identification for in-person voting as of 2024. Always check the current rule for your state. citeturn0search36

Do I need a photo ID to vote?

Not everywhere. Some states require photo ID, while others accept non-photo documents or use different voter-verification procedures.

Can I vote if I forgot my ID?

Possibly. Your state may provide an affidavit, provisional ballot or another procedure. The exact process depends on state law.

Can I use a passport as voter ID?

A passport is accepted in some states, but not every state uses the same list of qualifying documents. Check your state's official election guidance.

Can I use a state ID card instead of a driver's license?

In many states, a qualifying state-issued ID card can be used where a driver's license is accepted. Check the state's current list.

Do voter ID rules apply to mail-in ballots?

They can. Mail voting may use different identification or verification procedures, including signatures or identification information. Check your state's absentee or mail-ballot rules.

Where can I verify my state's voter ID requirements?

Use the official election website for your state or local election office. The EAC and NASS provide state-by-state links to election officials and voter resources. citeturn0search9turn0search0
