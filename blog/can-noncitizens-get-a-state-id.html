Can Non-U.S. Citizens Get a State ID?

Understand the difference between REAL ID-compliant credentials and standard state-issued identification.

Last reviewed: September 2026

If you were born outside the United States, getting a state ID can feel more complicated than it should.

There are extra terms to navigate: lawful presence, immigration documents, I-94, permanent resident card, REAL ID, Social Security number. And the rules are not identical from one state to another.

But the basic idea is easier to understand once you separate the pieces.

Short answer: Yes, non-U.S. citizens can get state-issued identification cards in many circumstances. The exact requirements depend on the state and the person's immigration or legal-presence status. States commonly require identity, proof of lawful presence where applicable, residency and Social Security information or an applicable exception. REAL ID cards have additional federal standards.

Being a non-U.S. citizen does not automatically prevent you from getting an ID

A state ID is issued by the state, not by the federal government.

So the fact that someone is not a U.S. citizen does not, by itself, answer whether they can receive a state identification card. The real questions are usually:

  • Does the state issue an ID to someone in your situation?
  • What documents prove your identity?
  • What documents prove your lawful presence or other qualifying status?
  • What does the state require regarding an SSN?
  • Do you want a standard ID or a REAL ID?

USAGov says states decide the process and documents needed for REAL ID and lists a Permanent Resident Card as one example of an identity document a non-U.S. citizen may use. citeturn0search0

The important word here is state. There is no single DMV application that works the same way across the country.

Who counts as a non-U.S. citizen for these purposes?

"Non-U.S. citizen" covers a much wider group than many people realize.

It can include:

  • Lawful permanent residents
  • People with temporary visas
  • People with employment authorization
  • Students in qualifying immigration status
  • Certain people with Temporary Protected Status
  • Certain people with deferred action or other recognized forms of lawful presence
  • Other applicants whose immigration documents establish a qualifying status under state rules

Those categories are not interchangeable. A state may issue a credential with different validity periods or documentation requirements depending on the person's status.

California, for example, says its REAL ID program includes permanent residents and people with temporary legal status such as certain DACA and TPS recipients and holders of valid student or employment visas. citeturn0search6

What documents might a noncitizen need?

Think of the application as a series of questions the DMV needs to answer.

What the state needs to establish Possible documentation
Identity Passport, Permanent Resident Card, Employment Authorization Document or another accepted identity document
Legal presence/status Permanent Resident Card, valid visa and I-94, EAD or another state-approved immigration document
Social Security information SSN or an applicable state exception
State residency Lease, utility bill, bank statement, insurance record or another accepted document
Name changes Marriage record, court order or other legal name-change document where required

These are examples, not a universal checklist. North Carolina's DMV, for example, lists Permanent Resident Cards, Employment Authorization Documents and qualifying foreign passports with visas and I-94 records among documents it can accept as proof of legal presence. citeturn0search1

Rhode Island similarly lists Permanent Resident Cards, EADs and qualifying foreign passports with visas and I-94 documentation among its identity-document options for non-REAL ID credentials. citeturn0search3

Permanent residents have a different document trail

For a lawful permanent resident, the Permanent Resident Card, commonly called a Green Card, is often one of the key documents in the application.

It can establish identity and immigration status for purposes of the state's document requirements, although the exact rules still vary by state and credential.

USAGov specifically lists a Permanent Resident Card as an example of an identity document for REAL ID applicants who are not U.S. citizens. citeturn0search0

Bring the document the state actually lists. Do not substitute a photocopy, expired document or unrelated immigration notice unless the state's instructions say it is acceptable.

What about someone on a temporary visa?

Temporary visa holders can also encounter a different set of requirements.

A state may look at the passport, visa, I-94 and other documents together to establish identity and lawful presence.

North Carolina's current DMV guidance provides a good illustration: an unexpired foreign passport with an affixed U.S. visa and I-94 Arrival/Departure Record can be accepted as proof of legal presence for a REAL ID. citeturn0search1

The exact combination depends on the state and the immigration category. A student, exchange visitor and employment-based applicant may not have identical documentation.

Can a student on an F-1 visa get a state ID?

Potentially, yes.

A student in valid immigration status may be able to obtain a state ID if the state accepts the relevant documents and the applicant meets its other requirements.

The paperwork can include a passport, visa, I-94 and school-related immigration documentation. North Carolina, for example, lists an I-20 together with an I-94 or qualifying passport documentation among documents that can establish legal presence, although its page distinguishes which documents qualify for REAL ID. citeturn0search1

That distinction is important. A document can be useful for establishing legal presence without necessarily satisfying every requirement for a federally compliant REAL ID.

Can someone with an Employment Authorization Document get a state ID?

In states that accept the document and where the applicant otherwise qualifies, an Employment Authorization Document can be part of the application.

North Carolina lists an unexpired Employment Authorization Document as acceptable proof of legal presence for REAL ID. Rhode Island also lists an unexpired EAD among acceptable identity documents for its non-REAL ID credentials. citeturn0search1turn0search3

Again, the key is the state's list. An EAD does not automatically guarantee that every state will issue the same type of credential under the same conditions.

What if you have an SSN?

If you have a Social Security number, the state may require you to provide it as part of the application.

This is particularly important for REAL ID applications. USAGov says most state licensing agencies require proof of an SSN for REAL ID, while federal standards also account for applicants who are not eligible for an SSN. citeturn0search0turn0search22

If your name has changed, make sure your Social Security record and your immigration documents are consistent with the name you are using for the ID application.

If you do not have an SSN, the rules can be different. Our guide to getting a state ID without a Social Security number covers that issue separately.

Can you get a REAL ID if you are not a U.S. citizen?

Yes, lawful presence can qualify a noncitizen for a REAL ID in states that issue REAL ID-compliant credentials to people in that status.

USAGov explicitly lists a Permanent Resident Card as an acceptable identity document example for non-U.S. citizens applying for REAL ID. citeturn0search0

California goes further in its own guidance, stating that permanent residents and people with certain temporary legal statuses can receive REAL ID driver's licenses or identification cards if they can prove current legal presence. citeturn0search6

The catch is that a REAL ID may have a validity period connected to the person's authorized stay. California says temporary-status REAL ID cards expire based on the person's verified legal-presence period. citeturn0search6

What happens when your immigration document expires?

This is one of the most important practical differences for temporary-status applicants.

Your state ID does not necessarily have the same validity period as a U.S. citizen's standard credential.

California, for example, says a REAL ID issued to someone with temporary legal status can expire on the same date as the person's U.S. legal-presence document. citeturn0search6

Delaware takes a similar verification-based approach. Its DMV says noncitizens must provide immigration documents and that the legal status and authorized length of stay are verified through federal databases and the SAVE system. citeturn0search4

This is why keeping immigration documents current matters when dealing with a state-issued ID.

What is SAVE and why does the DMV use it?

You may see the acronym SAVE when researching this topic.

SAVE stands for the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements program. It is a federal verification system used by participating agencies to confirm immigration status information.

For example, Delaware's DMV says it uses SAVE to electronically verify immigration documents for noncitizen applicants and determines the authorized length of stay from federal records. citeturn0search4

This is why a DMV employee may need to verify an immigration document electronically instead of simply looking at the card and accepting it at face value.

What if the DMV cannot verify your status?

It does not necessarily mean that your application is permanently rejected.

Sometimes the issue is that the state cannot immediately verify the information electronically and needs additional documentation or a secondary verification process.

California says that when a legal-presence document cannot be verified electronically, the DMV may send documentation to USCIS for secondary verification. citeturn0search5

The practical lesson is to bring the original documents requested by your state and allow extra time if your status requires federal verification.

What about undocumented immigrants?

This is where you need to be careful with broad statements.

There is no single nationwide rule saying every undocumented person can or cannot receive a state ID. States have different laws and different credential systems.

California, for example, says it does not issue its standard ID card to people who are not legally present, while it separately offers AB 60 driver's licenses to residents who cannot provide proof of legal presence but meet the other requirements for that license. citeturn0search6turn0search16

That example should not be treated as the rule for all 50 states. If legal presence is an issue, check your state's current law and DMV guidance specifically for identification cards.

Can a noncitizen use a foreign passport as ID?

A foreign passport can be an important identity document, but whether it is sufficient for a state ID application depends on the state's requirements.

Some states accept a foreign passport together with a valid U.S. visa and I-94. North Carolina is one example. citeturn0search1

Other states may require additional documentation or accept different combinations.

So there is a difference between “this is a valid identity document” and “this document alone satisfies my state's ID application.”

Do you need a state ID if you already have a passport?

Not necessarily.

A valid passport is already a government-issued identity document, and it may be accepted for many purposes. But a state ID can be useful for everyday identification, state services and situations where a local state-issued credential is more convenient.

A passport and state ID also serve different systems. One is issued by the federal government through the State Department. The other is issued by a state.

If you need a credential primarily for everyday identification, the convenience of a state ID may be the reason to obtain one even when you already have a passport.

Do not confuse an immigration document with a state ID

An immigration document can help establish your legal status. It does not automatically become a state identification card.

The same applies in reverse: a state ID does not change your immigration status.

Those are separate government systems with different purposes.

This distinction also matters when looking at documents sold online that claim to resemble government identification. FakeIDs' explanation of fake IDs makes the useful point that an identification document gets its authority from the issuing system behind it, not simply from how official the card looks.

For another educational example, its discussion of genuine driver's licenses being mistaken for fake IDs shows why appearance alone is not the same thing as official verification.

If your state requires proof of lawful presence, the answer is to provide the documents the state recognizes, not to find a document that merely resembles one.

What should you bring to the DMV?

If you are a non-U.S. citizen applying for a state ID, build your document folder around these categories:

  1. Identity: Bring the identity document listed by your state.
  2. Immigration or legal presence: Bring the current document proving the status the state requires.
  3. SSN: Bring your SSN documentation if required, or evidence supporting an applicable exception.
  4. Residency: Bring the state's required proof that you live in the state.
  5. Name changes: Bring legal documentation if your names differ across records.
  6. REAL ID: If you want a REAL ID, use the state's REAL ID checklist rather than its basic ID checklist.

For example, Georgia tells non-U.S. citizens to present valid immigration documents in person to prove identity and lawful status. citeturn0search12

Georgia also says noncitizens must visit a DDS office for transactions that result in the issuance of a driver's license, identification card or permit because staff need to physically review the lawful-status documentation. citeturn0search15

A practical checklist for noncitizen applicants

Before your appointment:
  • ☐ I checked my state's rules specifically for non-U.S. citizens.
  • ☐ I know whether I am applying for a standard ID or REAL ID.
  • ☐ My passport, immigration document or other identity document is current and acceptable.
  • ☐ I checked whether my state needs my I-94, visa, EAD, Green Card or another document.
  • ☐ I know what the state requires for Social Security information.
  • ☐ I have the required proof of residency.
  • ☐ My name is consistent across my documents, or I have the required name-change evidence.
  • ☐ I know whether I need to appear in person.
  • ☐ I have checked the state's rules for expiration and renewal if my status is temporary.

Frequently asked questions

Can a non-U.S. citizen get a state ID?

Yes, in many circumstances. The state determines who qualifies and which documents are required. Lawful permanent residents and certain people with temporary legal status can qualify for state-issued IDs in states that provide those credentials.

Can a Green Card holder get a state ID?

Generally, a lawful permanent resident can apply for a state ID if the state's eligibility requirements are met. A Permanent Resident Card is specifically listed as an acceptable identity document example for REAL ID applicants by USAGov. citeturn0search0

Can someone on a student visa get a state ID?

Potentially, yes. The state may require a passport, visa, I-94 and other documentation establishing the applicant's identity and lawful presence.

Can you get a REAL ID if you are not a U.S. citizen?

Yes, if you meet the state's requirements and can establish the required lawful presence. USAGov and state DMV guidance provide for noncitizen applicants in qualifying circumstances. citeturn0search0turn0search6

Will a state ID expire when my visa or immigration status expires?

It can. Some states tie the credential's validity to the period of verified legal presence. California explicitly does this for certain temporary-status REAL ID cards. citeturn0search6

Do I need an SSN to get a state ID as a noncitizen?

It depends on the state and your circumstances. Some applicants may have an SSN, while others may qualify for an exception if they are not eligible for one.

Can a foreign passport be used to apply for a state ID?

It can be part of the required documentation in some states. For example, North Carolina accepts certain foreign passports with a valid U.S. visa and I-94 for proof of legal presence. citeturn0search1
