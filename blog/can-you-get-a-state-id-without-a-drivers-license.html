Can You Get A State ID Without Driver's License?

Understand the difference between REAL ID-compliant credentials and standard state-issued identification.

Last reviewed: September 2026

You do not need to drive a car to have a government-issued photo ID.

That sounds obvious, but the distinction gets surprisingly blurry. A driver's license gives you permission to drive. A state identification card gives you a way to prove who you are and, depending on the card, your age and other information, without giving you driving privileges.

Short answer: Yes. In general, you can get a state ID without having a driver's license. The exact application process, documents, age rules and fees are set by your state. You may also be able to choose a REAL ID-compliant state ID instead of a standard one.

What is a state ID?

A state ID is a government-issued identification card for people who need official identification but do not need a driver's license.

California's DMV, for example, describes its ID cards as documents used to prove identity or age that do not authorize someone to operate a motor vehicle. California issues ID cards to people of any age. citeturn1search3

Other states use terms such as non-driver ID, identification card or state ID. New York, for example, calls its credential a non-driver photo ID card. citeturn1search6

The name changes from state to state. The basic idea does not: you can have an official state identification credential without holding a driver's license.

The easiest way to think about it is this: a driver's license combines identification with driving privileges. A state ID provides identification without the driving privilege.

Why would someone get a state ID instead of a driver's license?

Plenty of people have no reason to drive.

You might live somewhere with good public transportation. You might be too young to drive. You might have stopped driving. Or you may simply want a government-issued photo ID without going through the process of getting a driver's license.

New York explicitly describes its non-driver photo ID as providing secure proof of identification comparable to a photo driver's license. citeturn1search6

A state ID can be useful for everyday situations where an organization needs to establish your identity or age, even though you never intend to get behind the wheel.

Do you need a driver's license to apply for a state ID?

No. Having a driver's license is not the purpose of a state ID in the first place.

What can complicate things is already having a driver's license from the same state or another state. Some states will not let you maintain both credentials at the same time, or they may require you to surrender one when you obtain the other.

Georgia, for example, says an applicant with a U.S. driver's license, permit or ID card from another state must surrender the out-of-state credential when applying for a Georgia state ID. citeturn1search2

So the real question is not whether you need a driver's license to get an ID. You generally do not. The question is whether your state has rules about holding multiple state credentials.

What documents do you need to get a state ID?

This is where state requirements start to matter.

Most state ID applications ask you to establish some combination of:

  • Identity: who you are
  • Date of birth: when you were born
  • Social Security number: where required
  • Residency: where you live
  • Lawful status or citizenship: where required
  • Name change: if your current legal name differs from your identity documents

USAGov explains that the exact documents and process are controlled by the state. For REAL ID applications, states generally require proof of identity, Social Security number and state residency. Examples include a U.S. birth certificate or passport, Social Security documentation and a lease, utility bill or bank statement. citeturn1search0

If you want a deeper explanation of the identity-document side of the process, see our guide to what counts as proof of identity. For address requirements, our proof of residency guide breaks down the difference between proving who you are and proving where you live.

Can you get a REAL ID without a driver's license?

Yes.

This is one of the most useful things to know about REAL ID: REAL ID is not limited to driver's licenses. A state can issue a REAL ID-compliant identification card to someone who does not drive.

USAGov says you can choose to make either a driver's license or a state identification card REAL ID-compliant when you apply or renew, subject to your state's process. citeturn1search0

New York makes the distinction explicit: its REAL ID can be issued as a driver's license, learner permit or non-driver ID. citeturn1search10

That means a person who never drives can still have a REAL ID card for situations where a federally accepted form of identification is required.

Why would you want a REAL ID state ID?

The main reason is federal identification requirements.

Since May 7, 2025, TSA has required travelers age 18 and older to present a REAL ID-compliant state license or ID, or another acceptable form of identification, at airport security checkpoints for domestic flights. A passport is one example of an alternative. citeturn1search1

So if you do not drive but travel domestically, a REAL ID-compliant state ID can be useful. You do not need a driver's license just to have a federally compliant state identification card.

That said, you do not necessarily need a REAL ID card if you already carry another accepted federal ID. The TSA's current identification guidance lists other acceptable documents. citeturn1search1

What documents are needed for a REAL ID state ID?

The details vary by state, but the documentation generally falls into the same buckets: identity, Social Security number and residency.

California provides a useful example. Its DMV requires one identity document and two different residency documents for a REAL ID, along with the Social Security number on the application. It also has additional requirements when the applicant's current name does not match the identity document. citeturn1search4turn1search5

That does not mean every state uses California's exact checklist. It is an example of why checking the actual state's list matters.

What if your name is different on your documents?

This is one of the easiest ways to turn a simple ID application into an extra trip to the DMV.

Suppose your passport has your former surname but your current legal name is different. The state may ask for documentation connecting the two names, such as a marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order.

USAGov notes that name-change procedures vary by state and that certified documents such as marriage or divorce records and court orders can be used when notifying government agencies of a legal name change. citeturn1search8

Do not assume that a clerk can simply overlook a mismatch. Check your state's checklist before your appointment.

Can you get a state ID if you have never had an ID before?

Yes, although you will need to establish your identity using the documents your state accepts.

This is an important distinction: not having a driver's license is not the same thing as not having documents that prove your identity.

A first-time applicant may use documents such as a passport, certified birth certificate or other accepted identity evidence, depending on the state. The exact list can be surprisingly specific, so use the state's official checklist rather than assuming a document will qualify.

What if you lost your old ID?

A replacement application is usually different from a first-time application.

USAGov says rules for replacing government-issued ID cards vary and directs people to their state's motor vehicle agency for replacement driver's licenses or state ID cards. citeturn1search7

If you have also lost the underlying documents you need to establish identity, the process can take longer because you may need to replace a birth certificate, Social Security card or other supporting record first.

Can you get a state ID if you are under 18?

In many states, yes. The minimum age and application process vary.

California, for example, says it may issue an ID card to a person of any age. Georgia requires applicants under 18 to bring a responsible adult who can sign the application and show documentation of the relationship. citeturn1search3turn1search2

So if you are applying for a minor, check the state's rules for both the applicant and the accompanying adult before going to the office.

How much does a state ID cost?

There is no single nationwide fee.

Each state sets its own fees, and some states offer reduced-fee or no-fee ID cards for people who meet specific requirements. California, for example, has reduced-fee and no-fee programs for certain applicants. citeturn1search3 Georgia currently lists a $32 state ID fee. citeturn1search2

Because fees change, use your state's official motor vehicle agency for the current price instead of relying on a national average.

What a state ID does not do

A state ID can be useful, but it is not a universal replacement for every other credential.

It does not give you permission to drive. It does not automatically prove citizenship. It does not automatically satisfy every organization's identification policy. And a standard state ID is not necessarily the same thing as a REAL ID-compliant state ID.

Think of the card as one piece of an identification system rather than a document that solves every identity question.

State ID, REAL ID and fake IDs are not the same thing

The word "ID" gets used loosely online, which can make the distinctions harder to follow.

A state ID is issued by a state government. A REAL ID is a state-issued driver's license or identification card that meets federal standards. A fake ID is something that falsely claims government authority or misrepresents an identity or credential.

FakeIDs.com's educational explanation of what a fake ID actually means makes this distinction clearly: the important issue is not simply whether a card looks convincing, but whether it was actually issued by the authority it claims to represent.

There is another useful angle here. A legitimate government credential can sometimes be questioned simply because it is unfamiliar or difficult to verify. FakeIDs.com's article about real driver's licenses being mistaken for fake ones explores why visual familiarity and actual authenticity are not always the same thing.

For an official state ID application, however, the answer is much simpler: use the documents and process specified by the state agency.

How to get a state ID without a driver's license

The exact process depends on your state, but the path usually looks like this:

  1. Find your state's motor vehicle agency. USAGov maintains a state-by-state directory of motor vehicle services. citeturn1search11
  2. Choose the identification-card service. Look for "state ID," "identification card" or "non-driver ID."
  3. Check the document list. Separate identity, residency, Social Security and name-change requirements.
  4. Decide whether you want REAL ID. If you travel domestically or need a covered federal credential, compare REAL ID with your other accepted IDs.
  5. Check the fee and appointment rules. Some states require an appointment while others allow walk-ins or online pre-application.
  6. Bring the required originals or certified documents. Do not assume photocopies or phone screenshots will be accepted.
  7. Complete the application and identity checks. This may include a photograph, signature or other verification steps.

Before you go to the DMV

A five-minute document check can save you an unnecessary trip.

  • Confirm you are using the correct state agency.
  • Check whether you are applying for a standard ID or REAL ID.
  • Make sure your name matches your identity documents.
  • Confirm how many proof-of-residency documents you need.
  • Check whether documents must be originals, certified copies or recent statements.
  • Check the current fee.
  • Bring any legal name-change documents that apply.
  • Check whether you need an appointment.
One final check: Requirements vary by state. The most reliable source is the official motor vehicle agency where you are applying. USAGov's state motor vehicle directory is a good place to find the correct agency. citeturn1search11

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a state ID if I don't know how to drive?

Yes. A state ID does not require driving privileges. It is specifically designed to provide identification without authorizing you to operate a vehicle.

Can I have a state ID without having a driver's license?

Yes. That is one of the main purposes of a state identification card. Your state may have separate rules if you already hold a driver's license or ID from another state.

Can a state ID be REAL ID-compliant?

Yes. REAL ID can apply to state identification cards as well as driver's licenses. USAGov says applicants can choose to make a state ID REAL ID-compliant when applying or renewing, subject to state procedures. citeturn1search0

Can I fly with a state ID?

You can use a REAL ID-compliant state ID for domestic air travel. A standard state ID that is not REAL ID-compliant is not enough by itself at TSA checkpoints where REAL ID requirements apply. You can use another accepted form of identification, such as a passport. citeturn1search1

Do I need a Social Security card to get a state ID?

Not necessarily the physical card in every situation. Requirements vary by state and credential. Some states require your Social Security number or an accepted document showing it. Check the state's current document list.

Can a minor get a state ID?

In many states, yes. Age rules and whether a parent or responsible adult must accompany the applicant vary by state.

How much does a state ID cost?

There is no national fee. Each state sets its own price, and some states have reduced-fee or no-fee programs for eligible applicants.

What is the difference between a state ID and a REAL ID?

A state ID is the credential itself. REAL ID describes a state-issued driver's license or identification card that meets federal standards for specified federal purposes.

Source-first reminder: US ID Laws is an educational reference. State ID requirements, fees and document lists can change. Verify the current rules with your state's official motor vehicle agency before applying.
