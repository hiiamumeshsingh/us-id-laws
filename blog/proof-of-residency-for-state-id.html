What Counts as Proof of Residency for a State ID?

Understand the difference between REAL ID-compliant credentials and standard state-issued identification.

Last reviewed: September 2026

Proof of residency sounds like one of those DMV requirements that should be easy. You live somewhere, so surely you have something with your address on it.

Then you look around and realize the electricity bill is in your partner's name, your bank statement is paperless, your lease is in a roommate's name and half your mail still goes to your old address.

That is where people get stuck.

The important thing to understand is that proof of residency is not the same as proof of identity. The state wants evidence that you actually live at the address you put on your application. What counts as that evidence depends on the state and, in some cases, whether you are applying for a REAL ID.

Quick answer: Common proof-of-residency documents include utility bills, bank or financial statements, leases, mortgage records, insurance documents, government mail, employment records and school records. But states set their own lists. Some require one document, others require two or more, and the rules can be different for REAL ID.

Why does the DMV care where you live?

Your address is part of your identity record.

When a state issues an identification card, it needs more than your name and date of birth. It also needs to know that you are actually a resident of that state and where official correspondence should be associated with your record.

That is why a passport or birth certificate alone may not solve the whole application. Those documents can establish who you are, but they do not necessarily establish where you currently live.

California, for example, requires acceptable residency documentation when someone has never previously held a California driver's license or ID card. citeturn0search9

What documents usually count as proof of residency?

There is no universal national list, but the same categories appear repeatedly across state DMV requirements.

California's current residency lists include utility bills, bank records, insurance documents, mortgage bills, leases, school documents, employment documents, tax records, government documents and several other categories. Georgia similarly accepts utility bills, financial statements, leases, government records, employer documents, school records and medical statements for residency purposes. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Those examples show the general pattern, but they are not a nationwide checklist. Always check the issuing state's current list.

How many proof-of-residency documents do you need?

This is one of the biggest state-by-state differences.

California currently requires two different printed documents for REAL ID residency. Both must show the applicant's first and last name and the same California mailing address listed on the application. citeturn0search0

Georgia also requires two residency documents for its REAL ID process. Its current guidance says the documents must come from separate sources or accounts and show the applicant's name and current residential address. citeturn0search1

That does not mean every state requires two documents for every ID application.

One document can be enough in one situation and not enough in another. The credential type, state and applicant's circumstances can all matter.

What if the utility bill is not in your name?

This is probably one of the most common real-world problems.

You may live with a spouse, parent, partner or roommate while someone else handles the household bills. That does not necessarily mean you cannot prove residency.

Some states have ways to connect you to the person whose name appears on the residency document.

California's REAL ID checklist, for example, says that if your name does not appear on a residency document, you may use a birth certificate, marriage license or domestic partnership registration to trace your relationship to the person named on the document. citeturn0search0

California also specifically says a minor child may use a residency document in a parent's name when the child provides documentation showing the relationship. citeturn0search22

But do not assume your state has the same exception. Check its rules before relying on someone else's bill.

Can you use a lease as proof of residency?

Often, yes.

A signed rental or lease agreement is one of the most straightforward ways to show that you live at an address.

California includes a rental or lease agreement signed by the owner or landlord and tenant or resident on its accepted residency list. Georgia also accepts signed leases and rental agreements under its residency categories. citeturn0search0turn0search1

If you are using a lease, check that the names, address and signatures meet your state's requirements. A screenshot of a rental listing is not the same thing as a signed lease.

What if you live with your parents?

You are not automatically out of options just because the house or apartment is in your parent's name.

Some states allow dependent applicants to use a parent's residency document with additional evidence of the relationship. California's REAL ID materials provide one example of this approach for minor children. citeturn0search22

If you are an adult living with a parent, the rules may be different. The safest approach is to look specifically for your state's rules on applicants who live with another household member.

Do not assume that a parent's utility bill automatically proves your residency.

What if you live with a roommate?

A roommate situation is similar, but there may not be a formal family relationship connecting you to the person named on the bill.

Your own bank statement, insurance document, employer record, lease or other accepted document may be easier.

If none of those shows your current address, check whether your state accepts a signed residency affidavit or another alternative. Some states provide specific options for applicants whose standard documents do not fit their living situation.

Georgia, for example, lists a residence-verification form and certain letters from shelters or similar facilities among its accepted residency evidence. citeturn0search1

What if you are a college student?

Students can run into an unusual problem: the address where they live and the address connected to their family records may be different.

School records can sometimes help.

California's accepted residency list includes qualifying school documents from public or private primary, secondary and post-secondary institutions. Georgia also lists school records, transcripts and certain student documentation among its residency categories. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Again, the details matter. A college email saying "welcome to campus" is not automatically the same thing as an accepted residency document.

Can a bank statement prove residency?

In many states, yes.

Bank and other financial institution records are commonly included on state residency lists. California accepts records from banks, credit unions and certain other financial institutions. Georgia accepts bank, credit union, investment, credit card and loan or financing statements under its residency categories. citeturn0search0turn0search1

The statement generally needs to show your name and the relevant address. Some states also impose a date requirement.

If you receive everything electronically, check whether your state accepts a printed statement. California's REAL ID checklist specifically refers to printed residency documents. citeturn0search0

How recent does proof of residency need to be?

There is no single national expiration period.

Some documents can be accepted based on their date. Others may be tied to a current tax year, school year or calendar year.

Georgia, for example, specifies different date ranges depending on the document category. Utility bills, financial statements and some mail generally must be dated within the past six months, while some government, school and employment documents can fall within broader current or preceding-year periods. citeturn0search1

That is why bringing a five-year-old statement is a bad gamble even if it still has your correct address.

Does a P.O. box count as proof of residency?

A P.O. box and a physical residence are not always treated the same way.

Georgia explicitly says P.O. boxes do not prove residency for its REAL ID process. citeturn0search1

California takes a more specific approach. A P.O. box can be used as the mailing address for a REAL ID, but one residency document must show both the P.O. box and physical residence address. citeturn0search0turn0search3

This is a good example of why copying another state's checklist can get you into trouble.

What if you recently moved?

Recent movers are often caught between addresses.

Your old bank statement shows your previous state. Your new utility account has not started yet. Your lease is signed, but your driver's license or ID still shows the old address.

Do not panic. Look for the state's alternative residency documents.

A signed lease, USPS change-of-address confirmation, new insurance record, government correspondence or other newly issued document may qualify depending on the state. California, for example, includes USPS change-of-address confirmation among its accepted residency documents. citeturn0search0

The important part is that the document must meet the state's actual requirements. A document showing an address by itself is not automatically acceptable.

What if you are staying somewhere temporarily?

This is where the standard "utility bill + lease" model stops being useful.

People stay in extended-stay hotels, shelters, transitional housing, shared homes and other arrangements for all kinds of reasons. Some states specifically account for these situations.

Georgia, for example, lists rent receipts for an extended-stay motel and certain letters from shelters or similar facilities as possible residency evidence. California includes letters from certain shelters, nonprofit organizations, faith-based organizations, employers or government agencies in its residency list. citeturn0search1turn0search0

If your housing situation is unusual, search the official state document list for your situation instead of assuming you need a utility bill.

What about people experiencing homelessness?

Not having a conventional permanent address does not necessarily mean you cannot obtain identification.

States may have alternative documentation procedures for people staying in shelters or other temporary arrangements.

California's REAL ID residency list, for example, includes a letter attesting that an applicant resides in California from certain shelters, nonprofit organizations, faith-based organizations, employers or government agencies. citeturn0search0

Because these programs can be very specific, use the state's current instructions rather than relying on advice from a general ID checklist.

What counts as proof of residency for REAL ID?

REAL ID is where the residency requirement becomes especially important.

Federal REAL ID standards apply to state-issued credentials, but the state still handles the actual application process and document verification.

California currently requires two different documents showing the applicant's name and California address for REAL ID. Georgia likewise requires two residency documents for its REAL ID process. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Typical examples include:

  • Utility or phone bills
  • Bank and financial statements
  • Insurance records
  • Lease or mortgage documents
  • Government correspondence
  • Employment records
  • School records
  • Tax documents
  • Vehicle registration or insurance documents

These categories are common, but the accepted list is still state-specific.

What gets proof of residency rejected?

Most problems are not dramatic. They are mismatches.

Your document may be rejected because:

  • The name does not match your application
  • The address is different from the address on the application
  • The document is too old
  • It is not one of the state's accepted document types
  • It only shows a P.O. box when a physical address is required
  • You brought one document when the state requires two
  • You used a screenshot or digital copy when printed documentation is required
  • The document belongs to someone else and you did not bring the required relationship evidence

For example, California's REAL ID checklist says both residency documents must show the applicant's first and last name and the same mailing address used on the application. citeturn0search0

Georgia similarly requires the name and current residential address and specifies that its two documents must come from separate sources or accounts. citeturn0search1

Proof of residency is not something you should try to fake

If you do not have the document you need, the answer is to find an accepted alternative, not to modify a bill or create a document that looks official.

That distinction is important because an identification application is an official government process. FakeIDs' explanation of fake IDs and government credentials discusses the difference between an actual government-issued identification document and something that only represents itself as one.

For another educational perspective, its discussion of ID authenticity checks shows why official credentials are evaluated against information and characteristics beyond simply having an address printed on a card.

If your residency situation does not fit the normal checklist, contact the state agency and ask which legitimate alternative documents it accepts.

What should you bring to the DMV?

If you are applying for a state ID, build your document set around three separate questions:

  1. Who are you? Bring the required identity and date-of-birth document.
  2. Where do you live? Bring the required residency documents.
  3. What other information does the state require? This may include Social Security information, lawful-status documents or name-change records.

For a REAL ID application, California's current process requires proof of identity, Social Security information and two proofs of California residency. citeturn0search4turn0search0

Georgia's REAL ID guidance similarly separates identity, Social Security and residency documentation. citeturn0search1

Keeping these categories separate makes the checklist much easier to understand.

A simple residency checklist

Before your appointment:
  • ☐ I know how many residency documents my state requires.
  • ☐ Each document is on the state's accepted list.
  • ☐ My name appears correctly, or I have the required relationship/name-change documents.
  • ☐ The address matches the address on my application.
  • ☐ The documents are recent enough for my state's rules.
  • ☐ I checked whether copies, originals or printed documents are required.
  • ☐ I checked special rules if I live with parents, roommates or in temporary housing.
  • ☐ I checked separate REAL ID requirements if I want a REAL ID.

Frequently asked questions

What is proof of residency for a state ID?

It is a document or set of documents used to show that you live at the address listed on your state ID application. Common examples include utility bills, bank statements, leases, insurance documents, government mail and school records.

Can a bank statement be proof of residency?

Often yes. Many states accept financial institution records when they show the applicant's name and current address. The age and format requirements vary.

Can I use a utility bill in my spouse's name?

Possibly, depending on the state. Some states provide ways to establish your relationship to the person named on the bill. California, for example, has a tracing-document option for certain applicants. citeturn0search0

Can I use a lease as proof of residency?

Often. Signed rental or lease agreements are commonly accepted, but check your state's requirements for signatures, names and dates.

Does a P.O. box count as proof of residency?

Not always. Georgia says a P.O. box does not prove residency for its REAL ID process, while California allows a P.O. box as a mailing address with additional physical-address documentation. citeturn0search1turn0search0

How many proof-of-residency documents do I need?

It depends on the state and application. Some situations require one document, while REAL ID applications in states such as California and Georgia require two residency documents. citeturn0search0turn0search1

What if I do not have anything in my name?

Check your state's alternative-document rules. Depending on your situation, you may be able to use a relationship document, school record, government document, shelter letter, employer document or another accepted form of evidence.
