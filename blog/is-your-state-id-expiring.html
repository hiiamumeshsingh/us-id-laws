Is Your State ID Expiring? Here’s What to Do

Understand the difference between REAL ID-compliant credentials and standard state-issued identification.

Last reviewed: September 2026

Most people do not think about their state ID until they need it.

Then they pull it out of a wallet, look at the expiration date and realize it is either about to expire or already has.

Renewing it is usually straightforward. The part that causes trouble is assuming every state handles renewal the same way. Some let eligible adults renew online. Some require an office visit in certain situations. An expired card can change your options. A name or address change can change them too.

Quick answer: Renew your state ID through the agency that issued it before the expiration date. Depending on your state and circumstances, you may be able to renew online, by mail or in person. If you want to upgrade to REAL ID, change your name, update certain information or renew after a long period of expiration, you may need to visit an office and provide additional documents.

Start with the date on the card

Your ID card has the answer to the first question: when does it expire?

Do not wait for a renewal notice to tell you. Notices are helpful, but the expiration date printed on the credential is what you should watch.

California tells ID holders to renew before expiration and offers several renewal methods depending on eligibility. Virginia allows ID renewal up to 12 months before the expiration date. citeturn0search0turn0search2

A good habit is to start the process while the card is still valid. That gives you time to deal with a document problem, an address change or an appointment if the online option is not available.

Renewal is different from replacing a lost ID

This sounds obvious, but the two transactions can get mixed up.

Renewal Replacement
Why? The card is expiring The card is lost, stolen or damaged
Old card Usually still in your possession Usually unavailable or unusable
Timing Usually tied to the expiration window Can be requested when the card is missing or damaged
Documents Often fewer if your record is unchanged May require identity verification if the record cannot be confirmed

If your card is still valid but you lost it, you generally need a replacement rather than waiting for the renewal window.

If it is sitting in your wallet but the expiration date is approaching, you need a renewal.

Can you renew a state ID online?

Sometimes, and this is one of the biggest conveniences states have added.

But online renewal is usually an eligibility rule, not a universal service.

Virginia, for example, allows eligible adults to renew an ID online every other cycle, subject to conditions such as the card not being expired and the applicant not needing certain changes or new documentation. citeturn0search2turn0search8

California also offers online ID renewal for eligible applicants. Its current rules include an expiration window and restrictions on certain changes, such as changing your address or applying for a REAL ID for the first time. citeturn0search1turn0search4

So if the website tells you that you cannot renew online, it does not necessarily mean there is a problem with your ID. You may simply fall outside the online eligibility rules.

When do you have to renew in person?

There is no single answer, but several situations commonly push an applicant toward an office visit.

You may need to go in person if:

  • Your ID has been expired for too long
  • You need a new photograph
  • You are changing your name
  • You need to correct important personal information
  • You are applying for REAL ID for the first time
  • Your identity or legal presence needs additional verification
  • Your state has reached the limit for consecutive online or mail renewals
  • You fall into an age or status category with different renewal rules

Virginia, for example, requires an in-person renewal for certain applicants, including people whose cards are expired, applicants who need a new photo and people who must provide proof of legal presence. citeturn0search2

California similarly limits online and mail renewal when applicants need certain changes or are applying for REAL ID for the first time. citeturn0search4

What if your ID is already expired?

Do not assume you need to start over immediately.

The answer depends on how long it has been expired and your state's rules.

California, for example, allows certain ID holders to renew online within a specified period after expiration, while its mail-renewal rules include an additional restriction when an ID has been expired for more than one year. citeturn0search4

Virginia takes a different approach. Its current guidance says that if an ID card is not renewed before expiration, the applicant may have to visit a customer service center and present proof of legal presence. citeturn0search6

That difference is exactly why searching for a generic "expired state ID" answer can be misleading. The state matters.

Do you need your old ID to renew?

Usually, having the current card makes the process easier, but the exact requirement varies.

Virginia tells applicants renewing at a customer service center to present their current ID card. citeturn0search2

If you no longer have the card, you are generally dealing with a replacement situation rather than a normal renewal. See our guide to replacing a lost or stolen state ID.

What documents do you need for renewal?

If nothing has changed and the state can verify your existing record, renewal can be relatively light on paperwork.

But you may need additional documents if you are:

  • Changing your name
  • Changing or correcting your address
  • Applying for REAL ID for the first time
  • Renewing a limited-term credential
  • Renewing after a long expiration period
  • Providing legal-presence evidence again

For REAL ID, USAGov says states generally require proof of identity, Social Security number and residency. Examples include a U.S. birth certificate or Permanent Resident Card, Social Security documentation and a lease, mortgage statement, utility bill or bank statement. citeturn0search12

Those are federal-standard examples, not a universal state checklist. Your state's licensing agency decides exactly what it will accept.

What if you want REAL ID when you renew?

This is where renewal and upgrading can become two different things.

If you already have a REAL ID, renewing it is generally treated as a renewal of an existing REAL ID credential, subject to your state's rules.

If you have a standard ID and want to make it REAL ID-compliant for the first time, expect additional documentation.

California says eligible applicants can renew an existing ID online, but its online rules exclude people applying for a REAL ID-compliant ID for the first time. Virginia likewise requires applicants who want to obtain REAL ID to visit a customer service center with the required documents. citeturn0search4turn0search8

USAGov's current REAL ID guidance says states decide the application process and documents needed. citeturn0search12

So if you have been putting off REAL ID, renewal can be a convenient time to deal with it, but do not wait until the last week if your state requires an office appointment.

What if your address changed?

An address change can take you out of the easiest renewal path.

California's current online renewal rules say certain personal information, including the address, cannot be changed during the online renewal transaction. Virginia allows an address update during its renewal transaction but requires the DMV record to contain the correct address for online renewal eligibility. citeturn0search1turn0search7

The practical lesson is simple: update your address before you start renewal if your state tells you to do so.

And remember that the mailing address printed on the card is not necessarily the same thing as proof of residency. If the state asks for residency documents, you still need to provide documents from its accepted list.

Our guide to proof of residency for a state ID covers the documents people commonly use.

What if your name changed?

A name change is another reason not to assume online renewal will work.

States often want legal documentation connecting the old name to the new one. Depending on the circumstances, that might be a marriage record, divorce decree or court order.

California says name changes must be processed at a DMV office rather than through its ordinary online renewal process. citeturn0search3

If your Social Security record also uses your old name, make sure you understand the state's sequencing requirements before you go. A mismatch between your government records can create a delay.

What if your ID is a limited-term ID?

This matters particularly for some non-U.S. citizens.

A limited-term credential can have a shorter validity period because its expiration is connected to verified legal presence or another temporary status.

Virginia, for example, says people who are temporarily authorized to be in the United States must renew their ID in person and provide proof of legal presence. citeturn0search2

California also says limited-term credentials can have expiration dates connected to the underlying identity or legal-presence document. citeturn0search0

If your immigration document is about to expire, do not wait until the ID itself expires to investigate the renewal rules.

Do seniors have different renewal rules?

Sometimes.

States can create different validity periods or renewal procedures for older adults.

California, for example, has a senior citizen ID card category for people age 62 and older, with a different validity period from its regular ID card. citeturn0search0

That does not mean every state offers the same program. Check the specific rules for your state and age group.

What about minors?

Children and teenagers can have different renewal rules because their ID validity period and application requirements may be different.

Virginia, for example, has separate rules for child identification privilege cards, including different validity periods and in-person requirements. citeturn0search11

If you are renewing a child's ID, do not use an adult renewal checklist without checking the state's child-ID rules first.

How much does renewal cost?

There is no nationwide renewal fee.

States set their own prices and may use different pricing based on the credential's validity period or the applicant's category.

Virginia currently charges $2 per year for an ID renewal, with a $10 minimum and $16 maximum. California's current materials list a $40 renewal fee for its standard identification card. citeturn0search2turn0search21

Those numbers are examples, not a national range. Check your state's current fee schedule before submitting the renewal.

What happens after you renew?

In many states, the new card is mailed to you rather than handed over at the counter.

California says renewed ID cards are mailed, and Virginia likewise mails renewed identification cards. citeturn0search0turn0search11

Your state may give you a receipt, temporary document or other confirmation while you wait. Do not assume that every organization will treat that document as equivalent to the physical ID.

If you need the card for travel, employment or another time-sensitive purpose, check the expected delivery timeline before your current credential expires.

What if the new card never arrives?

Do not immediately submit a second renewal.

First check the status of the existing transaction. California, for example, provides a DL/ID status service and says applicants should check their status if the card has not arrived within the expected timeframe. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Also confirm that the agency has the correct mailing address. Some agencies will not forward identification cards through the postal system.

Virginia specifically warns that the U.S. Postal Service will not forward its ID cards and tells residents to keep their DMV address current. citeturn0search11

One thing people get wrong about renewal

Renewing an ID is not the same as proving that the old card was genuine.

The state already has a record of your credential. The renewal process is about confirming that record, updating information where necessary and issuing a new credential.

That is also why websites offering to "renew" a state ID outside the state agency should make you cautious.

FakeIDs' explanation of fake IDs makes a useful distinction here: a government credential gets its authority from the issuing system behind it, not from the way the physical card looks.

And if you have ever wondered why a genuine government ID can still be questioned by a business, this discussion of legitimate driver's licenses being mistaken for fake IDs is a useful reminder that visual familiarity and official validity are different things.

For a renewal, stay with the issuing agency. That is the only route that actually updates the state's credential record.

A better way to plan your renewal

Do not wait until the expiration date is staring you in the face.

A few weeks or months before expiration, answer these questions:

  1. Is my ID still valid?
  2. Has my address changed?
  3. Has my legal name changed?
  4. Do I want REAL ID?
  5. Has my immigration or legal-presence status changed?
  6. Can I renew online, by mail or do I need an appointment?
  7. What is the current fee?
  8. How long will the replacement card take to arrive?

If all of your information is unchanged, you may have a very easy renewal. If any of those answers is "yes," check the state's special instructions before choosing the quickest-looking option.

State ID renewal checklist

Before renewing:
  • ☐ I checked the expiration date on my current ID.
  • ☐ I found my state's official renewal page.
  • ☐ I checked whether I qualify for online or mail renewal.
  • ☐ My address on the DMV record is current.
  • ☐ I know whether I need to change my name or other information.
  • ☐ I decided whether I want a REAL ID.
  • ☐ I have the required documents if an office visit is necessary.
  • ☐ I checked the current renewal fee.
  • ☐ I know how long the new card should take to arrive.

Frequently asked questions

How often do you have to renew a state ID?

It depends on the state and the type of ID. States set their own validity periods, so there is no single nationwide renewal schedule.

Can I renew my state ID online?

Sometimes. Eligibility depends on the state and your circumstances. Virginia and California both offer online renewal for certain eligible ID holders. citeturn0search2turn0search4

Can I renew an expired state ID?

Often, yes, but the process may be different after expiration. Some states allow renewal during a limited post-expiration period, while others require an in-person application or additional documents.

Do I need a new photo when I renew?

It depends on the state and the renewal method. Some online or mail renewals can reuse an existing photo, while an in-person renewal may require a new one.

Can I get REAL ID when I renew?

Yes, in many states. However, a first-time REAL ID upgrade may require an office visit and identity, Social Security and residency documents. citeturn0search12turn0search8

What if my name changed?

You may need to renew in person and provide legal name-change documentation. California, for example, requires name changes to be processed at a DMV office. citeturn0search3

What if my immigration status is temporary?

Your renewal process may include additional legal-presence verification, and the ID's validity may be tied to the authorized period of stay. Virginia and California both have special rules for certain temporary-status applicants. citeturn0search2turn0search0
