Can You Get a State ID Without a Social Security Number?

Understand the difference between REAL ID-compliant credentials and standard state-issued identification.

Last reviewed: September 2026

“I don't have a Social Security number. Can I still get a state ID?”

It is a reasonable question, and the answer is not as simple as yes or no.

A Social Security number is commonly part of a state ID application, but the rules are set by each state. Your situation can matter too. Someone who has an SSN but does not have the card in their possession is in a different situation from someone who has never been eligible for an SSN.

Quick answer: Some states require an SSN from applicants who have one, while others have procedures for applicants who are not eligible for an SSN. If you are legally present but not eligible for an SSN, a state may provide an alternative process. Do not assume that an ITIN, passport or another number automatically replaces an SSN. Check the exact rules for the state issuing your ID.

Why does a state ID application ask for your SSN?

The Social Security number is not printed on your photo ID as a substitute for identity. It is used as an identifying record that can help the issuing agency verify information.

The Social Security Administration says states may request an SSN when issuing a state non-driver identification card. Federal law also allows states to use SSNs in administering driver's license and motor vehicle laws. citeturn2search0turn2search3

That is why an ID application can ask for an SSN even though the number does not appear openly on the card.

California, for example, says its DMV verifies the SSN, name and date of birth with the Social Security Administration when required. citeturn1search6

There are three very different “I don't have an SSN” situations

This is where the question gets interesting.

When someone says they do not have a Social Security number, they could mean:

Situation What it means
I have an SSN but lost my card You have a number. The issue is finding or replacing the card, not obtaining a new number.
I am eligible for an SSN but have never received one You may need to resolve your SSN status before completing the state ID application.
I am not eligible for an SSN Your state may have a different procedure or exception.

Those situations should not be treated as interchangeable.

What if you have an SSN but lost the card?

This is usually the easiest situation to sort out.

Losing your Social Security card does not mean you lost your Social Security number.

If the state asks for your SSN, you generally need to provide the number or follow the state's verification process. You may not necessarily need to produce the physical Social Security card.

For example, California's current REAL ID guidance says applicants provide their Social Security number and lists a Social Security card, W-2 or pay stub among possible evidence. The California DMV also says a card is not required in some REAL ID situations when the SSN can otherwise be provided and verified. citeturn0search4turn1search5

So don't immediately assume you need to order a replacement card before you can apply for an ID.

What if you have never had an SSN?

This is more complicated because the reason matters.

U.S. citizens can request an SSN. Some noncitizens can also get one, particularly when they are authorized to work in the United States. The Social Security Administration says lawfully admitted noncitizens may receive certain services without an SSN, but an SSN is generally available to noncitizens with work authorization or a qualifying reason. citeturn2search6turn2search7

If you are eligible for an SSN but simply have never obtained one, your state's ID process may tell you to apply for one or provide evidence related to your application.

If you are not eligible for an SSN, the situation can be different.

Can you get a state ID if you are not eligible for an SSN?

Possibly.

This is one of the most important points in this article: not having an SSN does not automatically mean you are prohibited from getting a state ID everywhere.

State rules vary, and some states have specific procedures for people who are legally present but ineligible for an SSN.

California provides a useful example. Its current DMV documentation says applicants who are legally present in the United States but ineligible for an SSN are exempt from the SSN requirement for certain noncompliant DL/ID applications. citeturn1search36

That is a California rule. It should not be read as a nationwide guarantee.

The practical takeaway is simple: if you do not have an SSN because you are not eligible for one, look for your state's specific “no SSN,” “not eligible for SSN” or similar instructions.

Does an ITIN replace a Social Security number?

Generally, you should not assume that it does.

An Individual Taxpayer Identification Number, or ITIN, is issued by the IRS for federal tax purposes. It is not the same thing as an SSN.

A state may have a specific rule about what applicants without an SSN should provide. That does not mean an ITIN automatically satisfies the requirement.

If your state's ID instructions do not mention ITINs, contact the issuing agency before your appointment rather than showing up with an ITIN and assuming it will work.

What does REAL ID change?

REAL ID adds federal standards to state-issued driver's licenses and identification cards, but the state still runs the application process.

USAGov says that when applying for a REAL ID, most state licensing agencies require proof of identity, Social Security number and state residency. It also makes clear that the state determines the process and documents needed. citeturn0search4

That means you should be especially careful if you do not have an SSN and want a REAL ID.

Do not assume that a normal state ID exception automatically means you qualify for a REAL ID. The state's REAL ID rules may be different.

California illustrates this distinction. Its REAL ID guidance says applicants provide an SSN, with exceptions, and its separate documentation rules distinguish different types of credentials and applicants. citeturn1search5turn1search36

What if you are a noncitizen?

Having or not having an SSN is only one part of the application.

Depending on the state and credential, you may also need documents establishing identity, date of birth, residency and legal presence or another qualifying status.

California, for example, says applicants for its driver's licenses or ID cards may need a birth date/legal presence document. Its current guidance lists examples such as a U.S. birth certificate or passport for people born in the U.S., and immigration documents for certain noncitizens. citeturn1search3

The Social Security Administration also explains that many lawfully admitted noncitizens can receive certain services without an SSN, while eligibility for an SSN itself depends on circumstances such as work authorization. citeturn2search26

So “I don't have an SSN” does not by itself tell you whether you qualify for a state ID.

What if the DMV says your SSN does not match?

Sometimes the problem is not that you lack an SSN. It is that the information connected to it does not match the application.

A name change is a common example.

If your state verifies your SSN against Social Security records and your name or date of birth does not match, the application may be delayed until the records are corrected or the discrepancy is resolved.

California's DMV says it verifies the SSN, name and birth date with the Social Security Administration and will not issue or renew a credential until the required information is verified. citeturn1search6

If you recently changed your name, make sure your Social Security record and your identity documents tell the same story before your DMV appointment.

Do you need the physical Social Security card?

Not necessarily.

This is another distinction that gets lost in online checklists.

A state may ask for your SSN without requiring the physical card. Other situations may specifically require a document showing the number.

USAGov lists a Social Security card, W-2 or pay stub as examples of evidence for REAL ID applications, while California's REAL ID materials say applicants must provide the Social Security number and note that the card itself is not required. citeturn0search4turn1search5

Always follow the document list for your particular state and credential.

What if you refuse to provide your SSN?

If you have an SSN and your state requires you to provide it, refusing to give it can prevent the application from being processed.

California states this directly: if an applicant has a Social Security number, the law requires it to be provided on the application. citeturn1search2

That does not mean every state uses exactly the same rule. It means you should distinguish between not having an SSN and choosing not to provide an SSN you already have.

What if you are applying for your first state ID?

Build your application around the actual requirements rather than trying to find one magic document.

You may need separate evidence for:

  • Identity and date of birth
  • Social Security number or an applicable exception
  • State residency
  • Legal presence or immigration status, where required
  • Legal name changes, if applicable

California's current ID process, for example, separates these categories and requires an identity document, SSN information and residency evidence, with additional documentation for REAL ID. citeturn1search0turn0search2

That same basic structure appears in REAL ID guidance from USAGov, although the exact documents and exceptions remain state-specific. citeturn0search4

Do not confuse an SSN with proof of identity

This distinction is worth remembering.

An SSN helps agencies identify and verify a record. It is not a photo ID.

The Social Security Administration itself says the SSN card is not considered an identity document because it does not display identifying information about you in the same way an identity document does. citeturn2search5

That is why losing your Social Security card does not necessarily mean you have lost your ability to prove your identity. Your passport, birth certificate, state ID and other accepted documents serve different purposes.

And no, a document that “looks official” is not a workaround

If someone tells you that you can solve an ID application problem by buying a card that looks like a government credential, stop there.

A state ID gets its authority from the state that issued it and the records behind it. A document that simply imitates that appearance does not become a government credential because the design looks convincing.

If you cannot satisfy an SSN requirement, the solution is to find the state's legitimate exception or alternative process.

What should you do if you do not have an SSN?

Before visiting the DMV, answer these questions:

  1. Do I actually have an SSN? If you lost the card, that is different from never having a number.
  2. Am I eligible for an SSN? If you are unsure, check with the Social Security Administration.
  3. Does my state have an exception? Look specifically for applicants who are not eligible for an SSN.
  4. Am I applying for a standard ID or REAL ID? The requirements may differ.
  5. What other documents do I need? Identity, residency and legal-presence requirements may still apply.

If you are eligible for an SSN but do not have one, the Social Security Administration says you can request one for free. citeturn2search6

SSN and state ID checklist

Before your appointment:
  • ☐ I know whether I already have a Social Security number.
  • ☐ I know whether I am eligible for an SSN.
  • ☐ I checked my state's specific SSN requirement.
  • ☐ I checked whether the state has an exception for applicants who are not eligible for an SSN.
  • ☐ I know whether I am applying for a standard ID or REAL ID.
  • ☐ I have my identity and date-of-birth documents.
  • ☐ I have the required residency documents.
  • ☐ I have name-change documents if my records use different names.
  • ☐ I know whether the state wants the physical SSN card or only the number or another approved document.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a state ID without a Social Security number?

It depends on the state and why you do not have an SSN. Some states have procedures or exceptions for applicants who are not eligible for an SSN. Check the issuing state's rules.

Can I use an ITIN instead of an SSN?

Do not assume so. An ITIN is a tax identification number, not an SSN. Whether a state accepts any alternative number depends on its own rules.

Can I get an ID if I lost my Social Security card?

Possibly, and losing the card is different from not having an SSN. Some states can verify the number without requiring the physical card.

Do I need an SSN for REAL ID?

In most cases, state REAL ID agencies require Social Security information, but exceptions can exist. USAGov says the state determines the process and documents needed. citeturn0search4

Can a noncitizen get a state ID without an SSN?

It depends on the state, the applicant's status and the type of credential. The absence of an SSN does not by itself answer the question.

Can I apply for an SSN just to get a state ID?

The Social Security Administration says it does not assign an SSN solely because someone needs one to obtain a state-issued non-driver ID. Eligibility for an SSN is governed by separate rules. citeturn2search0
