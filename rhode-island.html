Rhode Island ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Rhode Island has specific documentation rules for driver's licenses, state identification cards and REAL ID. The state also requires photo identification at the polls. This guide explains the documents, residency rules, renewal, replacement, fees and voting requirements that apply in Rhode Island.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Rhode Island driver's license requirements

For a new Rhode Island permit or license, the DMV requires an identity document, Social Security information and two proofs of Rhode Island residency. Applicants under 18 also have additional driver-education and parent or guardian requirements. citeturn2search1turn2search5

Identity documents

The DMV's accepted identity list includes documents such as a valid U.S. passport, certified birth certificate, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, qualifying immigration documents, military ID and certain out-of-state driver's licenses. The document must establish the applicant's legal name and date of birth. citeturn1search2

Social Security information

The Social Security number must be provided on the application. A person who is not eligible for an SSN may instead provide a letter from the Social Security Administration confirming ineligibility, when the DMV's rules permit it. citeturn1search2turn2search1

Applicants under 18

Applicants under 18 who are obtaining a permit must bring an original Driver Education Certificate showing completion of the required classroom course. The DMV also requires a parent or guardian to accompany the applicant or provide the required notarized signature under the applicable circumstances. citeturn2search1

First license and renewal fees

The current DMV fee schedule lists a first license for an applicant age 18 or older at $44.50 and a standard five-year license renewal at $73.50. Age-based renewal periods and other credential types have different fees. citeturn0search2

Rhode Island state ID requirements

Rhode Island state identification cards are available to residents who meet the DMV's documentation requirements. The DMV states that an applicant must be a Rhode Island resident and cannot have a valid driver's license in Rhode Island or another state. citeturn1search1

Documents needed

  • One acceptable proof of identity
  • Social Security number or qualifying SSA ineligibility letter
  • Two proofs of Rhode Island residency
  • Legal name-change documentation when the current name differs from the identity document

Rhode Island's DMV also states that an ITIN is not accepted as a substitute for the required Social Security documentation for a state ID. citeturn1search1

Older residents

The DMV states that state identification cards have no cost for applicants age 59 or older. Other applicants should check the current State ID Fee Table for the applicable charge. citeturn1search1

Where state IDs are issued

Rhode Island's current documentation checklist notes that state ID transactions are handled at the DMV Cranston Headquarters rather than DMV satellite or AAA branch offices. Check the DMV before traveling because appointment and location procedures can change. citeturn2search3

Documents needed for a Rhode Island ID

Rhode Island uses separate document lists for standard non-REAL ID credentials and REAL ID credentials. The DMV's current checklist should be treated as the controlling list because accepted documents can depend on the transaction and the applicant's status. citeturn2search5

Proof of identity

Examples include a valid U.S. passport, certified birth certificate, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, qualifying permanent resident card, employment authorization document, qualifying foreign passport with U.S. visa and I-94, certificate of naturalization or certificate of citizenship. citeturn1search2

Original documents

For REAL ID, Rhode Island requires original identity documents rather than photocopies. If a name differs from the identity document, an authorized government document proving the name change is required. citeturn0search1

Name changes

Rhode Island accepts government-issued documents such as marriage certificates, adoption decrees or other authorized documents to establish a legal name change. Foreign-language documents may require a certified English translation. citeturn1search0

Proof of Rhode Island residency

Rhode Island requires two residency documents for new licenses, IDs and REAL ID credentials. A P.O. Box by itself is not accepted as the residential address. citeturn1search2turn0search1

Common examples

  • Utility bill issued within the applicable period
  • Bank statement or personal check
  • Payroll check
  • Current lease agreement
  • Homeowner's or renter's insurance policy
  • Automobile insurance policy
  • Property tax bill
  • W-2
  • Current Social Security Administration statement
  • Current pension or retirement account statement
  • Valid Rhode Island driver's license or identification card

Family and shared housing

Rhode Island allows certain residency documents to be in the name of an immediate family member or another person with the same last name. Special documentation is also available for residents of shelters or halfway houses. citeturn0search1

Minors

For minors, the DMV accepts certain school records showing the student's residence and the Rhode Island license or ID of the individual signing the minor's application, in addition to the applicable residency documents. citeturn0search1

Rhode Island REAL ID requirements

REAL ID is optional in Rhode Island. A REAL ID-compliant driver's license or identification card has a white star inside a gold circle and can be used for covered federal purposes such as domestic air travel and access to certain federal facilities. A standard credential remains valid for driving. citeturn1search0

REAL ID checklist

  • One proof of identity
  • Social Security number or qualifying SSA ineligibility letter
  • Two proofs of Rhode Island residency
  • Legal name-change document when applicable

REAL ID applicants must bring the required original documents. A first REAL ID application must be completed in person. citeturn0search1turn1search0

REAL ID cost

Rhode Island states that there is no additional REAL ID cost when the credential is obtained within the normal renewal period. Outside the renewal period, the DMV currently lists an additional $28.50 charge on its REAL ID page. citeturn1search0

After the first REAL ID

Once a person has received a REAL ID credential, eligible future renewals may be completed online according to the normal renewal schedule. The first REAL ID application itself cannot be completed entirely online because the required documents must be reviewed in person. citeturn1search0

Non-U.S. citizens

Rhode Island allows qualifying non-U.S. citizens to apply for REAL ID. The DMV lists documents such as a permanent resident card or employment authorization document as possible identity and lawful-status evidence, together with the required Social Security and residency documentation. citeturn1search0turn1search2

New Rhode Island residents

Rhode Island law requires a person who moves to the state and establishes residency to obtain a Rhode Island operator's license within 30 days. For an out-of-state transfer, the DMV requires the current out-of-state license or, when unavailable, an acceptable certified driving record plus identity documentation. citeturn2search2

Transferring a REAL ID

A REAL ID issued by another state does not automatically transfer as a Rhode Island REAL ID. Applicants who want Rhode Island's REAL ID designation must provide the required Rhode Island documentation during the transfer transaction. citeturn2search2

Expired out-of-state license

If the out-of-state license has been expired for five years or more, the Rhode Island DMV says the applicant must take the learner's permit test and then schedule a road test. citeturn2search2

Rhode Island voter identification requirements

Rhode Island requires voters at polling places to present valid photo identification. The requirement applies to all voters voting at the polls. citeturn1search3turn1search4

Accepted photo IDs

  • Rhode Island driver's license or permit
  • Rhode Island Voter ID Card
  • U.S. passport
  • ID issued by a U.S. educational institution
  • U.S. military ID
  • ID issued by the U.S. Government or State of Rhode Island
  • Government-issued medical card
  • Tribal ID

The Rhode Island Board of Elections maintains the current accepted-ID list. citeturn1search3

Free Rhode Island Voter ID

The Secretary of State issues free voter photo ID cards to eligible voters who do not possess one of the accepted forms of photo identification. citeturn1search3

If you do not have valid photo ID

A voter who cannot present valid identification at the polls can cast a provisional ballot. The Board of Elections provides the applicable provisional-ballot process. citeturn1search3turn1search7

Voting by mail

Rhode Island does not require identification to vote by mail ballot. This is different from the in-person polling-place requirement. citeturn1search3

First-time voter registration

Federal identification rules can also apply to certain first-time voter-registration applicants who register by mail. Rhode Island lists driver's license or state ID numbers, the last four digits of an SSN and other acceptable identification documents for those circumstances. citeturn1search5turn1search6

Renewing a Rhode Island driver's license or ID

Rhode Island's current fee schedule lists a five-year standard license renewal at $73.50. The renewal period changes for older drivers: age 71 has a four-year renewal, ages 72 through 74 have a three- or two-year renewal depending on age and age 75 and above has a two-year renewal. citeturn0search2

Online renewal

Standard Rhode Island licenses can be renewed online on eligible renewal cycles. REAL ID applications that require the initial document review must be completed in person, although later eligible REAL ID renewals can use the normal online renewal process. citeturn1search0

Non-U.S. citizens

The DMV's current checklist states that non-U.S. citizens must bring their supporting documents for review when completing renewal or duplicate transactions. citeturn2search5

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Rhode Island ID

Rhode Island offers duplicate credential services. The current DMV fee schedule lists a duplicate driver's license at $28.50. Other ID and credential replacement charges can vary by transaction. citeturn0search2

What to bring

For a renewal or duplicate transaction, the current DMV checklist generally calls for the application and one identity document. Non-U.S. citizens must bring the supporting documents applicable to their status. citeturn2search5

If your credential was stolen

If a credential was stolen and personal information may have been exposed, consider reporting the theft and watching for identity misuse. The DMV's duplicate-credential process is the official way to replace the state credential.

Rhode Island name and address changes

Name changes

Rhode Island requires an original government-issued document establishing the legal name change, such as a marriage certificate or divorce decree. The DMV notes that applicants should allow time for the name change to be reflected in Social Security Administration records before completing the DMV transaction. citeturn2search5

Address changes

The DMV's checklist identifies an address-change transaction using the applicable form and current Rhode Island license or identification card. Check the DMV's current service instructions for the available online or in-person option. citeturn2search6

Rhode Island IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Rhode Island allows qualifying non-U.S. citizens to obtain state credentials. The DMV's document lists include permanent resident cards, employment authorization documents and certain foreign passports with valid U.S. visas and I-94 documentation among acceptable identity or status documents. citeturn1search2

Social Security requirements

Applicants must provide a Social Security number or, where permitted, an SSA letter confirming that they are not eligible for an SSN. Rhode Island does not accept an ITIN as a substitute for the required Social Security documentation. citeturn1search1

F-1 and F-2 visa holders

The DMV lists additional documentation for F-1 and F-2 applicants, including the passport and I-20, together with the required Social Security information and two proofs of Rhode Island residency. citeturn2search1

Rhode Island ID law FAQs

Is REAL ID mandatory in Rhode Island?

No. REAL ID is optional. A standard Rhode Island license or ID remains valid for driving and other state purposes, but travelers may need another federally accepted document for covered federal uses.

How many residency documents do I need for a Rhode Island REAL ID?

You need two Rhode Island residency documents. The DMV does not accept a P.O. Box by itself as the residential address.

Does Rhode Island require photo ID to vote?

Yes. Rhode Island requires valid photo identification at polling places. The state provides a free voter ID card for eligible voters who do not have another accepted form of photo ID.

What happens if I forget my ID when voting in Rhode Island?

If you cannot present valid identification at the polls, you can cast a provisional ballot.

Can I vote by mail without ID in Rhode Island?

Yes. The Rhode Island Board of Elections states that identification is not required to vote by mail ballot.

How long do I have to get a Rhode Island license after moving?

Rhode Island says new residents must obtain an operator's license within 30 days of moving to the state and establishing residency.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a Rhode Island ID?

Yes, qualifying non-U.S. citizens can obtain Rhode Island credentials by providing the identity, lawful-status, Social Security and residency documents required for their circumstances.

Does Rhode Island accept an ITIN for a state ID?

No. The DMV states that an ITIN is not accepted as a substitute for the required Social Security documentation.
