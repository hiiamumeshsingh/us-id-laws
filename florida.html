Florida ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Florida's Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles issues driver licenses and identification cards. This guide covers Florida's document requirements, proof of residency, REAL ID, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and the rules that apply to people who are not U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Florida's credential system also includes special rules for immigrants, non-immigrants and people whose driving privilege is suspended or revoked.

Florida driver's license requirements

Florida's Class E driver's license is the ordinary license for operating non-commercial motor vehicles. New drivers must satisfy the applicable knowledge, vision, driving and age requirements.

Florida uses a graduated licensing system for younger drivers. A minor generally begins with a learner's license and progresses through the restrictions that apply to intermediate driving before receiving an unrestricted Class E license.

Moving to Florida

A person who becomes a Florida resident and holds a driver's license from another state must obtain a Florida license under the state's residency rules. The DMV requires the applicable identity, Social Security and Florida residential-address documents.

Existing Florida credentials

Florida credentials are issued for specific periods depending on the applicant. U.S. citizens and immigrants may be eligible for online renewal when they meet FLHSMV's requirements, while some applicants must renew in person.

Florida state ID without a driver's license

Florida offers an identification card for people who need a government-issued photo ID but do not need a driver's license.

The current Florida Driver License Handbook lists $25 for an original ID card and $25 for a replacement or renewal ID card.

Applicants must bring the required identity, Social Security and Florida residency documents. All documents must be original or certified; FLHSMV says photocopies and notarized copies are not accepted for the document-verification process.

Documents needed for a Florida ID

Florida's document process has three main parts: primary identification, Social Security documentation or applicable secondary identification and two proofs of Florida residential address.

Primary identification

U.S. citizens can generally use a certified U.S. birth certificate, valid U.S. passport or passport card, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, certificate of naturalization or certificate of citizenship.

A hospital birth certificate is not accepted as the primary identity document. FLHSMV requires the birth certificate to be issued by a government agency.

Social Security number

Florida accepts documents such as a Social Security card, W-2, SSA-1099, non-SSA-1099 or pay stub showing the full Social Security number. The document requirements can differ for applicants who are not eligible for an SSN.

Original or certified documents

Florida is explicit that photocopies and notarized copies are not accepted. Bring the original document or a certified copy issued by the appropriate agency.

Proof of Florida residency

Florida requires two documents showing your Florida residential address. The documents are used to establish where you actually reside, not simply where you receive mail.

Common examples include:

  • Deed, mortgage statement or residential lease
  • Florida vehicle registration or title
  • Utility bill
  • Insurance policy or bill
  • Bank or financial statement
  • Government mail
  • Mail from a financial institution

FLHSMV's current handbook says both documents must show the Florida residential address. Applicants should use the current FLHSMV What to Bring checklist because acceptable residency documents can depend on the applicant's circumstances.

Florida REAL ID requirements

Florida began issuing REAL ID-compliant credentials in 2010. A compliant Florida driver's license or ID card has a star in the upper-right corner.

Getting a REAL ID requires the source-document verification process. The applicant must establish identity, date of birth, Social Security number and Florida residential address using the documents required by FLHSMV.

Do you need a Florida REAL ID?

A REAL ID-compliant credential is useful when you need a state credential for federal purposes covered by the REAL ID rules, including TSA identity requirements for domestic air travel. A U.S. passport or another TSA-accepted credential can also satisfy federal identification requirements.

If you already have a Florida credential without the star and want a REAL ID, FLHSMV requires the applicable original or certified source documents for the upgrade.

Florida voter identification rules

Florida requires voters to present a current and valid photo ID with a signature when voting during early voting or on Election Day.

Accepted photo identification includes:

  • Florida driver's license
  • Florida identification card
  • U.S. passport
  • Debit or credit card
  • Military identification
  • Student identification
  • Retirement center identification
  • Neighborhood association identification
  • Public assistance identification
  • Veteran health identification card issued by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs
  • Florida concealed-weapon license
  • Employee identification issued by a federal, state, county or municipal government entity

If the photo ID does not contain the voter's signature, another identification document with the voter's signature must also be provided.

A voter who does not have acceptable identification can still vote a provisional ballot. Florida's Department of State explains that the ballot can count if the voter is otherwise eligible and the required verification process is satisfied.

Renewing a Florida ID

Florida identification cards can be renewed through the methods available to the applicant. The current FLHSMV handbook lists $25 for ID card renewal.

Florida's online renewal rules are not universal. For example, the handbook says a person is not eligible for online renewal if the previous renewal was by phone, mail or online, or if the person is not a U.S. citizen or permanent resident.

Driver license renewals have separate requirements. Drivers age 80 and older must pass a vision examination when renewing a driver's license.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Florida ID

A Florida ID card can be replaced when it is lost, stolen or damaged. The current fee listed by FLHSMV is $25.

Replacement transactions can require identity verification. If personal information has changed, such as a legal name, the applicant must provide the documentation needed to connect the old information with the new information.

If a stolen ID contained personal information, replacing the card is only one step. Review accounts and records that could be affected by the exposed information.

Name and address changes

When a legal name changes, Florida requires documentation that connects the old name with the new name. Examples include a marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order.

Florida requires residents to update the address on their driver's license or ID card. FLHSMV provides online services for eligible customers, and a new credential can be requested when necessary.

Keep the address on your DMV record current because it is used for state credential and motor-vehicle records.

Florida IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Florida issues driver's licenses and identification cards to eligible immigrants and non-immigrants who can establish the required identity and immigration status.

Immigrants

FLHSMV accepts documents such as a Permanent Resident Card, qualifying I-551 evidence and certain immigration orders or notices that establish lawful status. The documents must be original or certified.

Non-immigrants

Non-immigrants can use qualifying USCIS or U.S. Customs and Border Protection documentation, including an I-94 with the required supporting documents and a valid passport with the appropriate U.S. visa when applicable.

Credentials for non-U.S. citizens can be limited by the period of authorized stay. FLHSMV verifies immigration information before issuing or renewing the credential.

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

Florida treats the driver's license and ID-card issuance process as a controlled government credential system. Florida Statute §322.212 addresses several forms of unlawful conduct involving fraudulent driver's licenses and identification cards, including false information in an application and altered credentials.

For a state-specific explanation of the legal issues around fraudulent Florida credentials, see Is a Fake ID a Felony in Florida?. It is general educational material. The Florida statute and official state guidance are the controlling sources.

Florida ID law FAQs

Can I get a Florida ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Florida issues identification cards separately from driver's licenses.

How many proof-of-residency documents does Florida require?

Two documents showing your Florida residential address are required for a Florida driver's license or ID card application.

How much does a Florida state ID cost?

The current Florida Driver License Handbook lists $25 for an original ID card and $25 for replacement or renewal.

Does Florida require ID to vote?

Yes. Florida requires current and valid photo identification with a signature. If the photo ID does not include a signature, another signature identification is required.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a Florida ID?

Yes, eligible immigrants and non-immigrants can obtain Florida credentials when they provide the required identity, Social Security and immigration documents.

How can I tell whether my Florida ID is REAL ID compliant?

A REAL ID-compliant Florida driver's license or ID card has a star in the upper-right corner.
