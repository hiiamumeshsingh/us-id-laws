REAL ID: What It Is, Who Needs It and How to Get One

Understand the federal REAL ID framework, when it matters, what documents states may require and how to check your state's process.

Last reviewed: September 25, 2026

REAL ID is a federal standard, but your state or territory issues the actual driver's license or identification card. Requirements and application procedures therefore vary by jurisdiction.

What is REAL ID?

The REAL ID Act established minimum security standards for state-issued driver's licenses and identification cards that are accepted for certain federal purposes. A REAL ID is not a separate federal ID card. It is a state-issued credential that meets the federal standards.

The U.S. government's REAL ID guidance explains that you can generally choose to make a driver's license or state ID REAL ID-compliant when applying for or renewing the credential. citeturn0search0

Who issues a REAL ID?

Your state's driver's licensing or identification agency issues the credential. The federal government establishes the standards, while each state or territory handles applications, document review, fees, appointments and card issuance.

This distinction matters because there is no single national REAL ID application. The document list and process you follow depend on the state that issues your credential.

When do you need a REAL ID?

REAL ID matters when a federal purpose requires a REAL ID-compliant credential or another federally accepted form of identification. The rules are different from ordinary state identification requirements.

For air travel, the Transportation Security Administration explains what identification is accepted at TSA checkpoints. A REAL ID-compliant driver's license or state ID is one accepted option, but it is not the only type of acceptable identification. citeturn0search12

If you use another accepted form of identification, such as a passport, you may not need to upgrade your state credential solely for that trip. USAGov's REAL ID guidance provides additional information about alternatives. citeturn0search0

How can you tell if your ID is REAL ID-compliant?

Look at your state-issued driver's license or ID card. REAL ID-compliant cards generally have a marking that identifies them as compliant. The exact design varies by state.

USAGov notes that a star in the upper-right area of a license or state ID is one common indication of REAL ID compliance. Because card designs differ, check your state's licensing agency if you are unsure. citeturn0search0

What documents do you need for REAL ID?

The exact list comes from your state's issuing agency. In general, applicants may need documentation establishing several categories of information:

  • Identity and date of birth, such as an acceptable birth certificate or passport.
  • Social Security number, where required, or documentation concerning eligibility for an SSN.
  • State residency, often through documents showing your residential address.
  • Lawful status, when applicable under the REAL ID requirements.
  • Name changes, if your current legal name differs from the name shown on identity documents.

For examples of commonly accepted document categories, see USAGov's REAL ID page. The examples are not a substitute for your state's current document list. citeturn0search0

Do you have to get a REAL ID?

Not necessarily. A state can continue to issue credentials that are not REAL ID-compliant. Whether you need a REAL ID depends on how you use your identification and whether a federal purpose requires a compliant credential or another accepted form of ID.

For example, you may continue using a non-REAL-ID state credential for purposes where that credential is accepted under state or other applicable rules. USAGov confirms that non-REAL-ID-compliant driver's licenses and state IDs can still be available through state agencies. citeturn0search0

Can you get a REAL ID without a driver's license?

Yes, where your state offers a REAL ID-compliant non-driver identification card. REAL ID standards apply to covered state-issued driver's licenses and identification cards, so you do not have to be a driver simply because you want a REAL ID.

The application process and eligibility rules for a non-driver REAL ID are controlled by the issuing state.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a REAL ID?

Eligibility depends on the applicant's status and the rules of the issuing state. REAL ID applications can involve documentation of lawful status, and the state agency determines which documents it accepts for the application.

If your situation involves immigration documents or a limited-term credential, use the relevant state agency and official federal immigration guidance rather than relying on a general checklist.

What happens if your state ID is not REAL ID-compliant?

A non-REAL-ID credential does not automatically stop being a valid state-issued identification document. The practical issue is whether the particular place or federal purpose you are dealing with requires REAL ID or another accepted form of identification.

For air travel, check the current TSA identification requirements before your trip rather than assuming that a state ID alone will be accepted. citeturn0search12

REAL ID requirements by state

The federal standard is national, but the application experience is not. Your state determines the exact documents, fees, appointment process and other procedures used to issue a REAL ID.

REAL ID and passports

A passport is a separate form of identification issued by the federal government. A REAL ID is a state-issued driver's license or identification card that meets federal standards.

For many federal identification purposes, an acceptable passport can serve as an alternative to a REAL ID-compliant state credential. The exact requirements depend on the purpose, so check the agency responsible for the activity.

Common REAL ID mistakes

  • Assuming every state accepts the same document list.
  • Showing up without enough proof of residency.
  • Forgetting documentation for a legal name change.
  • Assuming a standard state ID is automatically REAL ID-compliant.
  • Waiting until travel or another important deadline to check the credential.
  • Using an unofficial website instead of the state's licensing agency for application instructions.

Frequently asked questions

Is REAL ID the same as a driver's license?

No. REAL ID is a federal standard that applies to covered state-issued driver's licenses and identification cards. A driver's license is the state credential itself.

Is REAL ID a federal ID card?

No. REAL ID-compliant credentials are issued by states and territories.

Can I use a passport instead of a REAL ID?

For some federal purposes, yes. For air travel, TSA lists passports and other accepted identification alongside REAL ID-compliant credentials. Always check the current requirements for the specific activity. citeturn0search12

Can I still have a standard driver's license?

Yes. States can continue to issue non-REAL-ID-compliant credentials. Whether a standard credential is sufficient depends on what you need it for. citeturn0search0

Do all states use the same REAL ID documents?

No. States determine their application process and the documents they accept. Use your state's licensing agency for the current checklist. citeturn0search0turn0search2
