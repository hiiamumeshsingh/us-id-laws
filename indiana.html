Indiana ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Indiana's Bureau of Motor Vehicles issues driver's licenses, learner's permits and identification cards. This guide explains Indiana ID requirements, REAL ID documents, residency, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and rules for people with temporary or permanent lawful status.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Indiana's current BMV process combines identity, lawful-status, Social Security and residency verification into the initial credential application. Once the required REAL ID documentation has been established, many routine transactions do not require the complete document set again.

Indiana driver's license requirements

Indiana driver's license applicants must satisfy the BMV's identity, lawful-status, Social Security and residency requirements and complete the testing applicable to the license type.

Applicants age 16 or 17 who are working toward a driver's license must hold an Indiana learner's permit for at least 180 days and provide a completed supervised-driving practice log. Applicants age 18 or older do not have the 180-day learner's-permit holding period and do not have to complete the supervised-driving log.

Identity and documentation

When applying for a new Indiana driver's license, the BMV requires original versions or certified copies of the required documents. The basic categories are one identity document, one lawful-status document, one Social Security document and two Indiana residency documents.

Foreign nationals

Indiana licenses issued to foreign nationals expire on the same date as the person's authorization to lawfully remain in the United States. Foreign nationals must visit a BMV branch for each renewal and provide the required identity, lawful-status, Social Security or ineligibility and Indiana residency documentation.

Indiana State ID without a driver's license

Indiana identification cards may be issued to an Indiana resident of any age who does not have a driver's license. A person cannot hold both an Indiana identification card and a driver's license at the same time, even if the driver's license was issued by another state.

For a new ID card, the BMV requires original or certified copies of documents proving identity, lawful status, Social Security number and Indiana residency.

Free Indiana ID for voting

Indiana can issue a free identification card to eligible U.S. citizens who are at least 18 and eligible to vote, provided they do not have an Indiana driver's license and supply the required documentation. The BMV also prioritizes ID-card transactions around Election Day.

The standard identification-card fee is currently $9 for an initial, renewal, amended or replacement ID. The fee does not apply to eligible applicants receiving the free voting ID.

Documents needed for an Indiana ID

Indiana's BMV requires four basic categories of documents for a new license, permit or identification card.

Identity

Applicants must provide one acceptable document proving identity. The BMV's documentation list includes qualifying passports, birth records, permanent resident documents, employment authorization documents and other approved identity records depending on the applicant's status.

Lawful status

Applicants must establish lawful status in the United States. U.S. citizens can use qualifying citizenship documents. Non-U.S. citizens must provide immigration documents showing the status recognized by the BMV.

Social Security number

Applicants must provide one document proving their Social Security number. People who are not eligible for an SSN must follow the BMV's alternative documentation process for proof of ineligibility.

Indiana residency

Two documents proving Indiana residency are required. The documents must satisfy the BMV's current rules for name, address and date or source of the document.

Proof of Indiana residency

Indiana requires two printed documents proving Indiana residency for a new driver's license, permit or identification card.

Examples include a computer-generated utility, credit-card, doctor or hospital bill issued within 60 days, a bank statement, a pre-printed pay stub, a Medicaid or Medicare statement and other documents on the BMV's approved list.

Indiana Residency Affidavit

Applicants who cannot provide the two required residency documents may qualify to use an Indiana Residency Affidavit. Indiana lists specific situations, including minors, adults living with another person, incapacitated applicants, people living in group residential facilities, certain homeless applicants, people residing in a motor vehicle and rural-route residents.

The person signing the affidavit must provide the supporting documents required for the particular situation. A residency affidavit is not a general substitute available to every applicant.

Indiana REAL ID requirements

Indiana requires new license, permit and identification-card applicants to provide documentation establishing identity, lawful status, Social Security number and Indiana residency. When those requirements are satisfied, the BMV issues a REAL ID-compliant credential.

Indiana's REAL ID-compliant credential is generally used for federal identification purposes covered by the REAL ID Act. A credential marked "Not for Real ID Purposes" is not accepted for those federal purposes, although Indiana says it can still be used to drive and vote.

Two residency documents

The Indiana REAL ID process requires two printed documents proving Indiana residency. Common examples include a recent utility or medical bill, bank statement, pre-printed pay stub or benefit statement.

Subsequent transactions

Indiana says REAL ID documentation is generally not required again on subsequent transactions unless the applicant has temporary or permanent resident status. Foreign nationals with permanent or temporary lawful status must provide the required documentation at each renewal.

Indiana voter identification requirements

Indiana law requires voters to present government-issued photo identification before casting a ballot at the polls on Election Day. The Indiana Secretary of State identifies four requirements for an acceptable voting ID:

  • The ID must display the voter's photograph.
  • The name must conform to the name on the voter registration record.
  • The ID must display an expiration date and be current or have expired after the date of the most recent general election, with specific exceptions for certain military, veteran, tribal and government-issued IDs.
  • The ID must be issued by the State of Indiana or the U.S. government.

In most cases, an Indiana driver's license, Indiana photo ID card, military or veteran ID or U.S. passport satisfies the state's photo-ID requirements.

College IDs

Indiana changed its voting-ID rules effective July 1, 2025. A document issued by an educational institution is no longer accepted as proof of identification for this purpose.

If you do not have acceptable ID

A voter who cannot or does not present an ID meeting the requirements may cast a provisional ballot. The voter generally has until noon 10 days after the election to provide the required documentation or affirm that a statutory exemption applies.

Renewing an Indiana ID or driver's license

Many Indiana residents can renew a driver's license or identification card online or at a BMV Connect kiosk. Indiana generally allows eligible residents to renew online every other renewal, meaning a branch visit may only be needed once every 10 to 12 years.

Indiana identification cards can generally be renewed online when the applicant is a U.S. citizen and Indiana resident, there is no change to the name or address, the ID is not more than 180 days expired, the applicant is within 24 months of the renewal date and the previous photo is on file.

For driver's licenses, additional online-renewal restrictions apply. Drivers age 75 and older generally must renew at a branch, although Indiana provides a limited online or kiosk option for qualifying applicants who recently submitted a professional vision screening.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Indiana ID

Indiana allows eligible residents to replace an identification card online, at a BMV Connect kiosk or at a branch. Customers with lawful temporary or permanent status must visit a branch for a replacement ID.

A replacement driver's license can also be handled through eligible online, kiosk or branch transactions. If personal information has changed, supporting documents may be required and the applicant may need to visit a branch.

Indiana's BMV also offers an optional Express Credential service for $25 in addition to the normal credential fee. The service prioritizes credential processing and mailing but does not guarantee next-day delivery.

Name and address changes

If your legal name changes, Indiana requires documentation connecting the previous name with the new name. The BMV requires branch processing when a name or residential address needs to be changed as part of a renewal.

Applicants should keep their residential address current with the BMV. A new credential may be needed when the address printed on the credential changes, depending on the transaction.

Foreign nationals must continue to meet the lawful-presence documentation requirements when amending or renewing their credentials.

Indiana IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Indiana issues driver's licenses and identification cards to eligible foreign nationals who can establish identity, lawful status, Social Security information or ineligibility and Indiana residency.

Credentials issued to foreign nationals expire on the date their authorization to lawfully remain in the United States expires. This means the expiration date can be different from the standard period that applies to other Indiana residents.

At each renewal, foreign nationals with permanent or temporary lawful status must visit a BMV branch and provide documentation of identity, lawful status, Social Security number or proof of ineligibility and Indiana residency.

People without an SSN

Indiana has a documentation process for applicants who are not eligible for a Social Security number. The applicant must provide the proof of ineligibility required by the BMV rather than simply leaving the Social Security requirement blank.

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

Indiana treats a government-issued driver's license or identification card as an identity credential tied to the person to whom it was issued. A counterfeit document, an altered credential or another person's genuine credential does not become valid simply because the card itself was originally issued by the BMV.

Indiana ID law FAQs

Can I get an Indiana ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Indiana identification cards are available to residents of any age who do not have a driver's license. You cannot hold an Indiana ID and a driver's license at the same time.

How many residency documents do I need for an Indiana ID?

Two documents proving Indiana residency are required for a new license, permit or identification card. An Indiana Residency Affidavit may be available to applicants who meet specific eligibility conditions.

Does Indiana require photo ID to vote?

Yes. Indiana requires government-issued photo identification at the polls, subject to the state's specific requirements and statutory exceptions.

Can I get a free Indiana ID for voting?

Yes. Eligible U.S. citizens of voting age who do not have an Indiana driver's license can receive a free state-issued ID for voting purposes when they provide the required documentation.

Is an Indiana college ID accepted for voting?

No. Effective July 1, 2025, an identification document issued by an educational institution is no longer accepted as proof of identification for Indiana voting purposes.

Can non-U.S. citizens get an Indiana ID?

Yes, eligible foreign nationals can receive Indiana credentials when they establish identity, lawful status, Social Security information or ineligibility and Indiana residency.

Can I renew my Indiana ID online?

Many U.S. citizen Indiana residents can renew online if they meet the BMV's eligibility rules. Foreign nationals with temporary or permanent lawful status must renew at a branch.
