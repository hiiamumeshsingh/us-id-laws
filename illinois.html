Illinois ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Illinois issues driver's licenses and state identification cards through the Secretary of State's Driver Services facilities. This guide explains Illinois ID requirements, REAL ID documents, residency, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and rules for non-U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

Illinois uses a document-group system rather than one single universal checklist. The documents required depend on whether the transaction is a first-time application, REAL ID application, renewal, correction or duplicate.

Illinois driver's license requirements

To obtain an Illinois driver's license, applicants must establish the information required by the Secretary of State, including legal name, date of birth, Social Security number when applicable, Illinois residency and written signature.

First-time applicants must visit a Driver Services facility and complete the testing required for the license class. Depending on the applicant and transaction, this can include vision screening, written testing and a road test.

New Illinois residents

Drivers moving to Illinois can use a valid driver's license from their previous state or country for up to 90 days while becoming established as an Illinois resident. Once applying for an Illinois credential, the applicant must surrender the out-of-state license or ID.

Illinois does not recognize an international driving permit as a substitute for an Illinois driver's license. The Secretary of State says a person who is temporarily in the United States and authorized to remain may drive with a valid license issued by their home country, subject to the applicable rules.

Restricted local license

Illinois also has a restricted local driver's license for qualified drivers who live in areas with populations of 3,500 or less and need to drive only within a limited area. This is a specialized credential and is separate from a standard Illinois driver's license.

Illinois State ID without a driver's license

Illinois issues state identification cards separately from driver's licenses. There is no minimum age for a state ID card.

A standard Illinois State ID is generally valid for five years. Illinois residents age 65 and older can apply for a free, non-expiring State ID. Illinois also provides a free 10-year State ID for qualifying people with disabilities when the required disability form is completed.

A state ID application uses the same general identity-document system as a driver's license application. The applicant must provide the documents required for the particular transaction.

Documents needed for an Illinois ID

Illinois groups acceptable documents into four categories. One document can sometimes satisfy more than one group, but the exact number depends on the transaction.

Group A — written signature

Group A establishes a written signature. Examples include a canceled check, qualifying consular card, tuition invoice or official university mail and certain utility bills.

Group B — date of birth

Group B establishes date of birth and can also establish lawful status when the document is marked as acceptable for that purpose. Examples include a certified birth certificate, U.S. passport, naturalization certificate, permanent resident card, employment authorization document and certain USCIS records.

Group C — Social Security number

Group C documents must contain the applicant's name and full Social Security number. Examples include a Social Security card, W-2, qualifying 1099, pay stub or verified Illinois DMV record.

Group D — Illinois residency

Group D documents must contain the applicant's full name and Illinois residence address. Examples include a bank statement, lease, mortgage, insurance policy, utility bill, pay stub, government mail, school records and certain medical or financial statements.

Illinois does not accept photocopies for these identification-document transactions. Hard-copy documents must meet the Secretary of State's current validity and timeframe rules.

Proof of Illinois residency

For a standard first-time Illinois driver's license or ID, applicants generally provide one Group D document. For a first-time REAL ID, two residency documents are required.

The documents must show the applicant's full name and residence address. Illinois accepts a range of records, including bank statements, canceled checks, credit-card statements, lease or mortgage documents, insurance policies, utility bills, government mail and school records.

Printed electronic documents can be used for REAL ID residency when they meet the state's requirements. Account numbers must be visible where required by the checklist.

Moving within Illinois

Illinois residents who move within the state must notify the Secretary of State of the new address within 10 days. The address can be updated online, although proof of the new address will be needed when the license or ID is later renewed if the address has changed.

Illinois REAL ID requirements

An Illinois REAL ID is a driver's license or identification card with a star in the upper-right corner. REAL ID is optional for driving, voting and ordinary identification purposes. It becomes relevant for covered federal purposes such as boarding domestic flights or entering certain secure federal facilities.

First-time Illinois REAL ID: Bring one document proving identity, date of birth and U.S. citizenship or lawful status, one document establishing the full Social Security number when required, two documents proving Illinois residency and one document establishing written signature.

Original documents matter

Illinois requires original hard-copy documents for REAL ID verification. Photocopies are not accepted, and images displayed on a cellphone are not accepted as substitutes. A printed electronic statement can be used when it qualifies as an acceptable residency document.

Do you need a REAL ID?

You do not need a REAL ID simply to drive in Illinois, vote or identify yourself for ordinary state purposes. A valid U.S. passport and other federally accepted documents can also satisfy federal identification requirements.

Illinois voter identification rules

Illinois does not have a general requirement that every registered voter present photo identification at the polling place. However, identification can be required in specific situations, particularly for certain voters who registered by mail and are voting for the first time in an election jurisdiction.

Illinois also uses identification during voter registration. The state online registration system can use an Illinois driver's license or State ID together with the required Social Security information. People registering in person without those credentials can use other accepted forms of identification under the state's registration rules.

Provisional ballots

A provisional ballot can be used when a voter's registration or eligibility is questioned. One situation involves a voter who is required to show identification because of the way the voter registered but does not have the required ID at the polling place.

Because local election authorities handle polling-place procedures, voters should check their county election authority or the Illinois online voter registration system for current election information.

Renewing an Illinois ID or driver's license

Illinois offers online renewal for eligible driver's license and ID card holders who receive the required renewal authorization information. Not every transaction qualifies for online renewal.

A person who wants to obtain a REAL ID credential must visit a DMV facility because the REAL ID document-verification process requires the applicant to present the required documents.

Illinois also has age-based driver's license fees and testing requirements. Drivers age 79 and older must take a driving test when renewing their licenses, while other renewal requirements depend on the driver's record and transaction.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Illinois ID

Duplicate Illinois driver's licenses and State IDs can be obtained through a Driver Services facility, subject to the transaction requirements and current fee schedule.

Illinois provides fee exemptions in several situations. For example, duplicate driver's licenses or IDs can be free for certain older residents, active-duty military members and people whose credential was stolen when the required police report is provided.

If the replacement also involves a name, date of birth, Social Security number or gender change, Illinois requires documentation linking the previous information to the new information.

Name and address changes

When a legal name changes, Illinois requires documents connecting the previous name with the new legal name. The type of document depends on the reason for the change, such as marriage, divorce or a court order.

Illinois residents must notify the Secretary of State of an address change within 10 days. An address can be updated online, but proof of the new address may be required at the next renewal.

If a name, date of birth, Social Security number or gender is corrected on a credential, the Secretary of State requires documentation establishing the change.

Illinois IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Illinois provides driver's licenses and State IDs to eligible non-U.S. citizens who meet the applicable identity, residency and lawful-status requirements. Illinois changed its credential system so eligible non-U.S. citizens who previously used the Temporary Visitor Driver's License pathway can obtain a standard driver's license or State ID when they meet the current requirements.

Social Security numbers

Applicants who have a Social Security number generally provide the required Group C documentation. Illinois also has specific procedures for applicants who are ineligible for an SSN. A person applying for a qualifying VISA or NONVISA credential does not present Group C documents and instead signs a declaration stating that the person is ineligible to obtain an SSN.

Temporary lawful presence

People with temporary lawful status must provide documentation establishing their lawful status. The expiration of a credential can depend on the applicant's authorized period of stay and the specific credential type.

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

Illinois law specifically addresses unlawful use of driver's licenses and permits, fictitious or unlawfully altered credentials and fraudulent driver's licenses or permits. Displaying another person's license as your own, lending a license for another person's use and knowingly possessing certain fraudulent credentials can result in criminal or administrative consequences.

Under Illinois Vehicle Code Section 6-301, a violation of the unlawful-use provisions is generally a Class A misdemeanor with a minimum fine of $500 or 50 hours of community service, while a second or subsequent violation is a Class 4 felony. Illinois ID law FAQs

Can I get an Illinois State ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Illinois issues State IDs separately from driver's licenses and there is no minimum age for a State ID.

How many documents do I need for an Illinois REAL ID?

For a first-time REAL ID, Illinois generally requires one document from Groups A, B and C plus two documents proving Illinois residency from Group D.

Do I need a REAL ID to drive in Illinois?

No. A REAL ID is optional for driving, voting and ordinary identification. It is relevant to federal purposes covered by the REAL ID rules.

Do Illinois voters need photo ID?

Illinois does not generally require every registered voter to show photo ID at the polls. Identification can be required in specific registration and first-time-voter situations.

Can people age 65 or older get a free Illinois ID?

Yes. Illinois residents age 65 and older can apply for a free, non-expiring State ID.

Can non-U.S. citizens get an Illinois driver's license?

Yes, eligible non-U.S. citizens can obtain Illinois credentials when they meet the applicable identity, residency and lawful-status requirements. Illinois has replaced the former TVDL pathway with standard credentials for eligible applicants.

How long can a new resident drive with an out-of-state license?

Illinois says drivers moving to the state may use a valid license from their previous state or country for up to 90 days while establishing Illinois residency, subject to the applicable rules.
