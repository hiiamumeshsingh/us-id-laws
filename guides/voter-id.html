ELECTION ID GUIDE

Voter ID Requirements

Understand how voter identification rules work, what documents may be accepted, what alternatives may exist and why the requirements can differ from one state to another.

Important
Voter ID rules are state-specific.

The type of identification required, whether an alternative is available and what happens if you cannot provide the requested ID depend on the jurisdiction.

Educational reference: Voting procedures and identification requirements can change. Always verify the current rule with your state's election authority before an election.

What is voter ID?

Voter ID refers to identification requirements that may apply when a person registers to vote, votes in person, or completes another part of the election process. The rules are established through state election law and procedures.

There is no single voter ID requirement that applies in exactly the same way across all U.S. states. A state may require a photo ID, accept other forms of documentation, use a signature or voter-record verification process, or provide a procedure for voters who cannot present the requested identification.

When might identification be required?

Registering to vote

Registration can involve identity and eligibility information. The documents or information requested depend on the state and registration method.

Voting in person

Some states require or request identification when a voter checks in at a polling place or voting center.

First-time voters

Federal or state rules can create additional documentation requirements for certain first-time voters, depending on how they registered and the circumstances.

Mail or absentee voting

Identification and verification rules for mailed ballots can differ from in-person voting procedures.

What types of ID may be accepted?

Where a state requires identification, the accepted documents are defined by that state's election authority or election law. Depending on the jurisdiction, examples can include government-issued photo identification or other documents used to establish identity.

Do not assume that every government-issued ID is automatically accepted. A document can be valid for another purpose but still fall outside a state's specific voter-ID list or conditions.

Check whether the state specifies an expiration period, address requirement, photograph requirement, name requirement or other condition for the particular document.

Photo ID and non-photo ID rules

States that require identification can structure their rules differently. Some may focus on photo identification while others may accept documentation that establishes identity without requiring a photograph.

The distinction matters because having a valid driver's license or state ID does not by itself tell you whether that credential satisfies the voting requirement in every state.

What if you do not have the required ID?

States can provide different procedures for voters who cannot present the requested identification. Depending on the jurisdiction, a voter may be able to complete an affidavit, sign a declaration, provide another form of documentation, vote provisionally or follow another verification process.

The exact procedure is important. Do not assume that being unable to show one particular document automatically means you cannot cast a ballot.

Check the rule before election day. The procedure for a voter without the requested ID can be different from the procedure for someone whose name or registration information cannot be confirmed.

Voter ID vs. registering to vote

Voter identification at the polling place is not the same issue as voter registration. Registration establishes whether a person is registered and eligible under the applicable rules. Election-day identification procedures determine how identity is verified when voting.

Some states may also require identification information during registration, while others use different verification methods. These processes should be checked separately.

Voter ID and REAL ID are different

REAL ID is a federal framework governing standards for certain state-issued driver's licenses and identification cards. Voter ID requirements are election rules established through state law and election procedures.

A REAL ID-compliant driver's license or state ID may be an accepted form of identification in a jurisdiction, but REAL ID status itself does not determine whether a state has a voter ID requirement.

Voter ID requirements for absentee and mail voting

Mail voting can involve different verification steps from voting in person. Depending on the state, a voter may need to provide identifying information on the ballot materials, match a signature or complete another verification process.

Because mail-ballot rules vary substantially, voters should use the instructions supplied with their ballot and the current guidance from their state or local election authority.

Address and name changes

Keeping voter registration information current can help avoid problems at check-in. If your name or address has changed, review the state's process for updating voter registration before an election.

A mismatch between voter-registration records and identification documents does not have the same consequences in every state, so the state's current procedure should be checked rather than assumed.

Students, older voters and other special circumstances

Some voters may have circumstances that affect which documents they have available. Students, older adults, people who recently moved, voters with disabilities, people experiencing homelessness and voters living temporarily away from home may need to review additional state-specific instructions.

The relevant election authority can explain what alternative documentation or procedures are available in the jurisdiction.

Do not confuse voter ID with novelty identification

Commercial novelty or replica identification products are not substitutes for identification accepted under election law. A state election authority determines which documents can be used for voting purposes.

FakeIDs.com, for example, describes its products as novelty identification cards and states that they are not government-issued identification. Such products should not be presented as voter identification or used in an attempt to satisfy an election requirement.

How to check your state's voter ID rule

Identify your state

Election rules are state-specific, so begin with the state where you are registered or intend to register.

Check the election authority

Use the state's official election website or the relevant local election office for the current identification requirements.

Review the accepted documents

Confirm exactly which IDs or alternative documents are accepted and whether expiration, address or name conditions apply.

Check the fallback procedure

If you do not have the listed ID, find out what the state says you should do before arriving at the polling place.

Confirm the timing

Check registration deadlines, early voting rules, polling-place information and any election-specific instructions.

Common voter ID mistakes

  • Assuming every state uses the same voter ID rule
  • Assuming a driver's license is always required
  • Assuming every government-issued photo ID is accepted
  • Ignoring expiration or document-condition requirements
  • Confusing REAL ID requirements with voter ID requirements
  • Waiting until election day to discover that a document or registration record needs attention
  • Assuming the procedure for a missing ID is the same in every state
  • Relying on old election information when rules may have changed

Voter ID checklist

Before voting, verify:
  • Whether your state requires identification for your voting method
  • Which documents are accepted
  • Whether the document must be current or meet an expiration rule
  • Whether your name and address need to match voter records
  • What alternative procedure applies if you do not have the requested ID
  • Whether early voting or mail voting uses different verification rules
  • Your registration status and polling-place information
  • Any election-specific instructions from the official election authority

Frequently asked questions

Does every state require voter ID?

No single rule applies identically across all states. Identification requirements and verification procedures are determined by the applicable state election rules.

Can I vote without a driver's license?

Whether a driver's license is required depends on the state's accepted identification and voting procedure. A driver's license is not automatically the only possible form of identification.

Is a state ID accepted for voting?

That depends on the state and the particular identification requirement. Check the state's official list of accepted documents.

What happens if I forget my ID?

The procedure varies by state. Some jurisdictions provide an alternative verification process, while others may use provisional voting or another procedure.

Does REAL ID affect whether I can vote?

REAL ID and voter ID are separate frameworks. REAL ID concerns standards for certain state-issued credentials, while voter ID rules concern election procedures.

Are mail ballots subject to voter ID rules?

Mail voting can use different identity-verification procedures from in-person voting. Follow the instructions supplied by the election authority for the ballot.

Where can I verify the current rule?

The state or local election authority is the appropriate source for current voter identification requirements, accepted documents and election procedures.

Find your state's voter ID information

Voter identification requirements are state-specific. Use the state directory to find your jurisdiction, then verify the current rule with the official election authority.
