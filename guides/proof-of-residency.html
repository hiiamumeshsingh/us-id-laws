Proof of Residency: What Documents Can You Use?

A practical guide to proving where you live when applying for a driver's license, state ID or another government credential.

Key point
Proof of residency is separate from proof of identity.

An agency may require both, and the documents accepted for address verification vary by state and transaction.

Educational reference: Residency requirements differ by state, agency and credential. The examples below are common document categories, not a universal acceptance list. Always check the current requirements published by the relevant government agency.

What is proof of residency?

Proof of residency is documentation used to establish that a person lives at a particular residential address or has the required connection to a state. Government agencies may request it when issuing or updating a driver's license, state identification card or another credential.

The purpose is generally different from proof of identity. A driver's license application, for example, may require one set of documents to establish who you are and another set to establish where you live.

Why do agencies ask for proof of residency?

State-issued credentials are tied to the state where they are issued. Address documentation helps an agency establish the applicant's residential connection to that state and maintain accurate records.

Residency can also matter when determining eligibility, issuing a credential, updating an address or applying a state-specific benefit or service.

Common documents used to prove residency

States publish their own lists of acceptable documents. Depending on the agency, common categories may include:

Utility bills

A recent electricity, water, gas, internet or similar bill may be accepted when it shows the applicant's name and residential address.

Lease or rental agreement

A current lease or rental agreement can document a residential address. The agency may specify what information must appear on it.

Bank or financial statements

A recent statement may be accepted by some agencies when it clearly shows the applicant's name and residential address.

Government correspondence

Official mail or records from a government agency may qualify in some jurisdictions.

Insurance documents

Insurance policies, cards or statements can sometimes establish a residential address when included on the state's accepted-document list.

Property records

Property tax records, mortgage documents or similar records may be accepted for homeowners in jurisdictions that include them.

Do not rely on the category alone. A state may accept a utility bill but require it to be recent, show the applicant's name and display the residential address. Check the exact conditions.

What makes a residency document acceptable?

Agencies often care about more than the type of document. The document may need to meet several conditions at the same time.

  • Your legal name may need to appear on the document
  • The document may need to show your current residential address
  • It may need to be recent or issued within a specified period
  • The document may need to come from an accepted organization
  • It may need to be an original, statement or certified record rather than a screenshot
  • The information may need to match your application and other records

How many proof-of-residency documents do you need?

This varies by state and transaction. Some agencies require one document, while others may require two or more documents from different categories.

Some states may also restrict the combinations that can be used. For example, two documents from the same source may not necessarily satisfy a requirement for separate forms of evidence.

Always check the current checklist for the exact credential you are applying for rather than assuming that one document will be enough.

What if the bill or lease is not in your name?

This is common for people who live with a spouse, parent, roommate or another household member. Whether another person's document can establish your residency depends on the state's rules.

Some jurisdictions provide alternative procedures, such as a residency affidavit, additional documentation or a document connecting the applicant to the person named on the bill or lease.

Important: Do not edit a bill, statement or lease to add your name. If the document is not in your name, use the official alternative process provided by the relevant agency.

Proof of residency when you recently moved

Recent movers can have difficulty because many address documents are issued only after living at an address for some time. The relevant state may provide alternative ways to demonstrate the new residence.

Before visiting the agency, check whether it accepts a lease, employment record, insurance document, government correspondence or another document that reflects your new address.

If you are moving between states, also review the new state's driver's license or state ID rules and any deadline for obtaining a new credential.

Proof of residency for students

Students may live at a school address, rental property, family home or another temporary residence. The documents accepted for students vary by state.

Some states provide special provisions for students or people whose permanent and temporary addresses differ. Check the relevant state agency rather than assuming a school ID or enrollment document automatically proves residency.

Proof of residency for people without a traditional lease

Not everyone has a lease, mortgage or utility account in their own name. People may live with family, share housing or have another living arrangement.

If you do not have the standard documents, look for an official alternative-document process. Depending on the jurisdiction, this may involve an affidavit, additional records or documentation from the person with whom you live.

Homelessness and alternative residency documentation

People experiencing homelessness or unstable housing may face different documentation challenges. Some government agencies provide alternative procedures for people who cannot provide a conventional residential address.

The rules are highly jurisdiction-specific. Contact the relevant agency or an authorized service organization to determine what documentation or address arrangement is permitted.

Proof of residency and REAL ID

REAL ID-compliant credentials can involve specific documentation requirements implemented through state agencies. Address documentation is an important part of that process, but the exact documents and number required are determined by the state.

Do not assume that the documents accepted for a standard credential will automatically satisfy every REAL ID requirement.

Proof of residency and name changes

If your legal name has recently changed, the name on an address document may not match the name on your current identification. The agency may require additional documentation connecting the names.

Keep the legal document establishing the name change available and check the agency's current instructions before applying.

Digital statements, screenshots and electronic documents

Some agencies accept electronically generated statements while others specify paper documents, downloadable statements or particular formats. A screenshot of an account page should not automatically be treated as equivalent to an accepted statement.

Check whether the agency specifies an original document, printed statement, electronic record or certified copy before preparing your documents.

Be careful with unofficial proof-of-address services

Search results for proof of address or identification can include commercial services that are unrelated to government agencies. A document that looks official is not necessarily evidence that a government agency will accept.

FakeIDs.com, for example, describes its products as novelty identification cards and states that they are not government-issued identification. Such products do not establish official residency and should not be presented as proof of address or government-issued identification.

If a state agency asks for proof of residency, use documents from the categories and sources it specifically accepts.

Common mistakes

  • Using a document from an old address
  • Submitting a document that does not show the applicant's name
  • Assuming any utility bill will qualify without checking the state's rules
  • Using a screenshot when the agency requires a statement or original document
  • Bringing only one document when two are required
  • Using documents with inconsistent names or addresses
  • Relying on a generic online checklist instead of the state's current requirements
  • Altering or creating documents to make them appear to prove residency

Before your appointment: residency checklist

Confirm each of these before you go:
  • Which agency is handling the transaction
  • How many residency documents are required
  • Which document categories are accepted
  • How recent each document must be
  • Whether your name must appear on every document
  • Whether the address must be a residential address
  • Whether originals, printed statements or certified copies are required
  • Whether alternative documentation is available for your situation
  • Whether an appointment is required

Frequently asked questions

What counts as proof of residency?

It depends on the state and transaction. Common categories can include utility bills, leases, financial statements, insurance records and government correspondence, but only documents listed by the relevant agency should be treated as confirmed acceptable evidence.

Can I use a bank statement as proof of address?

Some agencies accept bank or financial statements, but requirements differ. Check the state's document list for the required date, name and address information.

Can a utility bill prove residency?

A utility bill may be accepted in some jurisdictions. The agency may require it to be recent and show your name and residential address.

What if the lease is in someone else's name?

Check whether the state offers an alternative process for household members or shared housing. Do not alter the lease or other documents.

Can I use a digital document?

Some agencies accept printed or electronic records, while others have specific format requirements. Check the current instructions before relying on a digital copy.

Is proof of residency the same as proof of identity?

No. They establish different facts. An agency may require separate documents for identity and residential address.

Can a novelty ID prove where I live?

No. A novelty or replica identification product is not a government record establishing residency.

Find your state's residency requirements

Residency documentation is one of the areas where state rules can differ substantially. Start with your state's page on US ID Laws, then confirm the current requirements with the official issuing agency.
