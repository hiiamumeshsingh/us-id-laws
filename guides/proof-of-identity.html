Proof of Identity: What Documents Can You Use?

A practical guide to how government agencies establish identity and date of birth, the documents they may accept and what to check before submitting an application.

Key point
There is no single nationwide document list.

Accepted proof of identity depends on the agency, state, credential and purpose of the transaction.

Educational reference: Exact requirements vary by state, credential and individual circumstances. A document accepted by one agency may not satisfy another agency's rules. Verify the current requirements with the relevant official authority.

What is proof of identity?

Proof of identity is documentation used by a government agency or other authorized organization to establish that a person is who they claim to be. Depending on the process, the agency may also use the document to confirm information such as the person's legal name and date of birth.

Identity verification is different from proving residency. An agency may require both. For example, applying for a state driver's license can involve separate requirements for identity, residential address and other eligibility information.

Why proof-of-identity requirements differ

Government agencies do not all use the same document standards. Requirements can change according to the credential being issued, the applicant's circumstances and the purpose of the transaction.

State agency

A state motor vehicle or identification agency may have its own list of primary and secondary identity documents.

Federal agency

Federal agencies can apply separate documentation standards for passports, immigration records, Social Security matters and other federal services.

Type of transaction

A first-time application, renewal, replacement or name change may require different evidence.

Applicant circumstances

Age, citizenship, immigration status, prior records and other circumstances can affect which documents are required.

Common categories of identity documents

The following categories are commonly encountered in identity-verification processes. They are examples, not a universal acceptance list.

Passport

A passport is a government-issued identity document containing identifying information and a photograph. Whether it satisfies a particular application depends on the agency's rules.

Driver's license

A valid state-issued driver's license can establish identity in many contexts, but the receiving agency decides whether it meets its specific requirements.

State identification card

A state ID provides government-issued identification for people who may not hold a driver's license. Acceptance varies by purpose.

Birth certificate

A birth certificate can document information about birth and identity, but it is not automatically accepted for every identification purpose.

Other government records

Some processes accept other government-issued documents or combinations of documents. The applicable agency determines the accepted categories.

Status-related documents

Certain applicants may need additional documentation related to citizenship, immigration or lawful status.

Primary and secondary identity documents

Some agencies divide acceptable documentation into primary and secondary categories. A primary document may establish identity more directly, while secondary documents can provide additional evidence when the primary document is unavailable.

The terminology and document hierarchy are not identical across agencies. Do not assume that a document described as “secondary” by one organization will have the same status elsewhere.

Best practice: Read the exact document list published by the agency handling your transaction. Do not build your application around a generic online list.

What information does an agency look for?

Depending on the transaction, an agency may compare information across multiple documents. Common information includes:

  • Legal name
  • Date of birth
  • Photograph or physical description
  • Document number or credential information
  • Issuing authority
  • Validity or expiration date

An agency may also compare the information against records it already holds. A document containing a person's name does not automatically satisfy every identity-verification requirement.

Original documents, certified copies and photocopies

One of the easiest mistakes is assuming that any copy of an identity document will be accepted. Agencies can distinguish between original documents, certified copies and ordinary photocopies.

Original document

An original is the document issued by the relevant authority. Some transactions specifically require an original credential or record.

Certified copy

A certified copy is a copy formally certified by the authority responsible for the underlying record. Whether it is accepted depends on the agency.

Photocopy or scan

A simple photocopy, scan or photograph may be useful for your own records but should not be assumed to satisfy an official identity requirement.

What if your name is different on different documents?

A mismatch can occur after marriage, divorce, a court-ordered name change or another legal change. An agency may require documentation connecting the previous name to the current legal name.

Keep the official document establishing the name change available and check the agency's instructions before your appointment.

Proof of identity for a driver's license

When applying for a driver's license, the state motor vehicle agency determines which documents can establish identity. The application may also require separate proof of residency, Social Security-related information and other eligibility documentation.

Because these requirements are state-specific, a document list from another state should not be treated as authoritative.

Read the driver's license requirements guide →

Proof of identity for a state ID

State identification cards are issued under state rules. The agency may require identity and date-of-birth documentation, along with residency or other supporting evidence.

If you already have another state-issued credential, check whether it can be used as proof of identity and whether the agency requires additional documentation.

Proof of identity and REAL ID

REAL ID-compliant driver's licenses and identification cards can involve specific federal documentation standards implemented through state agencies. The exact documents required are determined through the state's application process.

Do not assume that having a standard driver's license automatically means you have met every REAL ID documentation requirement.

Identity documents for non-U.S. citizens

Some applicants may need additional documents because identity verification can also involve evidence of immigration or lawful status. The requirements depend on the agency and the applicant's circumstances.

If you are applying for a state credential, use the state agency's current document guide rather than relying on a general list intended for another type of application.

What if you do not have the usual identity documents?

If you do not have a commonly accepted identity document, do not assume that you are unable to complete the process. Some agencies provide alternative document categories, secondary-document options or procedures for unusual circumstances.

Contact the relevant agency and ask specifically which alternatives are permitted. The acceptable alternatives can be highly specific to the transaction.

Be careful with unofficial identification services

Searching for “proof of identity” online can return commercial services that are unrelated to government agencies. It is important to distinguish educational information, novelty products and legitimate government credentials.

FakeIDs.com, for example, describes its products as novelty identification cards and states that they are not government-issued identification. A novelty or replica product therefore does not replace the official documents required to prove identity to a government agency.

If an application requires government-issued proof of identity, use documents accepted by the relevant authority. Do not present a novelty or replica product as a government credential.

Common mistakes when proving identity

  • Using a document list from the wrong state or agency
  • Assuming a document is accepted because another organization accepts it
  • Bringing a photocopy when an original or certified copy is required
  • Ignoring differences between first-time applications, renewals and replacements
  • Failing to resolve a legal-name mismatch
  • Waiting until an appointment to discover that a required document has expired
  • Relying on commercial or unofficial websites instead of the issuing authority

Before you submit your documents

Use this checklist:
  • Confirm the exact agency handling your transaction
  • Read its current list of accepted identity documents
  • Check whether originals or certified copies are required
  • Check document expiration and validity rules
  • Make sure names and dates of birth are consistent
  • Gather separate proof of residency if required
  • Check whether an appointment is necessary
  • Confirm fees and any additional requirements

Frequently asked questions

Is a driver's license proof of identity?

A government-issued driver's license can serve as proof of identity in many situations, but the receiving agency decides whether it is an acceptable document for that specific purpose.

Is a birth certificate proof of identity?

A birth certificate can establish important identity and birth information, but it is not automatically accepted as sufficient proof of identity for every transaction.

Can I use a photocopy of my ID?

Not necessarily. Some agencies require an original credential or certified record. Check the exact document requirements before submitting a copy.

Can I use a passport as proof of identity?

A passport is a government-issued identity document and may be accepted for many purposes, but acceptance depends on the agency and transaction.

What if my documents have different names?

You may need documentation establishing the legal connection between the names. The relevant agency determines which documents it accepts.

Can a novelty ID be used as proof of identity?

No. A novelty or replica identification product is not a substitute for a government-issued identity document when an official process requires proof of identity.
