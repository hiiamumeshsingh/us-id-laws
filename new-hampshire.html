New Hampshire ID Laws: What You Need to Know

New Hampshire issues driver's licenses and non-driver identification cards through the Division of Motor Vehicles. This guide covers identity documents, residency, REAL ID, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and rules for non-U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

New Hampshire driver's license requirements

New Hampshire's standard driver's license is an operator license. Applicants must establish the required identity and address information and complete the licensing requirements that apply to their age and license type. The DMV application specifically distinguishes original licenses, renewals, transfers, motorcycle endorsements and REAL ID applications. citeturn1search2

Identity

New Hampshire's document checklist accepts primary identity documents such as a certified U.S. birth certificate, Certificate of Naturalization or Citizenship, valid U.S. passport, military identification and qualifying permanent-resident, asylee or refugee documentation. A second identity document can include a Social Security card, W-2, 1099, qualifying prior license, government employee ID, marriage certificate or other listed document. citeturn1search0

New Hampshire residents moving from another state

When transferring from another state, the applicant must relinquish the out-of-state driver's license. New Hampshire residents who drive in the state must obtain a New Hampshire license within 60 days after establishing domicile/residence. citeturn1search0turn1search1

Teen drivers

Applicants under 18 have additional requirements, including a driver education certificate, parental authorization certificate and certification of additional supervised driving when applicable. citeturn1search0

New Hampshire non-driver identification cards

New Hampshire offers non-driver identification cards to residents who need official identification without a driver's license. The current DMV application lists the non-driver ID fee at $10. citeturn1search2

Voting-only identification

New Hampshire also provides a photo identification card specifically for voting purposes at no charge. A voter without an approved photo ID can obtain a voucher from a local town or city clerk or the Secretary of State and present it to the DMV. citeturn0search1

REAL ID and non-driver IDs

A New Hampshire non-driver ID can be issued as a REAL ID-compliant credential when the applicant completes the federal document requirements. The REAL ID option is separate from the free voting-only photo ID.

Documents needed for a New Hampshire ID

New Hampshire's standard non-REAL-ID checklist generally requires one or two primary identity documents, or one primary plus an additional identity document, along with proof of the New Hampshire legal address. The REAL ID checklist has additional federal-document requirements. citeturn1search0turn0search6

Identity documents

Examples of primary identity documents include a certified birth certificate, U.S. passport, Certificate of Naturalization or Citizenship, military ID and qualifying immigration documents. Secondary evidence can include a Social Security card, W-2, paystub with full SSN, marriage certificate, divorce decree, student ID or certain government documents. citeturn1search0

Social Security information

The DMV application requires Social Security information for an original New Hampshire driver's license or REAL ID. The REAL ID checklist accepts a Social Security card, paystub with the full SSN, W-2 or 1099 showing the full SSN. citeturn1search2turn0search6

Name changes

Documents used for identity and residency should reflect the applicant's current full legal name. If they do not, New Hampshire requires evidence of the legal name change, such as a marriage or civil-union certificate, divorce decree or probate-court authorization. citeturn1search0

Proof of New Hampshire residency

New Hampshire requires proof of the legal address listed on the application. The DMV's non-REAL-ID checklist includes a broad range of acceptable documents, such as a valid New Hampshire vehicle registration, signed lease or rental agreement, deed, current property-tax bill, mortgage statement, utility bill, government document, government check or qualifying payroll document. citeturn1search0

REAL ID residency

The REAL ID checklist requires two items showing proof of New Hampshire residency for the legal address on the application. The documents must satisfy the DMV's date and address requirements. citeturn0search6

Domicile and residence

New Hampshire says domicile and residence are equivalent for these purposes. A person establishes domicile by making a New Hampshire town or ward their principal place of physical presence, with actions such as leasing a primary home, obtaining resident vehicle registration, registering to vote or paying resident-only taxes providing evidence of intent. citeturn1search1

New Hampshire REAL ID requirements

New Hampshire offers REAL ID-compliant driver's licenses and non-driver IDs. REAL ID is optional for state purposes, but a compliant credential or another TSA-accepted document is required for covered federal purposes such as domestic commercial air travel.

What you need for REAL ID

  • Proof of identity and lawful status/citizenship as required by the federal checklist
  • One document showing Social Security number
  • Two documents showing New Hampshire residency at the legal address
  • Legal name-change documents when applicable

New Hampshire's REAL ID checklist emphasizes that the identity and residency documents should reflect the applicant's current name. If names differ, the applicant must provide the chain of name-change documents. citeturn0search6

REAL ID is not proof of U.S. citizenship for voting. New Hampshire's Secretary of State specifically states that a New Hampshire REAL ID-compliant driver's license is not proof of U.S. citizenship. Citizenship must be established separately when registering to vote. citeturn0search7

New Hampshire voter identification requirements

New Hampshire requires all registered voters to show proof of identification at the polling place before receiving a ballot. The state accepts a driver's license issued by any state or federal government, a non-driver ID from New Hampshire or another state, a New Hampshire voting-only photo ID, U.S. armed-services ID, U.S. passport/passcard or verification of identity by an election official. citeturn0search1

Expired IDs

An acceptable photo ID remains valid for voting for five years beyond its expiration date. Voters age 65 or older can use an acceptable photo ID without regard to its expiration date. The name on the ID must substantially conform to the name on the checklist. citeturn0search1

Student IDs

New Hampshire does not accept student IDs as proof of identity for voting. Voters can instead use one of the other accepted forms of identification listed by the Secretary of State. citeturn0search1

Free voting-only photo ID

Voters who do not have an approved photo ID may obtain a photo identification card for voting purposes only, free of charge, from the DMV. The voter first obtains a voucher from a town or city clerk or the Secretary of State. citeturn0search1

Registering to vote

Voter registration is separate from presenting ID at the polls. To register, a person must be a U.S. citizen, be at least 18 on Election Day and be domiciled in the town or ward where they seek to vote. New Hampshire permits Election Day voter registration and does not impose a minimum period of residence before registering. citeturn0search2

Renewing a New Hampshire driver's license or ID

New Hampshire driver's licenses expire on the applicant's birthday every five years. The DMV mails renewal reminders to the last known mailing address. citeturn0search8

Renewal documents

For a standard renewal, the current valid New Hampshire license is generally sufficient. A New Hampshire license that has not been expired for more than one year can also be used under the DMV's renewal rules. If the license is unavailable and no photo or digital image is on file, an identity document from the DMV's primary-document list is required. citeturn1search0

REAL ID renewal

A REAL ID renewal can require the applicant to maintain the federal document requirements. Applicants should check the DMV's current REAL ID instructions if they are upgrading from a noncompliant credential or if their legal name has changed.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged New Hampshire ID

New Hampshire provides replacement driver's license and ID transactions through the DMV. When a current photo or digital image is already on file, the DMV's duplicate rules can reduce the amount of identity documentation required. If no photo or digital image is on file, an identity document from the primary list is required. citeturn1search0

Applicants should report a stolen credential when appropriate and request a replacement rather than continuing to use an old credential that may be compromised.

Name and address changes

New Hampshire requires an in-person DMV transaction for a legal name change. Supporting documentation must be presented. The DMV application lists marriage, civil-union and court documents among the records that can support a name change. citeturn1search3

Address changes

An address change can be reported through the DMV. If the applicant wants a replacement license or ID displaying the updated address, the DMV's current form lists a $3 fee for the replacement address transaction. citeturn1search3

New Hampshire IDs for non-U.S. citizens

New Hampshire issues credentials to eligible non-U.S. citizens who can establish the identity, status and residency required by the DMV. The state provides separate instructions for permanent residents, temporary-status applicants and REAL ID applicants.

Permanent residents

Permanent residents can apply for a New Hampshire REAL ID when they provide the required identity, immigration-status, Social Security and residency documentation. Immigration documents are subject to DMV verification.

Voting restrictions

New Hampshire voter registration is limited to U.S. citizens. A driver's license or REAL ID does not itself establish citizenship for voter registration. The Secretary of State specifically notes that non-U.S. citizens may not register to vote or vote in New Hampshire elections. citeturn0search7turn0search2

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

A New Hampshire driver's license or non-driver ID is a government-issued credential tied to verified identity and residency information. A counterfeit or altered credential, or another person's genuine credential used as one's own, does not become valid identification for the person presenting it.

For general educational context about the legal distinction between counterfeit IDs, altered documents and borrowed genuine identification, see Legal Boundaries of Collectible IDs. The article discusses novelty and collectible cards rather than New Hampshire-specific law.

New Hampshire ID law FAQs

Does New Hampshire require REAL ID?

No. REAL ID is optional for state purposes, but a REAL ID-compliant credential or another TSA-accepted document is required for covered federal purposes such as domestic air travel.

How many residency documents are needed for a New Hampshire REAL ID?

Two documents showing the New Hampshire legal address are required under the DMV's REAL ID checklist.

How long do new residents have to get a New Hampshire license?

A person who establishes New Hampshire domicile/residence and drives in the state must obtain a New Hampshire driver's license within 60 days.

Does New Hampshire require photo ID to vote?

Yes. Registered voters must show proof of identification at the polling place. The state accepts several forms of photo ID and offers a free voting-only photo ID.

Can I use an expired ID to vote in New Hampshire?

An acceptable photo ID remains valid for voting for five years beyond its expiration date. Voters age 65 or older can use an acceptable photo ID without regard to expiration date.

Are student IDs accepted for voting in New Hampshire?

No. The Secretary of State says student IDs cannot be used to prove identity at the polls.

Can non-U.S. citizens get a New Hampshire driver's license?

Eligible non-U.S. citizens can receive New Hampshire credentials after meeting the DMV's identity, status and residency requirements. Voting eligibility is separate and limited to U.S. citizens.

How much does a New Hampshire driver's license cost?

The current DMV application lists $50 for an operator or limited-privilege license and $60 for a REAL ID transaction.
