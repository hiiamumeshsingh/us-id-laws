New Mexico ID Laws: What You Need to Know

New Mexico uses a two-track credential system that distinguishes standard credentials from REAL ID-compliant licenses and identification cards. This guide covers documents, residency, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and requirements for non-U.S. citizens.

Last reviewed: September 26, 2026

New Mexico driver's license requirements

Anyone driving a motor vehicle on New Mexico highways and roadways must be licensed. First-time applicants apply at an MVD Field Office and complete the testing that applies to their situation. citeturn0search0

Standard driver's license

For a Standard Driver's License, MVD requires one proof of identity and age plus two proofs of New Mexico residency. At least one identity or identification-number document must also show the applicant's date of birth. citeturn0search1

REAL ID driver's license

For a REAL ID driver's license, MVD requires one proof of identification number, one proof of identity and two proofs of New Mexico residency. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable lawful-status requirements. citeturn0search1

Testing

First-time applicants generally take an eye test. Written and road testing requirements depend on whether the applicant has a current out-of-state license, how long a previous license has been expired and whether the application is a first license. citeturn0search0

Drivers ages 18 to 24

A first-time licensee between 18 and 24 must provide a certificate showing completion of New Mexico's “None for the Road” DWI Awareness Class. citeturn0search1

New Mexico state identification cards

New Mexico MVD issues secure identification cards. Applicants must visit an MVD Field Office for an ID transaction and receive a temporary credential while the permanent card is processed and mailed. citeturn1search1

Standard ID

The non-compliant Standard ID requires one proof of identity and identification number plus two proofs of New Mexico residency. People who cannot provide proof of lawful status may apply only for the non-compliant Standard ID. citeturn1search1

REAL ID

A REAL ID identification card requires one proof of identification number, one proof of identity and two proofs of New Mexico residency. Applicants seeking REAL ID must meet the applicable lawful-status requirements. citeturn1search1

Foreign license holders

A foreign visitor who is temporarily in the United States and plans to return home does not necessarily need a New Mexico license. A person who needs an official New Mexico identification document can apply for an ID while keeping a foreign license under the circumstances described by MVD. citeturn2search4

Documents needed for a New Mexico ID

New Mexico's documentation system separates identity or identification-number proof from residency proof. The exact acceptable-document list depends on whether the applicant wants a Standard credential or REAL ID.

Identity and age documents

Examples on MVD's standard document list include an original or certified birth certificate, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, qualifying state-issued photo credentials, certain tribal identification documents, military identification, Certificate of Citizenship, Certificate of Naturalization and valid permanent-resident documentation. citeturn2search7

Identification number

REAL ID applicants need one proof of identification number. MVD's process also verifies the relevant number through its systems where applicable. Applicants should use the current MVD acceptable-document list because documentation options can change.

Documents not in English

MVD requires a written English translation that is certified by the translator as a full and complete translation. An abstract translation is not sufficient. citeturn0search0

Proof of New Mexico residency

Two proofs of New Mexico residency are required for first-time driver's license and ID transactions under the MVD requirements. Residency evidence should show the applicant's New Mexico address.

Examples

  • Utility or insurance bill
  • Bank statement
  • Government correspondence
  • Other MVD-approved documents showing a New Mexico residential address

MVD's documentation rules are credential-specific, so applicants should check the current acceptable-document list before visiting a Field Office. citeturn0search0turn2search2

Physical address

A physical New Mexico residence is distinct from a mailing address. MVD rules for other credential classes also distinguish physical residency from a P.O. Box. citeturn2search5

New Mexico REAL ID requirements

New Mexico offers both REAL ID-compliant and standard credentials. REAL ID is the option designed for federal purposes covered by the REAL ID Act. MVD's current requirements use a simple three-part document structure: identification number, identity and two New Mexico residency proofs. citeturn0search0

REAL ID checklist

  • One proof of identification number
  • One proof of identity
  • Two proofs of New Mexico residency
  • Proof of lawful status as required
  • Original documents
Standard vs. REAL ID: If you cannot provide proof of lawful status, New Mexico MVD says you may apply only for the Standard Driver's License or Standard ID. citeturn0search1

Federal use

REAL ID-compliant credentials are intended for covered federal purposes. A person can use another federally accepted identity document, such as a passport, where applicable.

New Mexico voter identification requirements

New Mexico does not generally require voters to present identification at the polls in statewide elections. Instead, voters normally provide a verbal or written statement of their name, year of birth and registered address. citeturn0search2turn0search17

First-time voters who registered by mail

A physical ID is required when a person is a first-time New Mexico voter who registered by mail and did not include the required identification with the registration application. Accepted identification can include a current photo ID or a current utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck, student ID or other government document showing the voter's name and current address. citeturn0search2turn0search5

Same-day registration

New Mexico permits registration or registration updates on the same day as voting. When registering at a polling location, a physical photo ID issued by the federal government, state government, federally recognized Indian nation, tribe or pueblo, or an educational institution is required. If the address does not match the registration record, additional address documentation may be required. citeturn0search3

Municipal elections

Some municipal jurisdictions have local voter-ID ordinances. The Secretary of State specifically notes that Rio Rancho is an example. These local rules should not be confused with the statewide voting rule. citeturn0search2

Renewing a New Mexico driver's license or ID

New Mexico MVD handles license and ID renewals through its Field Offices and online services where eligible. A temporary credential may be issued while the permanent credential is processed and mailed. citeturn0search0turn1search1

Older drivers

Drivers age 79 and older must renew their licenses yearly, but MVD says they are not charged a renewal fee. citeturn0search4

REAL ID upgrades

If a person is renewing a standard credential and wants to obtain REAL ID, the applicant must satisfy the REAL ID document requirements rather than assuming the standard credential automatically converts.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged New Mexico ID

New Mexico provides replacement services for driver's licenses and identification cards. MVD may use an existing credential photo from its database when identity and identification-number information can be verified. citeturn2search4

MVD also issues temporary credentials while a replacement credential is processed. An old license or ID may be hole-punched and used with the temporary credential for a limited period when MVD allows it. citeturn0search0

Name and address changes

Name changes

Applicants changing the name on a New Mexico driver's license or ID must provide original or certified proof of the name change. MVD lists marriage licenses, court orders, divorce decrees, birth certificates and Certificates of Naturalization among acceptable documents. citeturn2search4

Address changes

By law, a driver or vehicle owner who moves must notify MVD within 10 days. Address changes can be made online or by submitting the MVD change-of-address form. If the new address needs to appear on the physical credential, the applicant must obtain a new license or ID and pay the applicable fee. citeturn2search0

New Mexico IDs for non-U.S. citizens

New Mexico provides a pathway to Standard credentials for people who cannot provide proof of lawful status. MVD states that applicants without proof of lawful status may apply only for the Standard Driver's License or Standard ID rather than REAL ID. citeturn0search1turn1search1

Documents for eligible non-citizens

MVD's acceptable-document lists include qualifying Employment Authorization Documents, permanent-resident documentation and certain other immigration or identity documents. Some documents are verified through SAVE. citeturn2search7

Foreign documents

Foreign-language documents must be accompanied by a certified full English translation. MVD does not accept an abstract translation in place of a complete translation. citeturn0search0

Counterfeit, altered and fraudulent identification

New Mexico MVD uses secure credentialing and identity verification procedures designed to protect against identity theft and fraudulent proof-of-identity documents. Applicants must submit valid original documents for credential issuance. citeturn0search0turn1search1

New Mexico ID law FAQs

What documents do I need for a New Mexico driver's license?

A Standard license generally requires one proof of identity and age plus two proofs of New Mexico residency. REAL ID requires one proof of identification number, one proof of identity and two proofs of residency, plus applicable lawful-status documentation.

Does New Mexico have REAL ID?

Yes. New Mexico issues REAL ID-compliant driver's licenses and identification cards as an option alongside standard credentials.

Can I get a New Mexico license without proof of lawful status?

MVD says applicants who cannot provide proof of lawful status may apply only for a Standard Driver's License or Standard ID, not REAL ID.

Does New Mexico require photo ID to vote?

Not generally for statewide elections. Most voters provide their name, year of birth and registered address. Certain first-time voters who registered by mail without submitting identification must show an accepted ID.

How many proofs of residency are required?

Two proofs of New Mexico residency are generally required for first-time driver's license and ID transactions.

How much is a New Mexico driver's license?

MVD currently lists $18 for four years or $34 for eight years. New residents moving from another state pay an additional one-time $15 DWI records-check fee.

How long does a New Mexico license last?

The standard license fee schedule provides four-year and eight-year license terms. Drivers age 79 and older renew annually without a renewal fee.

What happens if my New Mexico ID is lost?

MVD provides replacement services and may use an existing MVD photo when the applicant's identity and identification-number information can be verified.
