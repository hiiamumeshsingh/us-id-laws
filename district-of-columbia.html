District of Columbia ID Laws: What You Need to Know

District of Columbia ID laws cover DC driver's licenses, non-driver identification cards, REAL ID, Limited Purpose credentials, proof of identity and residency, voting, renewal, replacement and fees. This guide explains the main requirements in plain language and links to official DC sources.

DC DMVThe District of Columbia Department of Motor Vehicles issues driver's licenses, learner permits and non-driver identification cards.

District of Columbia ID requirements at a glance

Identity

DC DMV requires original documents proving identity and date of birth. Photocopies, altered documents and notarized copies are not accepted.

Residency

Two documents proving current District residency are generally required. The documents must show your name and current DC address.

Social Security

DC DMV electronically verifies the Social Security number. If it cannot verify the number, additional SSA documentation may be required.

Credential types

DC offers REAL ID credentials and Limited Purpose credentials. Limited Purpose credentials are available to eligible residents who cannot meet REAL ID requirements.

Important: DC DMV uses REAL ID as its federally compliant credential. A Limited Purpose license or ID is a different credential and does not satisfy federal REAL ID requirements.

DC driver's license requirements

If you live in the District for 60 days or more and operate a motor vehicle in public space, DC law generally requires you to obtain a DC driver's license. There are exceptions for certain students, military personnel, diplomats, members of Congress, presidential appointees and part-time residents.

REAL ID driver's license

DC DMV requires proof of identity, lawful presence, Social Security number and two proofs of current DC residency. Applicants must also demonstrate the ability to drive, pass a vision screening and complete the applicable knowledge and road tests unless an accepted driving credential satisfies the testing requirements.

Applicants must be at least 16 years old for a DC driver's license. Drivers age 70 or older have an additional mature-driver certification requirement completed by a physician before the DMV visit.

New residents

When a person becomes a DC resident and continues driving, the 60-day residency rule applies. A valid out-of-state driver's license can generally be converted when the applicant meets DC documentation and testing requirements.

DC non-driver identification cards

DC DMV issues identification cards to District residents who need official identification but do not want or need a driver's license.

A REAL ID non-driver ID is federally compliant. A Limited Purpose non-driver ID is available to eligible District residents who do not have a Social Security number or cannot satisfy the federal REAL ID documentation requirements.

A REAL ID non-driver ID applicant must be at least 15 years old, be a DC resident and not hold a valid driver's license or ID from DC or another jurisdiction. REAL ID non-driver IDs are generally valid for eight years for U.S. citizens. Non-U.S. citizens receive a credential tied to their approved period of stay.

Free DC IDs

DC DMV provides free non-driver IDs to certain eligible residents, including District residents age 65 and older, qualifying recently returning citizens and residents experiencing homelessness. Documentation requirements still apply unless a specific alternative process is available.

Proof of identity, lawful presence and residency

Proof of identity and age

DC DMV requires one original source document showing the applicant's full legal name and date of birth. Examples include an unexpired U.S. passport or passport card, U.S. birth certificate, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, Certificate of Naturalization or Certificate of U.S. Citizenship.

Proof of lawful presence

REAL ID applicants must prove lawful presence. U.S. citizens can use qualifying citizenship documents, while non-U.S. citizens provide the immigration documents applicable to their status. The identity document used for one category cannot simply be reused as the separate lawful-presence proof when DC DMV requires separate documentation.

Proof of Social Security number

DC DMV electronically verifies the SSN with the Social Security Administration. If the number does not verify electronically, the applicant must resolve the SSA record or provide the documentation DMV requests. Non-U.S. citizens who are not eligible for an SSN must provide an SSA ineligibility letter for a REAL ID credential.

Proof of DC residency

Two documents are generally required to prove current District residency. They must show the applicant's name and current DC address. Examples include recent utility bills, loan statements, home-security bills and qualifying school or social-service documents.

DC DMV will not accept one document for multiple proof categories. Original documents are required, and documents with an expiration date must still be valid when presented.

Name changes

DC DMV accepts specified source documents for legal name changes, including certified marriage certificates, divorce decrees, official U.S. court name-change orders, birth certificates for corrections and qualifying Social Security records.

DC REAL ID requirements

The DC DMV REAL ID driver's license and REAL ID identification card are federally compliant credentials. The REAL ID credential has a black star in the upper-right corner.

For a REAL ID driver's license, DC DMV requires proof of identity and age, lawful presence, Social Security number and two proofs of current DC residency. A driver's license applicant must also show proof of ability to drive and complete the required testing unless exempt.

The REAL ID driver's license is valid for up to eight years for U.S. citizens. For non-U.S. citizens, the expiration period depends on the length of authorized stay.

REAL ID is not a national ID. It is a federally compliant state-issued credential that can be used for purposes covered by federal REAL ID rules, including domestic air travel.

DC Limited Purpose driver's license and ID

DC DMV offers Limited Purpose driver's licenses and non-driver identification cards to eligible District residents who cannot obtain a REAL ID credential.

A Limited Purpose credential is not federally compliant and cannot be used for federal purposes that require REAL ID. It is intended to provide a state-issued credential for qualifying residents who meet the District's requirements.

The Limited Purpose program has its own documentation rules. Applicants should use the DC DMV Document Verification Guide before visiting a service center.

District of Columbia voting and identification

DC's motor-vehicle identification rules and its election rules are separate. Having a REAL ID is not the same thing as having a requirement to show a REAL ID when voting.

DC DMV applications can include automatic voter-registration enrollment for eligible applicants unless the applicant declines. The DMV's REAL ID materials specifically state that applicants can make voting-related designations on the credential.

For current voter registration, early voting, Election Day voting, absentee voting and identification procedures, voters should use the District of Columbia Board of Elections because election requirements are administered separately from DC DMV.

Check election officials for the current rule. Voter procedures can differ from DMV identification requirements and may change through District election law or Board of Elections guidance.

Renewing a DC license or ID

DC DMV offers renewal services for REAL ID and Limited Purpose credentials. When renewing, DMV may require source-document revalidation depending on the credential and transaction.

DC DMV says REAL ID and Limited Purpose credentials can be renewed through its available online and in-person services when the applicant meets the eligibility requirements. If your address or legal name changes, additional documentation may be required.

After completing a qualifying transaction, DC DMV issues a temporary paper credential valid for 45 days. The permanent credential is mailed to the address of record and is generally delivered within 10 business days.

Lost, stolen or damaged DC ID

DC DMV provides replacement services for driver's licenses and non-driver identification cards. A duplicate driver's license or non-driver ID currently costs $20.

Replacement transactions can require identity and residency documentation depending on the circumstances. DC DMV may also require source-document revalidation when issuing or replacing a REAL ID credential.

Name and address changes

DC DMV requires customers to keep the information on their driver's license or ID current. A legal name change requires specified certified documentation, and DMV says the Social Security Administration record should be updated before the DC credential is issued in the new name.

A DC address change requires an update with DMV. The current fee schedule lists a $20 change-of-address service. DC DMV does not mail credentials to P.O. Boxes.

DC ID requirements for non-U.S. citizens

Non-U.S. citizens can qualify for DC REAL ID credentials when they provide the identity, lawful-presence, Social Security and residency documents required by DC DMV.

The specific lawful-presence documents depend on immigration category. DC DMV lists qualifying I-94 records, Permanent Resident Cards, Employment Authorization Cards and other USCIS or federal documents for different statuses.

Applicants who do not qualify for REAL ID may be eligible for a Limited Purpose driver's license or non-driver ID, subject to the separate Limited Purpose requirements.

Fake IDs and fraudulent identification in DC

A DC driver's license or identification card is an official government credential. Counterfeit, altered or improperly used identification can create legal consequences separate from ordinary DMV requirements.

For general educational background on counterfeit identification and the distinction between a fake ID and identity theft, see Fake ID vs. Identity Theft: Where the Legal Line Is from FakeIDs.com.

This site does not provide instructions for creating, altering or using fraudulent identification. For a specific legal issue in the District, consult a qualified attorney or the appropriate government agency.

District of Columbia ID law FAQs

How long can I drive in DC before getting a DC driver's license?

If you live in the District for 60 days or more and operate a motor vehicle in public space, DC law generally requires a DC driver's license, subject to specific reciprocity exceptions.

How many proofs of DC residency are required?

Two documents proving current District residency are generally required for a REAL ID driver's license or non-driver ID. They must show your name and current DC address.

Is REAL ID mandatory in Washington, DC?

DC offers REAL ID and Limited Purpose credentials. A REAL ID is the federally compliant option. A Limited Purpose credential may be available to eligible residents who cannot meet REAL ID requirements.

Can I get a DC ID if I do not have a Social Security number?

A person who cannot obtain a Social Security number may be eligible for a Limited Purpose credential. DC DMV requires an SSA ineligibility letter for applicants who are not eligible for an SSN when applicable.

How old do you have to be to get a DC non-driver ID?

A REAL ID non-driver identification card is available to District residents age 15 and older, subject to the required documentation. Applicants under 18 need parental consent.

Can seniors get a free DC ID?

Yes. District residents age 65 and older are eligible for a free non-driver identification card. Required identity, Social Security and residency documentation still applies.

How long is a temporary DC license or ID valid?

DC DMV issues a temporary paper credential that is valid for 45 days after a qualifying transaction. The permanent credential is mailed to the address of record.

Does a DC REAL ID have a star?

Yes. DC DMV's REAL ID credential has a black star in the upper-right corner.
