Connecticut ID Laws: What You Need to Know

Connecticut issues driver's licenses, learner's permits and non-driver photo identification cards through the Department of Motor Vehicles. This guide covers Connecticut's document requirements, residency rules, REAL ID, voter identification, renewal, replacement, fees and other common ID questions.

Connecticut driver's license requirements

Connecticut's driver's license process starts with the learner's permit for new drivers. Residents age 16 and older can apply for a learner's permit. Adults age 18 and older have an adult learner's permit process.

Applicants must meet Connecticut's identity, Social Security, residency and legal-presence documentation requirements. New drivers also have to complete the knowledge, vision and road-testing requirements that apply to their license class and age.

Moving to Connecticut

If you become a Connecticut resident and already have an out-of-state license, the DMV has an exchange process. The documents required depend on the applicant's circumstances and whether identity has already been verified under the REAL ID system.

Drive-only licenses

Connecticut offers a drive-only license for certain residents who cannot establish legal presence. A drive-only license permits driving but is not a REAL ID and cannot be used for federal identification purposes.

Connecticut non-driver ID

A Connecticut non-driver photo ID is the state's main DMV-issued photo credential for people who do not have a valid motor vehicle driver's license or whose license is suspended.

A new non-driver ID is obtained through an in-person DMV appointment. Applicants complete Form B-230 and provide the required identity, Social Security, residency and legal-presence documents unless an existing Connecticut credential allows the DMV to simplify the document process.

A new non-driver ID is valid for seven years. The current DMV fee is $28.

Documents needed for a Connecticut ID

Connecticut DMV uses a document-verification system rather than accepting just any government-issued card. For a new license, permit, non-driver ID or REAL ID, the standard checklist calls for two forms of identification, proof of Social Security number, proof of Connecticut residency and proof of legal presence in the United States.

Identity

Original or certified documents are required. Examples can include a valid U.S. passport, certified U.S. birth certificate, certificate of naturalization or qualifying immigration documents. Two documents of the same type do not satisfy the two-form identification requirement.

Social Security number

Connecticut law requires the DMV to verify a Social Security number. Acceptable proof can include a Social Security card, a W-2 issued within the previous five years or a 1099 issued within the previous five years that shows the full SSN.

A non-U.S. citizen who is not eligible for an SSN must provide an ineligibility letter from the Social Security Administration.

Documents in another language

Connecticut DMV says documents in a language other than English must be translated by a DMV-approved translator.

Proof of Connecticut residency

Connecticut has a specific two-document residency rule for many DMV applications. You generally need two different forms of documentation from two different sources.

The documents must show your name and Connecticut residence address, be dated within 90 days unless the DMV checklist gives a different period and be computer-generated or otherwise meet the checklist's requirements.

Examples include postmarked mail, a bank or mortgage statement, utility bill, credit-card statement, insurance document, lease, property-tax bill, school record and certain government documents.

For a minor, a parent or legal guardian who lives at the same address can provide two qualifying documents addressed to the parent or guardian to establish the minor's Connecticut residency.

Connecticut REAL ID requirements

Connecticut DMV has used the federal REAL ID identity-verification standards for new applicants since 2011. A verified Connecticut license or ID carries a gold star.

For a first-time REAL ID verification, applicants must provide the required original or certified identity documents, Social Security documentation and two Connecticut residency documents. DMV reviews the documents against federal and state identity standards.

Once your identity has been verified for REAL ID, you generally do not need to submit the same identity documents again each time you renew, unless the DMV requires additional verification.

Travel after the federal REAL ID enforcement date

A REAL ID-compliant Connecticut credential can be used for federal purposes covered by the REAL ID rules, including TSA identity requirements for domestic air travel. A standard credential without REAL ID verification may not be sufficient for those federal purposes.

Connecticut voter identification rules

Connecticut uses different identification rules depending on the voter's circumstances.

Most voters

Most voters can present a current and valid photo identification showing their name and address, or a copy of a utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck or government document showing their name and address. Connecticut also allows a voter who does not present one of those documents to cast a provisional ballot.

Certain first-time federal voters

A separate rule applies to certain first-time voters who registered by mail after January 1, 2003, are voting for the first time in a primary or election involving federal candidates and have a mark next to their name on the official registry list. The Secretary of the State provides a specific list of acceptable documents for these voters.

These voting rules are separate from the DMV's REAL ID requirements. Having a REAL ID does not change Connecticut's election rules.

Renewing a Connecticut ID

Connecticut non-driver IDs can be renewed in person or by mail when the applicant meets the DMV's eligibility rules.

You can renew on the expiration date or as early as 180 days before the ID expires. If the ID has been expired for two years or more, Connecticut directs the applicant to follow the process for obtaining a new ID rather than the normal renewal process.

The current renewal fee is $24 for a six-year ID or $32 for an eight-year ID. The DMV determines which term applies and the renewal notice shows the amount due.

Certain applicants, including blind veterans and people living in shelters or transitional housing, may qualify for a no-fee ID renewal.

Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged Connecticut ID

If your Connecticut non-driver ID is lost, stolen or damaged, you can request a duplicate. The DMV provides online, in-person and mail options depending on the circumstances.

For an online duplicate request, the DMV requires information such as your name exactly as it appears on the current ID, date of birth, street address, ID number and Social Security number. If your name has changed since the last ID was issued, the online duplicate process is not available and you need to use the applicable name-change process.

The current replacement fee for a Connecticut non-driver ID is $30.

Name and address changes

Connecticut residents with a driver's license, learner's permit or non-driver ID must update their DMV address within 48 hours of a change of address.

Address changes can be completed online when the applicant is eligible. The DMV can also update the address on the credential through other available service channels.

A legal name change requires the current credential and certified documentation showing the change, such as a marriage license, civil union certificate, divorce decree or court document. Connecticut charges a $30 fee to make a name change on a driver's license or non-driver ID.

Connecticut IDs for non-U.S. citizens

Connecticut issues credentials to eligible non-U.S. citizens who meet the DMV's identity, residency, Social Security and legal-presence requirements.

Applicants who are not eligible for a Social Security number must provide proof of ineligibility from the Social Security Administration. Additional immigration documents may be required to establish legal presence and the period for which a credential can be issued.

Connecticut also offers a drive-only license for certain residents who cannot establish legal presence. This credential is intended for driving and is not a federal identification document.

Counterfeit, altered and borrowed identification

A Connecticut DMV credential is issued to a specific person after the DMV verifies identity and supporting documents. A counterfeit credential, an altered genuine credential or someone else's genuine credential can therefore create different legal issues from an ordinary lost card.

For general background on why people misunderstand the difference between a government-issued credential and a novelty or replica card, see Fake ID Myths and Mistakes That Get You Caught. It is general educational material; Connecticut law and DMV guidance control the legal requirements.

Connecticut ID law FAQs

Can I get a Connecticut ID without a driver's license?

Yes. Connecticut DMV issues non-driver photo IDs to residents who do not have a valid driver's license or whose license is suspended.

How many residency documents do I need?

For many DMV applications, you need two different residency documents from two different sources showing your Connecticut residence address. The standard documents generally must be dated within 90 days.

How much does a Connecticut non-driver ID cost?

A new seven-year non-driver ID costs $28. Renewal costs $24 for six years or $32 for eight years, and a replacement costs $30.

Does Connecticut require ID to vote?

Connecticut has ID requirements for voting, but the rule depends on the voter. Most voters can use an accepted photo or document-based ID, while certain first-time federal voters face a separate requirement.

How soon do I need to report an address change?

Connecticut requires residents with a DMV credential or vehicle registration to update the DMV within 48 hours after changing address.

Can a non-U.S. citizen get a Connecticut ID?

Yes, if the applicant meets the DMV's applicable identity, residency, Social Security and legal-presence requirements. Connecticut also has a separate drive-only license for certain residents who cannot establish legal presence.
